## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"行业职业"，75 个名词几乎全是职业名称，极易写成"词典式职业清单"。我用"退休老校长带毕业生逛小镇'百业日'博览会，逐个展位走访各行各业的熟人"作主线，按"手艺行当 → 服务与专业 → 企业与公职 → 漂泊与特殊行当 → 职业感悟"五幕推进，让每个职业作为"某个熟人正在做的工作"自然出现：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The craft row 手艺街 | 体力/手工艺职业 + blue-collar | 建立场景 |
| 2 | The service stalls 服务摊 | 服务/医护/专业职业 | 铺陈职业谱系 |
| 3 | The offices 办公区 | 企业/管理/公职 + 职场词 | 展现行业组织 |
| 4 | The wanderers 远方来客 | 漂泊/特殊/新兴职业 | 收束职业多样性 |
| 5 | What work means 职业感悟 | vocation/profession/career 等 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`blacksmith / carpenter / cobbler / tailor / weaver / mason` 等手艺行当集中在"手艺街"，每人手上有具体动作（打铁、刨木、补鞋）；`physician / dentist / nurse / psychiatrist / specialist` 集中在义诊摊；`employer / employee / firm / conglomerate / flexi-time` 落在企业展位；`vocational / entrepreneurial / manually / blue-collar / cast-iron` 五个形容词分别贴到对应行当；结尾用 `vocation / profession / career / occupation / unemployment` 点出"职业"主题的几个近义词的差别。

## 二、英文故事　The Hundred Trades Day

### 1. The craft row

On the town's **Hundred Trades Day**, old Mr. Vale, a retired schoolmaster, led his former students down the craft row. "Every **occupation** here was once a **guild**," he said. First came the **blacksmith**, hammering a **cast-iron** bar at his forge, and beside him a **forger** shaped another piece of hot metal. A **carpenter** planed a board **manually**, while a **masonry** worker laid stone and a **draftsman** sketched the design. A **cobbler** stitched a shoe, a **tailor** measured a coat, and a **weaver** worked a loom. These were the **blue-collar** trades, Mr. Vale said, proud and **vocational**, learned by doing rather than by books. An **apprentice** swept the floor, watching a **craftspeople**'s bench where skilled workers polished a **lapidary**'s gems. The boys watched, and for once no one joked about **manually** earned pay.

### 2. The service stalls

At the service stalls the whole town seemed to be at work. A **chef** in a white cap stirred a pot while a **butcher** weighed meat and a **barber** cut hair. A **physician** checked a patient's heart, a **dentist** leaned over another chair, and a **nurse** bandaged a hand; a **specialist** and a **psychiatrist** had come from the city to help. A **banker** and a **cashier** sat behind a counter, an **accountant** added figures, and a **clerk** stamped forms. A **consultant** gave free advice, a **counselor** and a **solicitor** answered legal questions, and an **announcer** with a **newscaster**'s voice called the news. A **reporter** and a **correspondent** wrote it down; an **operator** worked the switchboard and a **typist** typed the copy. A **recorder** kept the minutes, a **secretary** filed them, and a **messenger** carried the letters to the **postmaster**.

### 3. The offices

Beyond the stalls stood the **firm** and the larger **conglomerate**. An **employer** greeted an **employee** at the door; inside, an **assistant** brought coffee and an **inspector** checked the safety of the **operator**'s station. A **surveyor** unfolded a map, an **architect** studied the plans, and an **engineer** tested a machine with a **cop**'s curiosity for detail. A **plumber** fixed a leak, and an **operator** checked the wiring. A **porter** carried boxes for a **packer**, and an **attendant** held the door. A **hostess** welcomed visitors at the **flexi-time** desk, where staff chose their own hours. Government had a place here too: a **diplomat** spoke of distant treaties, a **solicitor** read the new rules, and an **inspector** wore a badge. "Work," said Mr. Vale, "is the **stewardship** of a shared town."

### 4. The wanderers

The last row held the restless trades. A **pilot** and a **seaman** told of distant ports; a **soldier** and a **fireman** spoke of danger. A **lifesaver** demonstrated a rescue, and a **bodyguard** watched the crowd. A **prospector** showed a stone from a mine, a **taxonomist** displayed labelled insects, and a **falconer** held a hawk on his glove. A **freelancer** with a laptop worked beside a **sibyl** who read fortunes and a **semiliterate** old man who signed his name with care. A **salesman** sold pens, a **curator** spoke of museums, and a **matron** asked for order. A **cop** on the corner smiled; even **unemployment**, Mr. Vale said, was a temporary **occupation** for some, not a verdict on a life.

### 5. What work means

At sunset the old man gathered the students under a tree. "You have met every **profession** today," he said, "but remember the difference. A job pays; a **vocation** calls; a **career** is the long road you walk; an **occupation** is simply what you do." He had been a teacher all his life, an **entrepreneurial** spirit in a quiet **profession**, and he had never once been ashamed of honest **manually** skilled work. "Whether you hammer iron like the **blacksmith** or balance books like the **accountant**," he said, "the town needs your hands and your mind." The students went home with dirt on their shoes and a new respect for the **craftspeople** and clerks who kept the place running. For one day, at least, every trade had been seen.

## 四、学习建议

1. **职业名词按场景成组记**：手艺行当 `blacksmith / carpenter / cobbler / tailor / weaver / masonry / forger`；医护 `physician / dentist / nurse / specialist / psychiatrist`；企业 `employer / employee / firm / conglomerate / flexi-time`；公职 `diplomat / inspector / solicitor`。成组记比孤立记牢固。
2. **近义辨析**：`job`（一份工作，最口语）→ `career`（职业生涯，长期的路）→ `profession`（需专业训练的职业）→ `vocation`（使命感召的职业）→ `occupation`（职业，填表用语）。故事结尾专门对比了这五个词。
3. **"-er/-or/-ist" 职业后缀**：`baker/barber/blacksmith`（-er 居多）、`actor/operator/inspector`（-or）、`dentist/specialist/taxonomist/psychiatrist/freelancer/scientist`（-ist 多为专业者）。看到后缀就能猜"从事…的人"。
4. **冷僻职业留痕**：`falconer`（放鹰人）、`lapidary`（宝石专家）、`sibyl`（女预言家）、`prospector`（勘探者）、`taxonomist`（分类学家）较书面，已在正文各出现一次，注意其行业色彩。
5. **搭配与词形**：`blue-collar`（蓝领）对应 `white-collar`（白领）；`vocational`（职业的）与 `vocation`（使命/职业）、`profession`（专业）同源；`manually` 是 `manual`（手工的）的副词形式。
