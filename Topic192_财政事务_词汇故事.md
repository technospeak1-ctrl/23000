## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"财政事务"，通货膨胀、资产负债、债券税种等术语密集，最易写成财经术语表。我用"一位小国财政部长面对财政危机、从接手到好转的一年"作主线，按"接手危机 → 盘点资产 → 危机加深 → 支付账单 → 形势好转"五幕推进，让每个词与搭配落在财政决策的具体动作上：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A minister takes office 部长接手 | 财政形势 + 通胀/指数词 | 建立人物与危机 |
| 2 | The balance sheet 资产负债表 | 债券/证券/资产负债词 | 铺陈财务状况 |
| 3 | The crisis deepens 危机加深 | 萧条/贬值/失业/避险词 | 矛盾激化 |
| 4 | Paying the bills 支付账单 | 付款方式/成本/税种词 | 应对危机 |
| 5 | The upturn 形势好转 | 到期/回升/筹资词 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`inflationary / inflation / devalue / devaluation / depreciate` 围绕"货币贬值—通胀"因果链分幕出现；`gilt-edged bonds / listed security / equity / portfolio / long-term asset / aggregate liability` 集中在"盘点资产"一幕；`payable at sight / deferred payments / balloon payment / down payment` 等付款方式集中在"支付账单"一幕；`value added tax / capital gains tax / unearned income` 落在税制改革决策里；`extroversion / gasoline / windfall` 等"跨语义"词分别贴到部长性格、能源价格与意外收入上，避免堆砌。

## 二、英文故事　The Minister's Ledger

### 1. A minister takes office

Elena Vale took office as **finance** minister of Aldermere on the first of April, and by noon she faced a **financial crisis**. Her **principal** adviser laid out the figures in **triplicate**: the **global** economy was slowing, **inflationary** pressure was building, and **inflation** had reached a **maximum** of twelve percent. The cost-of-living **index** had jumped, and every **billion** of new spending showed up to the last **decimal** place on her screen. Elena's **extroversion** had made her a popular speaker, but now she had to **realize** how thin the country's cushion was. "We built this **economics** on borrowed money," she told her team. She ordered an **immediate** review, because the **impact** of one wrong decision would be felt for years. Around her the **possible** options narrowed; only one **potential** path looked safe.

### 2. The balance sheet

The next morning the treasury's **portfolio** was laid bare. Years ago the country had bought **gilt-edged bonds** and **listed security** in steady firms, and the **gilt** certificates in the vault still looked impressive. But the **listed** shares had fallen, the **equity** in two state firms had been thinned by a **dilution** of new issues, and every **long-term asset** was hard to sell. Accountants argued over which **admissible assets** could be counted, while the **aggregate liability** on the **debt** roll kept growing. A **mortgage** on the old port and **depreciation** on the rail network were both larger than she expected; she feared the currency would **depreciate** further. "Our **cost-effectiveness** is poor," she said, studying **absorption costing** for the first time. Only the safest **gilt-edged** paper still held its value.

### 3. The crisis deepens

By autumn the **depression** had arrived. Food and **gasoline** prices rose together, so the government tried to **inflate** its way out and watched the currency **devalue** twice in a month; the second **devaluation** frightened lenders. Share prices **nosedived** to **rock-bottom**, and two banks looked ready to **collapse**. The **scale** of the trouble startled even seasoned officials. A **wildcat** strike at the docks stopped exports, and rumours that a contractor would **bilk** the treasury spread fast. Elena announced an **emergency** fund, but relief money could not **flow** fast enough. She arranged a credit line hedged with **risk capital**, yet even **junk bonds** now seemed too risky. When a **moratorium** on foreign debt was declared, **unemployment** climbed and more families claimed **unemployment benefit**. With wages **owing**, the public mood turned sour, and the treasury **owed** millions it could not repay.

### 4. Paying the bills

Elena's team worked through the payment files. Suppliers wanted **payable at sight** terms, while the treasury preferred **deferred payments** and a **balloon payment** at the end. The **down payment** on a new hospital had been made, but an **accidental cost** and an **accumulated deficit** in the health budget pushed **expenditure** past the plan. **Aggregate losses** at the state railway and the **average cost** of every public meal were printed in the same **neat** column; **normal operating expenses** looked modest beside them. To cover the **excess**, the ministry studied **economic efficiency** and **economical activity**, and closed one **uneconomical** office. A **value added tax** reform and a **capital gains tax** were proposed, along with a levy on **unearned income**. Officials asked for **antecedent money** before releasing contracts, and every **reply coupon** from the **mobile bank** campaign was counted. Farmers demanded a **warranty** on seed prices, while a **windfall** from one export deal briefly cheered the room. Nothing was **payable** until the accounts were signed.

### 5. The upturn

By spring the tide turned. Bond **maturity** dates arrived at last, and the **upturn** in trade surprised even the sceptics. Elena held a **fundraising** dinner for **potential customers** of a new savings scheme, hoping to **obtain** pledges and promising to **plough back** profits into local projects. "Keep some **near money** for emergencies," she warned, "and watch the **turnover** of every state firm." Larger firms enjoyed an **economy of scale** that the small ones could not match, so she asked an aide to **subdivide** the budget into clearer lines. The **mobile** exhibition vans carried the **mobile bank** to villages, and the scheme proved workable at last. On her desk lay the final report in **triplicate**; beside it a **neat** note read: "Sound **finance** is not luck — it is knowing who **owes** whom, and paying on time."

## 四、学习建议

1. **通胀链**：`inflationary`（通胀的）→ `inflation`（通胀）→ `inflate`（抬高物价）→ `devalue`（贬值）→ `devaluation`（贬值）→ `depreciate`（贬值/折旧），按"原因—过程—结果"一条线记，正是财政新闻的高频词族。
2. **资产负债表**：`portfolio`（投资组合）、`equity`（股权）、`listed security`（上市证券）、`gilt-edged bonds`（金边债券）、`long-term asset`（长期资产）是资产端；`aggregate liability`（债务总额）、`debt`、`mortgage`（抵押）是负债端，对照记忆。
3. **付款方式**：`payable at sight`（见票即付）、`deferred payments`（延期付款）、`balloon payment`（尾款大额）、`down payment`（首付）、`plough back`（再投资）串起"怎么付钱"的完整场景。
4. **税种搭配**：`value added tax`（增值税）、`capital gains tax`（资本收益税）、`unearned income`（非劳动所得）是财税阅读三大高频词，配合 `economic efficiency / economy of scale` 一起记。
5. **危机词**：`depression`（萧条）、`moratorium`（延期偿付）、`unemployment benefit`（失业救济）、`junk bonds`（垃圾债券）、`wildcat`（高风险的）都与经济下行同框，反面词 `upturn`（好转）、`windfall`（意外之财）在结尾出现，形成对照。
