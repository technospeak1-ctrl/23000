## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"高等教育"，词表几乎是一张**大学生活的物品与流程清单**（注册、课程、作业、考试、论文、学费……），最忌写成"校园名词罗列"。我用"一名大一新生林从一个秋天入学到学期结束的完整经历，室友梅一路相伴"作主线，按"注册日 → 第一周 → 课堂 → 挫折 → 学期末"五幕推进，把每件物品、每道流程都嵌进她的时间线：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Registration day 注册日 | 入学流程名词 + 住宿动词 | 建立人物与场景 |
| 2 | The first week 第一周 | 学习资料名词 + 阅读动词 | 铺陈学习细节 |
| 3 | The classes 课堂 | 学术形容词 + 表达/理解动词 | 展示学业推进 |
| 4 | The setback 挫折 | 出勤/惩罚名词 + 调整动词 | 情节转折 |
| 5 | The end of term 学期末 | 剩余名词 + 收束动词 | 点题与升华 |

**设计要点**：`academic research / appreciable difference / be beneficial to / liberal arts / particular reason` 五条常用搭配，以及 `accommodate to / adjust to / resit the exam / expel sb. from the school / interpret...as... / acquire knowledge / accomplish one's object` 等动词搭配全部落进故事的具体动作；`beneficial / appreciable / assigned / particular` 等褒义或中性形容词与 `absence / bitterness / disillusion / expel` 等负面词分置不同场景，形成"成长前后的对照"；抽象词如 `context / documentation / repetition` 通过林的具体学习动作落地，绝不写成清单。

## 二、英文故事　Lin's First Semester

### 1. Registration day

In September Lin **enrolled** as an **undergraduate** at the **academy**, a **coed** college on a green **campus**. She had worked two years to **enter** it. Her **admission** letter lay in her bag beside the **tuition** receipt and the **credential** she had proudly brought from home. At **registration** a tired clerk checked her name against a long list, and an **asterisk** marked the courses still open; she had to **register** for four of them on the spot. Her **classmate** Mei, a quiet **bookworm** with a stack of books, helped her carry the boxes to the dormitory, where the college would **accommodate** her for three years. An older student showed her how to **accommodate to** campus life. The **student association** gave each newcomer a map. The **semester** would run for sixteen weeks, but the **trimester** system confused her at first.

### 2. The first week

The **curriculum** was heavy. On the first day the professor **assigned** three **article**s and an **abstract** to read before Friday; the **assignment** was due at noon. Lin sat in the library with a **dictionary** and a pile of **reference** books, trying to **digest** a page of **academic research**. She would **extract** the key sentence from each paragraph and copy it into a notebook. In French class there was a **dictation** every Monday. She had learned the **basics** of **grammar** at home, but nothing had prepared her for **shorthand** notes or the **diagram**s in the science text. She read every **excerpt** twice and kept a **documentation** file of her sources. Mei showed her how to read a sentence in **context** instead of word by word. "Look for a **particular reason** behind every rule," Mei said, "or you will forget it by Easter."

### 3. The classes

Lin soon found that the **liberal arts** courses suited her best. She took a **minor** in history because a **particular reason** drew her to it: a grandmother who had told her stories. History was her **specialty**, and she wrote an **essay** every week. Her tutor said the reading was **beneficial to** any serious student, and Lin felt an **appreciable difference** in her own writing within a month. In seminar she had to **recite** a passage and **interpret** a poem **as** a reply to its age. When a classmate offered a wild reading, the teacher asked her to **elucidate** the line she found confusing. Lin tried to **assimilate** so much new information that her head ached; she would **assort** her notes by subject and **summarize** them at night. **Repetition** helped: seeing the same **argument** in three books made it clear. At a **symposium** on teaching, a visiting scholar gave a **refresher** talk on the **statistic**s of learning, and Lin saw that research could be **beneficial to** real classrooms too.

### 4. The setback

Midterm brought trouble. Lin's **attendance** was perfect, but an **absence** from the lab cost her a week of data. She had **disillusion**ed herself with the idea that hard work alone was enough, and a **bitterness** she had never known crept in. Her tutor warned that too many missed labs could **expel** a student **from the school**. A kind adviser helped her **adjust to** the new pace and **reschedule** her experiments for the following week. When a lab partner fell ill, Lin had to **resit** the practical **exam** alone; she sat up late and **accomplished** the **task** after all. Her **internship** at a publishing house the previous summer had taught her more than any textbook, and she won an **assistantship** in the department for the next term.

### 5. The end of term

By December Lin could see an **appreciable difference** in herself. She had **acquired knowledge** in the library and **acquired the skills** of careful work in the lab; next year she would start a study group and help the **association** welcome newcomers. Mei planned to **transfer** to another department, and the two friends promised to meet at the **symposium** in spring. The department would **institute** a new rule: every **undergraduate** must write a short **thesis** before receiving a **diploma**. Lin laughed at a **riddle** on the last page of her **essay** and thought how far she had come since the day she first tried to **enter** the **academy**. She had **accomplished** her **object**: to learn how to learn. The **trimester** would end soon, but the daily **repetition** of small habits would carry her through all the years ahead.

## 四、学习建议

1. **按大学生活时间线串词**：把 `registration / tuition / credential / curriculum / assignment / exam / thesis / diploma` 按"入学 → 上课 → 考试 → 毕业"排出顺序记忆，比孤立背单词牢固得多。
2. **"学期"三兄弟**：`semester`（两学期制）、`trimester`（三学期制）、`term`（学期，通用），注意不同国家的学制用词不同。
3. **学习动词链**：`digest`（领会）→ `extract`（摘录）→ `assimilate`（吸收）→ `assort`（归类）→ `summarize`（总结）→ `recite`（背诵），正好是林的学习流程。
4. **搭配聚焦**：`be beneficial to`（对……有益）、`accommodate to / adjust to`（适应）、`interpret...as...`（把……理解为……）、`resit the exam`（补考）都是高频考点，介词 `to / as` 不要混用。
5. **冷词留痕**：`assistantship`（助学金）、`refresher`（复习课）、`shorthand`（速记）、`documentation`（文献）、`asterisk`（星号）较书面或专门，已在正文自然出现，注意其校园语境。
