## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"理论技术 2"，词项横跨抽象理论、材料与化学、机器设备、田野考古与技术方法，极易写成"词典条目"。我用"技术馆的讲解员 Ms. Grey 带学生从理论室走到材料实验室、机器大厅、田野工作站，最后做一次收尾演示"作主线，按"理论室 → 材料实验室 → 机器大厅 → 田野工作站 → 收尾演示"五幕推进，让每个术语落在玻璃板、试样、机器或工具上：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The theory room 理论室 | 认知形容词 + 数学/理论名词 | 建立"深奥—可理解"的对照 |
| 2 | The materials lab 材料实验室 | 材料/化学名词 + 净化形容词 | 把术语落到试样与操作上 |
| 3 | The machines hall 机器大厅 | 设备名词 + 自动化动词 | 用机器运转串起技术词 |
| 4 | The field station 田野工作站 | 考古/自然名词 + 搭配 | 群像铺陈、扩展场景 |
| 5 | The closing demo 收尾演示 | 方法动词 + 全部搭配 | 点题与升华 |

**设计要点**：`abstruse / impalpable / simplistic` 与 `elemental / scrutable / discerning` 在理论板上形成正反对照；`crystallization / decomposition / vaporization / condenser / electrode` 集中在材料实验台，按"溶解—结晶—分解—蒸发—冷凝"的实验顺序出现。10 条常用搭配如 `a conceptual model / a mimic battle / advanced course / archaeological discoveries / automotive vehicles / fireproof material / fully automated / industrial economy / pioneering techniques / theoretical background` 全部写进具体场景；`commence with / react with` 两条动词搭配变成工作站的操作指令。

## 二、英文故事　Inside the Technology Museum

### 1. The theory room

Our guide, a **technocrat** named Ms. Grey, began in the quiet theory room. "Every machine starts with a **theorem**," she said, writing a **formula** on the glass. "Today we test **a conceptual model** of how a **reaction** stores energy." The idea sounded **abstruse**, but she broke it into **elemental** steps and worked **systematically** through each one. A model is **simplistic** if it ignores the **density** of the material, she warned; a good theory must be **scrutable**, not **impalpable**. A **discerning** student asked for the **derivation** of the **formula**, and Ms. Grey handed him a **datum** sheet and the **parameters** of the test with the **efficiency** of each part. "Without a solid **theoretical background**," she said, "you will only copy what you see."

### 2. The materials lab

In the materials lab the work became physical. An **auger** bored into a block of **asphalt**, and a fine **mesh** caught the dust. Through a narrow **aperture** we watched a **purified** liquid cool into **crystallization**; a second sample underwent **decomposition** and then **vaporization** under a **condenser**. "A **fireproof material** lining the **reactor** keeps the heat in," Ms. Grey said, touching a **fireproof** tile. Two **electrode** rods began to **emit** a faint spark, and the whole **atom** model on the wall lit up. She reminded us that every **reaction** must be measured against a **datum** from the first trial.

### 3. The machines hall

The next hall hummed with machines. A model **aircraft** hung above a **spaceship** nose; both ran on the same **electronics** board and the same **software**. A **converter** changed the current, and a row of **automotive vehicles** rolled past on a test track. "This line is fully **automated**," Ms. Grey said; "we **automate** the welding and **transistorize** the controls." On a screen, **e-mail** messages arrived through the **internet** in a **nanosecond**, and a warning light told us when to **recharge** a cell. The plan was to **retool** the whole line by summer.

### 4. The field station

At the field station the tone changed. A **conservationist** was cataloguing **archaeological discoveries** from a dig, while a coin collector showed a **numismatic** find and defended it as an **original**. A **paleontology** team had found a bone in a **crevasse**; their first survey was **fruitless**, but a **pioneering** method using a small drill saved the season. Ms. Grey spoke of the **industrial economy** that funds such work and of an **industrialized** city that had buried its own past. "A **mimic battle** once took place on this ground," she said, and she made us **commence with** a map before we **react with** any guess. The **terminology** of the dig, she added, changes every year.

### 5. The closing demonstration

For the last demonstration Ms. Grey asked us to **devise** a small test of our own. "First **distinguish** fact from guess," she said. "Then **simplify** the design, **standardize** the parts, **interconnect** the sensors, and **summarize** what you find in one page." We did. A student's model began to **emit** a tone; another tried to **assimilate** the new data into an older **formula** and failed, so he asked for help. Ms. Grey did not scold him. "Even **pioneering techniques** need a **discerning** user," she said. "An **advanced** course teaches the tools; only practice makes them yours." She **summarized** the day in one line: begin with a question, **devise** a test, and **commence with** the smallest **datum** you can trust. I left with a notebook full of **terminology** and a head full of questions.

## 四、学习建议

1. **按五幕分块记**：理论（`abstruse / elemental / scrutable / impalpable / simplistic / theorem / formula / derivation`）、材料（`crystallization / decomposition / vaporization / condenser / electrode / auger / asphalt`）、机器（`aircraft / spaceship / converter / electronics / software / recharge / retool`）、田野（`archaeological / numismatic / paleontology / conservationist / crevasse`）、方法（`devise / distinguish / simplify / standardize / interconnect / summarize`）。
2. **正反对照记**：`abstruse / impalpable / simplistic` 对 `elemental / scrutable / discerning`，在理论室同一块玻璃板上对照；`fruitless` 的初次勘察与 `pioneering` 的新方法也构成情节上的对照。
3. **按实验顺序串动词**：`devise → distinguish → simplify → standardize → interconnect → summarize`，再接 `emit / recharge / retool / automate`，想象一次完整的"设计—简化—标准化—互连—总结"的演示流程。
4. **搭配整串背**：`a conceptual model / a mimic battle / advanced course / archaeological discoveries / automotive vehicles / fireproof material / fully automated / industrial economy / pioneering techniques / theoretical background` 以及 `commence with`、`react with`，要连同故事原句一起记，尤其 `commence with` 与 `react with` 的介词不要记混。
5. **冷词留痕**：`numismatic`（钱币学的）、`auger`（螺旋钻）、`crevasse`（冰隙）、`nanosecond`（纳秒）、`transistorize`（装晶体管于）较专业，已在正文出现，注意其场景归属。
