## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"货物订购"，名词几乎全是采购、货运与商品词，搭配密度极高，极易写成"外贸术语表"。我用"连锁店采购经理泰莎·林恩向制造商拉杰·米特订购一批外套"作主线，按"订货簿 → 与厂方议价 → 条款与运输 → 落单 → 收货之后"五幕推进，让每个词与搭配成为这笔订单上的一次具体动作：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The order book 订货簿 | 采购/预订/商品词 | 建立人物 |
| 2 | At the maker 在厂方 | 议价/品质/价格形容词 | 制造博弈 |
| 3 | Terms and shipping 条款与运输 | 财务/生产/仓储词 | 铺陈流程 |
| 4 | Closing the deal 落单 | 交易动词 + 贸易方式搭配 | 情节高潮 |
| 5 | After delivery 收货之后 | 售后/市场词 + 收束 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`procurement / bookings / booking / backlog / catalogue / assortment` 集中在订货簿一幕；`haggle / differential / extra cost / flawed / inferior to / zero defect` 写成买卖双方面对样品的一次议价；`enormous expenditure / external pressure / assembly line / bonded goods / shipping department / container / dues` 落在合同与货运条款上；`buy / deal / enter / expend / produce / manufacture / monopolize / skyrocket` 八个动词串起"落单—生产—履约"的因果链；`barter trade / counter purchase / leveraged buyout / buy in bulk / purchase order / book confirmation` 等搭配全部变成这笔订单里真实的文件与交易方式。

## 二、英文故事　The Northgate Order

### 1. The order book

Tessa Lynn ran **procurement** for Northgate Stores, a retail chain. Her **barometer** was the morning **bookings** sheet: when a single **booking** fell through, the whole season suffered. A **backlog** of unpaid orders lay beside a thick **catalogue**, and she circled the **assortment** of coats she wanted. Her predecessor had kept a few **bespoke products** for special clients, and Tessa wanted more of them. In a **buyer's market** she could afford to choose; the **amount** on her first list was modest, but the **goods** had to be right. She sent an **online order** for samples and waited.

### 2. At the maker

The **manufacturer**, Raj Mitter, met her at his plant. Tessa began to **haggle** over the price of one **batch** of coats, and Raj said the **differential** between pure wool and a blend was small. She refused to pay an **extra cost** for a **frill** nobody wanted, and she would not accept a **flawed** lining or **faulty goods** from a rushed **batch**. "These are **inferior to** the sample," she said. Raj offered a **free gift** of scarves, but Tessa asked for **zero defect** instead. When she suspected he would **overcharge** her, she read every line of his ledger. At last they agreed on a price.

### 3. Terms and shipping

The **enormous expenditure** worried the board, and **external pressure** from the bank forced Tessa to cut the **indirect costs**. Raj's **assembly line** was modern and his **automation** kept wages low, but the **equipment** for a **green product** line was not yet paid for. He asked her to **franchise** his name in two counties, and a local **contractor** offered to build a **bonded house** for the **bonded goods** in transit. The **shipping department** booked space on a freighter built at the **shipyard**: forty **container**s of **cargo**, with the **dues** paid in advance. Tessa checked the **inventory** in the **warehouse** and studied the **life cycle** of the coats. Under a **hire purchase** plan she would pay over a year, and **agricultural credit** helped the wool farm. In the **free market** nothing lasted forever; an **end-product** could be copied in a season. **Finished goods** piled up on the dock.

### 4. Closing the deal

Tessa sent the **purchase order** and waited for the **book confirmation**. A rival **bidder** named Osman made a lower **bid**, hoping to **monopolize** the supply, and prices began to **skyrocket** in the trade press. Tessa refused to **buy off** the inspector, and she would not join his **barter trade** or his **counter purchase** scheme. Instead she agreed to **buy in bulk**, which let Raj **produce** more and **manufacture** the coats cheaply. "We **deal** with honest partners," she told him, and she would **enter into** a long contract and **enter upon** the work at once. The board discussed a **leveraged buyout** of Raj's firm, and Tessa had to **expend** hours explaining the risk. A **block booking** of warehouse space followed.

### 5. After delivery

When the **container**s arrived, Tessa kept the **receipt** and the **stub** in her file. The **rebate** came three weeks late, but a **follow-up** call settled it. The **retailer**s put the coats in the **flagship** store, and the **marketing** team photographed the **menswear** for the catalogue. A **jeweler** friend asked why a clothing order mattered so much; Tessa said every **merchandise** line was a promise, and a **commodity** was only as good as its maker. Her first **online order** had become the season's bestseller. The **bookings** for next spring were already full, and no clause would **expire** before the new sample arrived. She thought about **impulse buying** and how a **free market** rewards patience.

## 四、学习建议

1. **订单流程一条线**：`catalogue`（目录）→ `online order`（在线订购）→ `purchase order`（订购单）→ `book confirmation`（确认订货）→ `bookings`（订货）→ `receipt / stub`（收据/存根），正好是一笔订单从查询到入账的全过程。
2. **议价词成对记**：`haggle`（讨价还价）、`differential`（差价）、`extra cost`（附加成本）、`overcharge`（多要价）、`rebate`（回扣）都发生在"在厂方"一幕；`inferior to`（次于）和 `zero defect`（零缺陷）是品质判断的两端。
3. **货运与仓储词**：`shipping department`（运输部门）、`container`（集装箱）、`cargo`（货物）、`shipyard`（造船厂）、`bonded goods / bonded house`（保税货物/仓库）、`warehouse / inventory`（仓库/存货）成组记，是外贸高频场景词。
4. **贸易方式搭配**：`barter trade`（易货）、`counter purchase`（回购）、`hire purchase`（分期购买）、`leveraged buyout`（融资收买）、`buy in bulk`（大批购买）都写成拉杰与泰莎谈判中的具体方案，比单背术语牢。
5. **动词整串记**：`enter into`（签订）与 `enter upon`（着手）、`deal with`（打交道）、`expend hours`（花时间）、`skyrocket`（猛涨）都在"落单"一幕出现，配合 `monopolize / produce / manufacture` 一起复习"交易—生产"链条。
