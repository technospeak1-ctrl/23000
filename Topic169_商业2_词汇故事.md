## 一、编排逻辑

本主题"商业 2"词项多、搭配密（21 条），且横跨财务、保险、租赁、扩张多个子场，最易写成"商务术语表"。我用"Ray 与 Mo 合伙把地下室小作坊做成塑料橡胶制品行业领头羊，途中投保防灾、迁厂扩张、清仓转型"的经营史作主线，按五幕推进，让每个词与搭配都落在具体动作上：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A commercial beginning 商业起步 | 身份/账户/材料/证照 | 建立人物与公司 |
| 2 | Running the numbers 日常运营 | 运营/成本/数据/财务 | 展现经营数字 |
| 3 | Insurance and risk 保险与风险 | 保险/理赔/裁员/关闭 | 制造危机 |
| 4 | Expansion 迁厂与扩张 | 租赁/地产/财团/协议 | 情节推进 |
| 5 | The harvest 收成与回望 | 收益/赞助/偏好/收束 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`commercial / joint / raw / plastic / rubber` 贴到创业与材料；`running costs / consumable energy / raw data / quantifiable / lucrative business` 落到日常经营；`insurance policy / insurance premium / insurance claim / reinsure / insure` 串成一次投保与理赔；`lease / leasehold / property / relocate a factory / institutional investors / procure an agreement` 构成迁厂扩张；`harvest / jackpot / patronage / closing down sale / with exception of` 收束全篇。

## 二、英文故事　From Basement to Market Leader

### 1. A commercial beginning

Ray Vance wanted a **commercial** future, so he rented a damp **basement** as his first workshop and opened a **commercial account** at the bank on the corner. When his friend Mo joined him, the two signed a **joint bank account** agreement and formed a **joint** company to turn **raw** **material** into **plastic** and **rubber** parts. Their **motive** was simple: make goods people would buy again. To make the first **purchase** of machinery they set aside a **provision** from their savings and bought a **quantity** of used moulds. Mo, a trained **licentiate**, held the **license** that let them trade legally, and they celebrated their first order with a drink at the **bar** down the street.

### 2. Running the numbers

The workshop's **running** costs were low, but **consumable energy** and raw stock ate into the **margin**. Ray entered every figure in **raw data** sheets and made each cost **quantifiable** before he decided anything. The **lucrative business** they dreamed of did not come at once: a **promotion** in the local paper brought the first rush of buyers, and soon **proceeds** from the **aftermarket** for spare parts kept their **liquidity** healthy. To keep the **pipeline** full and cut waste they switched to **returnable** crates, and each **quantum** of output was checked. They studied the **comparison** between their prices and rivals' and added a **plus** here and there. Within two years the little firm was the **market leader** in its niche.

### 3. Insurance and risk

A fire in a neighbouring factory changed everything. Ray had taken out an **insurance policy** and paid the **insurance premium** on time, so when smoke damaged their stock he filed an **insurance claim** at once. The insurer sent an **advice note** explaining the **prompt day** for payment, and because Ray had also agreed to **reinsure** part of the risk the loss was shared. But the **expropriation** of a supplier's yard forced the **closure** of one line, and Ray had to **reject** a bad batch and announce a **redundancy** of two workers. He learned to **insure** against every hazard and to face **rejection** from cautious buyers without anger.

### 4. Expansion

With the claim settled the partners planned to grow. They chose to **relocate a factory** to a bigger town, taking a long **lease** on a site with **leasehold** rights and buying the **property** next door. A **consortium** of banks and a trade **federation** backed them, and **institutional investors** bought shares. Mo worked hard to **procure an agreement** with a foreign buyer, while Ray put their best samples **on display** at a trade fair and began to **commercialize** a new process that improved the **conversion** of scrap into parts.

### 5. The harvest

The **harvest** came slowly but surely. A lucky **jackpot** from a government contract, steady **patronage** from loyal clients, and a clear **preference** for their durable goods pushed sales up. Ray kept a **random check** on quality and, **with exception of** one faulty season, never slipped. When a rival cut prices, Ray chose to **contend for** the high end and **contend with** the cheap imports rather than follow them into a **closing down sale**. He would **repair** a machine before replacing it, and he measured the **relevance** of every decision against one question: would it still earn money next year? From a damp **basement** to the **market leader**, their story proved that care and timing **motivate** success more than luck.

## 四、学习建议

1. **创业链**：`commercial account`（商业账户）→ `joint bank account`（共同账户）→ `joint` company（合资公司）→ `license`（许可证）→ `market leader`（市场领导者），是一条公司从小到大成长线。
2. **运营数字词**：`running costs`（运营成本）、`consumable energy`（能耗）、`margin`（利润空间）、`raw data`（原始数据）、`quantifiable`（可计量）、`liquidity`（流动性）一起记，是看经营报表的高频词。
3. **保险词族**：`insurance policy`（保单）→ `insurance premium`（保费）→ `insure`（投保）→ `reinsure`（分保）→ `insurance claim`（索赔）→ `advice note`（通知单）→ `prompt day`（支付日），串起一次完整理赔。
4. **扩张与风险**：`lease / leasehold / property`（租约/租借权/房产）、`relocate a factory`（迁厂）、`consortium / federation / institutional investors`（财团/联合会/机构投资人）属于扩张词；`expropriation / closure / redundancy / rejection` 属风险词，正反同篇对照。
5. **动词搭配**：`contend for`（争夺）、`contend with`（对付）、`procure an agreement`（达成协议）、`on display`（陈列）、`with exception of`（除……之外）都有固定结构，配套记忆更牢。
