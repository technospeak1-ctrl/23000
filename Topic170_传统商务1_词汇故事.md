## 一、编排逻辑

本主题"传统商务 1"围绕旧式商号的经营：账簿、发票、快递、报价、附加费、免税、远期外汇一应俱全。我用"Hale 夫人经营一家百年老字号布行，从记账、讨价还价到外汇结算与风险防范"的一天到一生的经营史作主线，按五幕推进，让每个词与搭配都落到具体商务动作上：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | An illustrious old firm 百年老店 | 身份/账簿/产品/商誉 | 建立人物与商号 |
| 2 | The daily ledger 日常记账 | 发票/库存/收支/计数 | 铺陈经营日常 |
| 3 | A hard bargain 艰难议价 | 报价/契约/赔偿/还价 | 制造情节冲突 |
| 4 | Money matters 钱款与风险 | 外汇/保险/区分/认购 | 展现资金运作 |
| 5 | A fair name 回望与收束 | 剩余词 + 点题搭配 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`genuine goods at a fair price / domestic sales figures / master list / freehold / demotic / kermis` 贴到老店身份与商誉；`ledger / courier / invoice / parcel / inventory / out-goings / tax deductible / surcharge / respectively` 串起记账流程；`offer / non-negotiable demand / conditional contract / irrevocable / indemnity / backdate` 构成一次艰难议价；`forward exchange facilities / underinsured / overvalued / differentiate / similarity / oversubscribed` 落到资金与风险；`count in / count on / deduct taxes from one's salary / endow with` 作动词搭配收束。

## 二、英文故事　The House on the Corner

### 1. An illustrious old firm

Mrs. Hale ran an **illustrious** old trading house that had stood on the same street for a century. She kept a **master list** of every **make** of cloth, each stamped with the firm's **mark**, and she tracked her **domestic** trade in **domestic sales figures** that never lied. The shop stood in a **freehold** building her grandfather had bought. Above the door she painted a **demotic** motto: **genuine goods at a fair price**. Every autumn the town held a **kermis**, and her stall drew the biggest crowd.

### 2. The daily ledger

Each morning Mrs. Hale opened the **ledger**. A **courier** delivered the **invoices** and a **parcel** of samples, while she checked the **inventory** against the **output** of the back workroom. From the **earnings** she subtracted the **out-goings**. When winter trade **decreased** she tightened her belt. She would **count** the coins twice, **count in** the small tips, and **count on** the regular buyers. She knew the rule that lets one **deduct taxes from one's salary**, so the levy was **tax deductible** in part; a **surcharge** on rush orders still annoyed her. In the margins she **detailed** the two branches **respectively**, so no sum was ever unclear.

### 3. A hard bargain

A buyer made an **offer** for a large order, but his **non-negotiable demand** for a discount left no room. Mrs. Hale answered with a **conditional** proposal: a **conditional contract** would be **irrevocable** once signed, and any **indemnity** for late delivery was written in. She refused to **backdate** the agreement. The buyer's **representation** that he spoke for a large chain turned out to be true, and though his flattery tempted her she would not **overpay** for praise. She **estimated** the risk carefully; when the benefit **outweighs** the cost, she told herself, one should act, and she **shortened** the delivery time to close the deal.

### 4. Money matters

With the contract signed, money matters grew complex. Her bank offered **forward exchange facilities** for the foreign payment, yet she feared being **underinsured**, for the **overvalued** stock in the window was not fully covered. A rival's **noncommittal** answer to her inquiry told her little. That rival tried to **ingratiate** himself with **gratis** samples, but she saw through the flattery. She worked hard to **differentiate** her wares: the **differentiation** lay in workmanship, for mere **similarity** of price was no advantage. Every apprentice she trained was **endowed with** patience and skill. A **halt** in deliveries only sharpened her resolve, and when the new share issue was **oversubscribed**, her careful **avoidance** of debt was rewarded.

### 5. A fair name

Years later the **illustrious** name still hung above the door. Mrs. Hale had kept her **genuine goods at a fair price** promise, and the **domestic sales figures** grew each year. The **ledger** showed that the **conditional contract** had been honored and the **indemnity** never claimed. She looked back on the **courier**, the **parcel**, the **out-goings** and the **earnings**, and knew that honesty, more than any **surcharge** or discount, had built the house. When she finally **shortened** her hours, the buyers who **counted on** her were the first to send flowers.

## 四、学习建议

1. **老店身份词**：`illustrious / freehold / demotic / kermis / master list / mark / make` 一起记，勾勒出传统商号的历史感与门面。
2. **记账流程**：`ledger → courier → invoice → parcel → inventory → output → earnings → out-goings → count / deduct / surcharge`，是旧式商务一天的完整链条。
3. **议价契约词**：`offer / non-negotiable demand / conditional contract / irrevocable / indemnity / backdate / representation / outweigh / estimate`，谈生意时最常用。
4. **资金与风险**：`forward exchange facilities`（远期外汇）、`underinsured / overvalued`（投保不足/估值过高）、`oversubscribed`（超额认购）、`avoidance`（避免）属财务风险词。
5. **动词搭配**：`count in`（算入）、`count on`（依靠）、`deduct taxes from one's salary`（从薪水扣税）、`endow with`（赋予）、`differentiate`（区分）都有固定结构，宜成组记忆。
