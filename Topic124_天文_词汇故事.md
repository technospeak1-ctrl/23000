## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"天文"，形容词里挤满了"巨大""明亮""宇宙性"的同义修饰语，名词里又是清一色的天体名，最容易写成一份星表。我以"天文馆的月食观测夜：天文学家陈博士带学生从黄昏守到黎明"为主线，按"天文馆 → 夜空 → 太阳系 → 深空 → 黎明回望"五幕推进，让每个术语都成为一次次抬头、一架仪器或一场天象：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | At the planetarium 天文馆 | 场所器材 + 大/亮类形容词 + 航空词 | 建立人物、设备与夜的起点 |
| 2 | The night sky 夜空 | 月食链 + 恒星词 + 天体搭配 | 用一场月食串起观测词 |
| 3 | The solar system 太阳系 | 行星/轨道 + 彗星流星 + 宇宙射线 | 铺陈太阳系访客 |
| 4 | Deep sky 深空 | 星际词 + 巨大类形容词 + 深空搭配 | 推向宇宙尺度与哲理 |
| 5 | Dawn and reflection 黎明回望 | 地面/气候 + 回望词 + 收尾搭配 | 收束、点题、回收搭配 |

**设计要点**：15 条搭配全部落进具体画面——`a gigantic sculpture` 立在门厅、`solar panels` 铺在屋顶、`a horizontal surface` 上架着望远镜，`a luminous body / a celestial object / a lunar eclipse / a stellar performance` 出现在观测中，`cosmic ray / spatial arrangement / tremendous events / an unbounded ocean / an exotic flavor / a universal truth` 落在深空感想里，`a climatic factor / an unconditional surrender` 留给黎明。大小形容词按"场景"分工而非 synonym 罗列；`meteor / meteorite / meteoric`、`comet / cometary`、`orbit / orbital`、`astronomy / astrology` 同框出现，形成易混对照。

## 二、英文故事　The Night of the Lunar Eclipse

### 1. At the planetarium

At the **outset** of the evening, Dr. Chen, an **astronomer** who had spent her life in **astronomy**, opened the doors of the **spacious** **planetarium**. In the lobby stood a **gigantic sculpture** of a spiral **galaxy**, and on the roof **solar panels** drank the last light. Inside, the old **astrolabe** lay beside a radio **transmitter**, while a **telescope** waited on a **horizontal surface** near the great dome. "We are **aloft** in spirit before we even begin," she smiled, watching a plane climb **aerial** routes above the **horizon**. **Aviation** had opened the sky, she told Mei, but **aerospace** had opened the **universe**.

### 2. The night sky

As dusk fell, the **cosmos** opened above them, and the first **constellation** rose over the dome. Dr. Chen pointed out the first **celestial object** — a bright **luminous body** that was **Venus** — and explained that every **celestial** point followed a **sidereal** clock. The **lunar eclipse** began slowly: the Moon entered the **penumbra**, its **sheen** dimming, then slid into **totality** while the **corona** flared like a **crescent** of fire. At the **meridian** the **phase** changed, and the **horizon** grew dark. **Innumerable** stars **glittered**; the **dazzle** of the fully eclipsed disc made the crowd gasp. "A **stellar performance** by the old moon," Mei whispered.

### 3. The solar system and its visitors

Later the **solar** family took the stage. Each **planet** kept its **orbit**, and the **orbital** path of the **planetary** **satellite** of **Jupiter** climbed toward its **apogee**; the whole system turned in a great **counterclockwise** **rotation**. **Mars** shone red, **Saturn** wore its rings, and a small **asteroid** crossed the field like a **blip** on a screen. A **comet** swept past, its **cometary** tail brightening in a sudden **flare**, and a **meteor** burned up in a **meteoric** streak while a heavier **meteorite** survived to the ground. **Cosmic rays** rained silently through the **border** of the atmosphere. Near the **horizon** an **aurora** unfolded, and Mei thought she saw a **rainbow** in the dark.

### 4. Deep sky

Past the planets lay the deep sky. Through the **observatory** dome the **telescope** found a **quasar** at an **astronomical** distance, a **nova** that had flared, and a **supernova** whose **tremendous events** still lit a whole **galaxy**. A **concentric** ring of dust surrounded it, **contiguous** with a cloud of **chaos** left by an older star. "These are **monstrous**, **titanic** things," Chen said, "yet each began as an **infinitesimal** speck." A star's death was **instantaneous** on a human clock but slow in **interstellar** time. The **spatial arrangement** of the cluster was mapped on her screen; the **peak** of the supernova had come and gone. "An **unbounded ocean** of **space**," she murmured, "with an **exotic flavor** no traveller can bottle — and one **universal truth**: every **celestial object** obeys the same law."

### 5. Dawn and reflection

At dawn a recovery **capsule** made its **touchdown** on the plain, its **translucent** shell glowing in the **sunlit** sky. The **climatic factor** that would shape the week's weather was already at work, and the **ubiquitous** light spread over everything. Chen gathered the students by the **observatory** steps. "Long ago people studied **astrology** and named stars after **Zeus**," she said. "Today we ask for the **unconditional surrender** of old myths to evidence." She pointed to the **sunlit** **horizon** where the last stars still **glittered** **aloft**, high above the **aerial** haze. The Moon was only a pale **sheen** now, but the lesson would stay: the sky is a book, and every clear night turns another page.

## 四、学习建议

1. **天体谱系一条线**：`celestial object → lunar eclipse / moon → planet / satellite → comet / meteor / meteorite → galaxy → quasar / nova / supernova → universe / cosmos`，沿"由近及远"夜空走一遍，比背星表牢得多。
2. **"巨大"形容词按场景分工**：`gigantic`（雕像）、`monstrous / titanic`（天体）、`tremendous`（事件）、`unbounded`（海洋）、`astronomical`（距离）、`spacious`（场馆）——六个词各有搭配，不要当成同义词混背。
3. **月食链串时间方位词**：`lunar eclipse → penumbra → totality → corona → crescent → phase`，再配 `sidereal`（恒星时）、`meridian`（子午线）、`horizon`（地平线），正好是一次完整的观测记录。
4. **易混辨析**：`astronomy`（天文学）与 `astrology`（占星学）只差开头；`meteor`（流星）/`meteorite`（陨石）/`meteoric`（流星的）与 `comet`（彗星）/`cometary`（彗星的）同框记；`orbit`（轨道）/`orbital`（轨道的）、`stellar`（恒星的/一流的，配 `stellar performance`）也要分清词性。
5. **15 条搭配按幕归堆**：设备（`gigantic sculpture / solar panel / horizontal surface`）→ 夜空（`celestial object / a luminous body / lunar eclipse / stellar performance`）→ 太阳系（`cosmic ray`）→ 深空（`spatial arrangement / tremendous events / unbounded ocean / exotic flavor / universal truth`）→ 回望（`climatic factor / unconditional surrender`），一幕一组， Recall 时按画面取词。
