## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"化学"，几乎全是元素名、化合物名与实验术语，稍不留神就会写成一张元素周期表般的清单。我以"化学开放日：教授带十二名学生用一天时间鉴定一种神秘白色粉末"为主线，按"准备室 → 元素墙 → 反应台 → 生命化学角 → 揭晓"五幕推进，让每个术语都成为实验动作或场景的一部分：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The preparation room 准备室 | 实验器材 + 物质形态 + 性质形容词 | 建立人物、任务与悬念 |
| 2 | The wall of elements 元素墙 | 元素/单质 + 矿物 + 有机无机 | 铺陈物质谱系 |
| 3 | The tests of change 反应台 | 反应动词 + 过程名词 + 工业词 | 用实验动作串起变化 |
| 4 | The living chemistry corner 生命化学角 | 酸碱 + 生化分子 + 生命物质 | 把化学落到人体与生活 |
| 5 | The verdict 揭晓 | 剩余形态词 + 状态词 | 收束、点题、回收搭配 |

**设计要点**：`intrude into`、`ooze out`、`rectify errors` 三条搭配落在"液体侵入裂缝—树脂渗出—学生改正笔记"的实验动作链里；`-ize` 动词族（`catalyze / crystallize / vaporize / vulcanize`）按"催化—结晶—蒸发—硫化"排成一条反应线；`organic / inorganic / hydrothermal / photochemical / molecular / chemical` 等形容词分别贴到元素墙与生命角的具体场景；`quicksilver` 写成"托盘里滚动的一滴水银"，`hem` 写成"教授抚平白大褂的衣边"，避免冷词悬空。所有元素与化合物都是"被实验涉及的东西"，不是孤立罗列。

## 二、英文故事　The Mystery Powder

### 1. The preparation room

On a cool Monday morning Professor Rowan, a **biochemist** in a crisp white coat, unlocked the door of the **laboratory**. It was Chemistry Open Day, and twelve students had come to solve one problem: identify a mystery white powder. On the demonstration bench she laid out a **cylindrical** glass **receptacle**, an **oval** porcelain dish, and a flat **rhombus** of test paper. One blue **crystal** of copper salt glinted beside a small **compound** she had prepared the night before. "The powder looks **unconsolidated**," she said, tapping the dish, "but its **extent** of solubility and **percentage** purity are exact. When it **dissolves**, watch the **viscosity** of the liquid — that will reveal the **ingredient** you are after." She smoothed the **hem** of her coat and reminded them that **chemical** work begins with **molecular** questions, not **sheer** guesswork; what seems **irrational** to a beginner is often a **theoretical** pattern waiting to be tested. Mina wrote down every word; Tom, still half asleep, fumbled with his pen.

### 2. The wall of elements

On the far wall a chart of the elements ran from **hydrogen** to **uranium**, and Rowan walked the class along it. **Carbon**, she said, was the backbone of life; **oxygen** and **nitrogen** filled the air; **chlorine** and **fluoride** guarded the water; **sulfur** and **phosphorus** fed the soil. Metals such as **magnesium**, **potassium**, **zinc**, **aluminum** and **silicon** built the solid world, while **lime** and **limestone** were quarried by the tonne. **Silica** and **silicate** formed the sand, and **nitrate** salts lay in the fertiliser bag. In the gas corner **helium**, **neon** and **radon** sat in sealed tubes beside **tritium**, a rare **isotope** of hydrogen. A drop of **quicksilver** rolled across the tray like liquid light. "Some of these elements are **organic** neighbours," she added, "others are strictly **inorganic**." She explained that **hydrothermal** vents on the seafloor and **photochemical** reactions in the upper air both make new substances, so chemistry reaches from the deep ocean to the sky.

### 3. The tests of change

Rowan lit the burner and began the tests. She asked Mina to **dissolve** a pinch of the powder in warm water; as the liquid cooled, blue crystals would **crystallize** out. Tom held a tube over the flame to **distill** off the clear **vapor**, and the heat would **vaporize** any solvent left behind; soon **condensation** gathered on the cold glass. "A catalyst can **catalyze** the same change far faster," Rowan said, dropping in a speck of **peroxide**; at once bubbles rose and a white **precipitation** settled at the bottom. Strong acid can **resolve** a mineral, she explained; over years the rain will **weather** stone, **corrosion** will eat the iron rail, and water will **intrude into** the smallest crack. On the screen, **desalination** plants drank seawater, **gasification** turned coal into gas, **smelting** yielded pure metal, and **gilding** laid a thin skin of gold on a button. Sulfur can **vulcanize** soft rubber into a tough tyre, a **thinner** cleans the brush, and **kerosene** burns in the lamp. A drop of resin began to **ooze out** of the seam. "When the **propellant** is spent and the furnace falls into **inactivity**, the reaction is over," she said. Mina checked her figures and had to **rectify errors** in her notes; the result, though **unstable** at first, settled into a clear pattern.

### 4. The living chemistry corner

The last bench belonged to life. Here Rowan kept the **biochemistry** books and introduced the class to **biochemical** reactions. "Your body is a **chemical** factory," she said. "An **enzyme** speeds up every step, and **assimilation** turns food into you." She pointed to a model of a **carbohydrate** chain and a **hydrocarbon** fuel; even a trace of **endotoxin** from a bacterium, she warned, could upset the whole system. In the acid corner stood bottles of **acetic** acid and **acidic** solutions, a jar of **alkali**, a bottle of **ammonia**, and a dish of **bicarbonate**. "An **amino** acid is the alphabet of protein," she said, and **melanin** gives your skin its colour. **Formaldehyde** preserved the specimens, **methanol** burned as a fuel, and **nicotine** — a bitter **alkaloid** in the tobacco leaf — was the poison she made them respect. Mina laughed at the idea that a **fibrous** plant could hide so much chemistry.

### 5. The verdict

By late afternoon the answer lay on the bench: the white powder was **bicarbonate**, harmless and useful. Rowan summed up the day. "A single **compound** is rarely pure," she said. "What you found is a **conglomerate** of grains, a **temporal** snapshot of a reaction that began long before you arrived." She showed a photo of **scuba** divers exploring a **hydrothermal** vent, where minerals **crystallize** in the dark, and a photo of stone sculpted by slow **weathering**. The early **presumption** that the powder was common salt had proved wrong. Chemistry, she concluded, is not a list of bottles: it is the **molecular** story of **organic** and **inorganic** matter, tested in the **laboratory** and trusted because the **percentage** of error was small. Tom, who had finally **rectified** his notes, smiled. The **sheer** pleasure of finding the answer, he wrote, was worth every minute.

## 四、学习建议

1. **元素一条线**：`hydrogen → carbon → nitrogen / oxygen → sulfur / phosphorus → magnesium / potassium / zinc / aluminum / silicon → helium / neon / radon → tritium / uranium`，按"气体—非金属—金属—稀有气体—放射性"的顺序走一遍元素墙，比孤零零背名字牢得多。
2. **`-ize` 反应动词族**：`catalyze / crystallize / vaporize / vulcanize` 都是"使……发生变化"，配 `dissolve / distill / resolve / intrude / ooze / rectify / weather` 一起记，正好是一条完整的实验动作链。
3. **酸碱与生命对照**：`acetic / acidic ↔ alkali / ammonia / bicarbonate` 是酸碱两端；`amino acid / carbohydrate / hydrocarbon / enzyme / assimilation / endotoxin` 归到"生命化学"场景，配合 `biochemistry / biochemist / biochemical` 词族一次记牢。
4. **易混辨析**：`crystal`（结晶的/晶体）与 `crystallize`（使结晶）、`vapor`（水蒸气）与 `vaporize`（蒸发）、`corrosion`（腐蚀，名词）与 `resolve`（溶解，动词）、`organic`（有机）与 `inorganic`（无机）都是词形或词义易错对，故事里都给了同框场景。
5. **三条搭配串成实验叙事**：液体 `intrude into` 裂缝 → 树脂 `ooze out` 渗出 → 学生 `rectify errors` 改正笔记；把搭配放进动作里，比背中文释义更不易忘。
