## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"产品销售 2"，词量最小（24 词），但搭配极密集（17 条），且多为合同、折扣与骗术词，最易写成"销售条款表"。我用"一位自动贩卖机销售经理从布展、签合同到识破经销商骗局、最终销量回升"作主线，按"布展 → 签约 → 骗局 → 核对细节 → 回升"五幕推进；每幕用更细的场景描写与前后呼应把 24 个词撑开，绝不空泛：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A new line of machines 新机布展 | 物品细节名词 + 预订搭配 | 建立产品 |
| 2 | The written deal 书面合同 | 合同/折扣/贸易搭配 | 铺陈交易 |
| 3 | A slippery distributor 狡猾经销商 | 欺骗动词 + 风险搭配 | 制造冲突 |
| 4 | Checking every detail 核对细节 | 校对动词 + 预算搭配 | 展开考验 |
| 5 | A resurgence 销量回升 | 收束词 + 点题 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`slot machine / awning / folder / label / grime / minutia / sort` 等名词贴到布展与验货的具体物件上；17 条搭配如 `writing contract / irrevocable letter of credit / volume discount / force majeure / countermand an order / sliding budget` 沿签约与维权流程依次出现，成为"她签了什么、取消了什么"的一部分；`cozen / inveigle / foist / stonewall / underbid / surcharge / pre-empt` 等动词落在骗局与防范的动作链里，前后反复呼应让短词表也饱满。

## 二、英文故事　The Machine That Kept Its Word

### 1. A new line of machines

Grace Lin sold **slot machine**s for a living, and her newest line needed a good **awning** over the shop door before the autumn fair. She kept every **folder** of drawings in order and checked the **label** on each machine twice. A speck of **grime** could spoil the look of any **sort** of dispenser, so she wiped the display each morning before the doors opened. She had learned to watch the **minutia** — a loose screw, a faded mark, a crooked tray — because buyers noticed such small things. Her **junk mail** bin was full of offers from dubious suppliers, but a single **advance reservation** from a hotel chain gave her real hope. She pinned that letter inside her **folder** and went to work.

### 2. The written deal

The hotel sent a **writing contract** and asked for **written permission** to place the machines in its lobby. Grace opened an **irrevocable letter of credit** at the bank and studied the **going rate** for such machines before she named her price. She offered a **volume discount** for ten units and a **wholesale discount** for twenty, knowing the **ultimate consumer** was a hotel guest who wanted a quick snack after a long flight. This was **visible trade** in the fullest sense: goods you could see, touch, and count on the counter. "A fair price builds a **resurgence** in orders," she told her small team, "and a clear **label** on every machine builds trust."

### 3. A slippery distributor

Not everyone played fair. A distributor tried to **cozen** her with praise and **inveigle** her into a side deal that would line his own pocket. When she refused, he tried to **foist** damaged units on her and **stonewall** her calls for weeks. He would **underbid** the hotel, then **surcharge** the freight later — a trick he had used on other sellers. He claimed **force majeure** when a shipment ran late, but Grace knew the **windfall loss** was his own fault, not an act of God. She had once placed an order with him in error, and now she would **countermand** it. "I **countermand an order** that was never confirmed," she wrote, citing the clause in their agreement. That afternoon she checked the **label** on every returned unit and photographed the **grime** under the trays.

### 4. Checking every detail

Grace knew the **fallibility** of any human plan, and she did not want to **err** in public again. She would **apprise** the hotel of every change and **tabulate** the delivery dates in a clean sheet. She **juxtaposed** the two price lists side by side and **melded** them into one clear chart. A small change could **transfigure** the look of a machine — a brighter **label**, a cleaner **awning** — and that was worth more than a loud promise. These careful steps would **pre-empt** complaints and **necessitate** fewer returns later. Even in a **slack season** she kept a **sliding budget**, and she relied on the **comity of nations** to settle the cross-border invoice fairly. She read the **minutia** of every clause twice before she signed.

### 5. A resurgence

By autumn there was a real **resurgence** in sales. Grace's **stinginess** with promises had become a strength: she never pledged what she could not deliver, and buyers noticed. The **ultimate consumer** kept buying, the **going rate** held steady, and the **writing contract** was renewed for another year. "The **minutia** matter," she wrote in her report, "and honesty **meld**s a business faster than any trick." She looked at the clean **awning**, the spotless **label**s, and the quiet **slot machine**s humming in the lobby, and she knew the deal had kept its word.

## 四、学习建议

1. **签约流程链**：`advance reservation`（提前预订）→ `writing contract`（书面合同）→ `written permission`（书面许可）→ `irrevocable letter of credit`（不可撤销信用证）→ `going rate`（时价）→ `volume discount / wholesale discount`（数量/批发折扣），是一条完整的销售签约线。
2. **折扣与贸易搭配**：`volume discount`（数量折扣）、`wholesale discount`（批发折扣）、`visible trade`（有形贸易）、`ultimate consumer`（最终用户）、`slack season`（淡季）、`sliding budget`（滑动预算）一起记，是销售条款高频词。
3. **风险与意外词**：`force majeure`（不可抗力）、`windfall loss`（意外损失）、`comity of nations`（国际礼让）、`sliding budget`（滑动预算）常在合同争议场景同现，配合 `countermand an order`（取消订货）一起记。
4. **骗术动词群**：`cozen / inveigle / foist / stonewall / underbid / surcharge` 串成"哄骗—诱骗—强塞—搪塞—低价抢标—加收费用"，是销售贬义动词的一条线。
5. **细节与校对动词**：`apprise`（告知）、`tabulate`（制表）、`juxtapose`（并列）、`meld`（合并）、`transfigure`（改观）、`pre-empt`（预防）、`necessitate`（使必要）、`err`（出错）配上名词 `minutia / fallibility / folder / grime / label`，把"核对细节"这一幕记牢。
