## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"论文书目"，核心是学位论文从选题、查书目、搭提纲到校订的全流程术语，极容易写成一张"论文格式清单"。我用"研究生 Maya 写一篇关于语言认知的 **dissertation**"作主线，按"注册立项 → 查卡片目录 → 搭建提纲 → 字句与细节 → 终稿审阅"五幕推进，让每一个术语都成为她手里正在处理的一张卡片、一个符号或一次与导师的商议：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | At the registrar's office 注册处 | 学籍手续 + 学位论文 + 研究方法 + 指导动词 | 建立任务与人物关系 |
| 2 | The card catalogue 卡片目录 | 书目器物 + 资料类型 + 查阅动词 | 铺陈文献搜集的日常 |
| 3 | Building the outline 搭建提纲 | 纲要名词 + 结构形容词 + 取舍动词 | 把资料组织成文章骨架 |
| 4 | Words and details 词句与细节 | 语言学术语 + 文本形容词 + 探究动词 | 深入字面与符号的微观层 |
| 5 | The final review 终稿审阅 | 修改动词 + 认知抽象词 + 评价句 | 收束并点明治学态度 |

**设计要点**：`characterize sb. as sth.`、`cite an example`、`opt for`、`overlap with` 四条搭配分别落在导师评价、举例、取舍章节和检查重合的动作里；`outline / overview / synopsis / summary / compendium` 一组"概要词"按由简到繁的顺序在第 3 幕依次登场；`abbreviation / alphabet / hyphen / syllable / transcription` 集中在第 4 幕，形成"字面学"语义场；`cognitive / schematic / synoptic / typographical / phonetic / alphabetical` 六个形容词各自贴住一个具体物件或页面。所有术语都是 Maya 真正写、读或改的东西，不是孤立罗列。

## 二、英文故事　Maya's Dissertation

### 1. At the registrar's office

Maya had come to the **registrar**'s office to **enroll in** the spring **semester**. She carried her **application** in a folder and a **notebook** full of questions. "Your **dissertation** topic is set," said Dr. Pell, "but you must settle on a **methodology** and attend a **seminar** before you begin." Maya nodded. She had spent three years in the **philosophy** department studying the **cognition** of language, and now she wanted to test a **cognitive** model against real texts. Dr. Pell **instructed** her to **review** the reading list first after a short **consultation**. "A good paper," he said, "**characterizes** its subject before it judges it." He **characterized** her as a careful reader and sent her down to the library.

### 2. The card catalogue

In the library the old **catalogue** still stood beside the computer terminal. The **librarian** showed Maya how to trace a **bibliography** on her subject and how to follow every **reference** back to its original **source**. She drew a **copy** of an **article** out of the **archives**, together with a **manifold** set of notes left by a former student. An **autobiography** of a famous **lexicographer** sat nearby, beside a heavy **encyclopedia** and a slim **compendium** of **linguistics**. On a separate shelf lay examples of **calligraphy** and a **chronology** of early printing. Maya **skimmed** the cards and then stopped at a **marginal** note in a familiar hand; she wrote a **brief** of what she had found and resolved to **cite an example** from it. A **biometrics** report on handwriting pressure caught her eye for a moment, but she turned back to her own subject.

### 3. Building the outline

Back at her desk, Maya thought through the **design** of the paper. She wrote an **outline**, then a fuller **overview**, and at last a **synopsis** she could keep on a single card. A **summary** of each section went into the margin, and a **synoptic** table showed how the chapters related to one another. She drafted a **preface**, a **foreword**, and a short **preamble** before the first chapter. The **content** page listed every heading, and an **extraction** from a longer book served as her main evidence. "The **principle** is simple," she told her roommate: "state the **demand** your subject makes on a reader, then answer it with **detail**." A **schematic** diagram helped her see the whole shape at once. When two chapters **overlapped with** each other a second time, she **opted for** the shorter version. She knew a **majority** of her classmates would keep the longer one, and a third **alternative** had even been suggested, but she had her own reasons.

### 4. Words and details

This was the part Maya loved most. She **abbreviated** the long titles and checked every **abbreviation** against the **alphabet**, putting the entries in **alphabetical** order. A single **hyphen** changed the meaning of one term, and one **syllable** mattered in the **phonetic** **transcription** she had made. That **transcription** was full of **irregularity**, and the **typographical** **marking** in the old edition was hard to read. She found that an **antonym** could clarify a definition as well as a synonym, and that an **adverbial** **clause** often carried the writer's real intention. A **conjunction** joined two ideas that did not quite agree, and a **complicated** rule behind it proved **intricate** rather than arbitrary. The **lexicographer** in the autobiography had spent forty years on just such **detail**, and Maya **apprehended** at last why the work mattered. She worked out a simple **formula** for citing sources, added a **glossary** of her own, and **affixed** a note to the final page.

### 5. The final review

On the night before the deadline, Maya **revised** the whole draft. She **paraphrased** two long quotations and **emended** three **typographical** errors she had missed. She **reviewed** every page once more and let her mind **recur** to the question that had started it all: how does a reader **apprehend** an **intricate** argument? Her supervisor **enlightened** her with a single comment in the **marginal** space, and she felt completely **engrossed** in the work again. She had **opted for** clarity over display, and she was glad of it. The next morning she handed in the **dissertation** with its **bibliography**, its **glossary**, and a **reference** list in **alphabetical** order. "You have **characterized** the field honestly," Dr. Pell said when he read it, "and that is a **complicated** thing to do."

## 四、学习建议

1. **一条论文流水线**：`registrar → enroll → semester → seminar → methodology → dissertation → outline → reference → bibliography → revise → emend`，把第 1、3、5 幕按真实时间顺序串背，术语就不再是一张清单。
2. **"概要词"分阶记**：`brief`（最短）→ `summary`（摘要）→ `synopsis`（大纲）→ `outline`（提纲）→ `overview`（概述）→ `compendium`（纲要）→ `encyclopedia`（百科），按篇幅从短到长排列；形容词 `synoptic / schematic` 分别配 `synoptic table / schematic diagram`。
3. **字面学一组**：`alphabet → alphabetical → abbreviation → abbreviate → hyphen → syllable → phonetic → transcription → irregularity → typographical → marking`，全收在第 4 幕，靠"字母—缩写—音节—转写—排印"的层次一次记牢。
4. **易混辨析**：`cite`（引用）与 `reference`（参考文献）、`source`（出处）是同一件事的三个角度；`revise`（修订）、`emend`（校订）、`paraphrase`（改写）都指改文字，但目的不同——改结构、改错字、换说法。
5. **动词搭配优先**：`characterize sb. as sth.`、`cite an example`、`opt for`、`overlap with` 四条都是写作高分表达，直接套用在自己的读书报告里最有效；`enroll in` 也常和 `course / program` 连用。
