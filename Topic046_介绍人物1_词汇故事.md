## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"人物品质形容词 + 人物名词"，最容易写成清单。我的解法是让它成为一场**肖像展**：老画家 Ruth 受镇长之邀，为镇上九个人画像并题词，每幅画题一句点破其性格。正面与负面品质在相邻画框里形成对照——`virtuous` 对 `vicious`、`reliable` 对 `untrustworthy`、`sincere` 对 `pretentious`。人物名词（`genius`、`clown`、`miser` 型人物、`gentleman`）成为被画的"人"。

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The commission 受邀 | 正面品质 + 人物名词（身份） | 建立框架 |
| 2 | The admired 令人敬重者 | 卓越/可靠/正直形容词 + 品质名词 | 正面群像 |
| 3 | The flawed 有瑕疵者 | 负面品质形容词 + 缺点名词 | 反面群像 |
| 4 | The odd ones 古怪者 | 气质/神态/脾气词 | 中间地带 |
| 5 | The last portrait 最后一幅 | 剩余词 + 升华 | 收束主题 |

**设计要点**：把 `as sober as a judge`、`as stubborn as a mule`、`a tough nut`、`a shrewd businessman`、`a reliable friend` 等搭配直接写进题词；`appearance / dignity / figure / temperament / virtue / weakness / willpower` 等名词成为 Ruth 落笔时权衡的"维度"。

## 二、英文故事　The Portrait Gallery

### 1. The commission

Ruth was a **remarkable** old painter, **renowned** in the county and **preeminent** among her peers, though she remained **obscure** outside it. The mayor, a **dignified** figure with real **dignity**, asked her to paint the town's most memorable **figures**. "Capture each **appearance**," he said, "but show the **quality** underneath." Ruth was **scrupulous** in her work and **steadfast in** her promise to be honest. She was also **sincere**, **upright**, and **sociable** — a **sociable child** at heart — yet **reticent about** her own **genius**. A true **gentleman**, the mayor was **obliging** and ** courteous**; he trusted her **willpower** and her **sapient** eye. Ruth had been **studious** and **versatile**, a **versatile actor** in her youth; her **slim** figure and **virile** energy hid her years, and her **sturdy** hands never tired. Now she would paint ten portraits, one for each kind of person. **Vital** energy still burned in her; her hand was **robust**, her mind **sober**, **as sober as a judge**.

### 2. The admired

The first portrait was Dr. Hale, an **estimable** man and a **reliable friend** whose **remarkable achievements** in medicine were known to all. He was **conscientious** and **scrupulous in** his work, **precise** and **thorough**, never **oblique** in his answers. Next came Mrs. Lin, an **obliging**, **sympathetic** woman, **sympathetic to** every sufferer, **tender** as **a tender kiss**, with **slender fingers** and a **gentle** **temperament**. Her **virtue** was patience; her **strength** was kindness. Then the **shrewd businessman** Mr. Vale — **shrewd**, **sophisticated**, a **sophisticated columnist** in his spare time — whose **straightforward** deals and **outspoken** honesty made him **preeminent** in trade. Beside him hung the **youthful**, **idealistic** teacher, **devoted** and **industrious**, whose **loyalty** was **steadfast** and whose **straightforward**, **righteous** honesty won every pupil's trust. Ruth painted a **genius** inventor, **ingenious** and **vigorous**, with an **agile** mind and a **promising** future; a **steadfast** soldier, **upright** and **valiant**, **tenacious** under fire; and a **saintly** widow, **virtuous**, **benevolent**, **charitable**, her **faith** unshaken. The mayor's **outright** manner and **undisguised** pride in the town pleased her; even his **rhetoric** was honest. Each had **integrity**; each had **willpower**; each, in Ruth's **objective** eye, was **admirable**.

### 3. The flawed

The next wall was darker. There was Mr. Gripe, a **petty**, **snobbish** man, **pretentious** in a **pretentious manner**, whose **vanity** — **a vain girl**'s vanity grown old — showed in every **gesture**. Beside him, the **ruthless** moneylender, **vicious**, leading **a vicious life**, **ruthless** to debtors and **cruel** in his **greed**; Ruth painted the **menace** in his eyes. A **fraudulent** clerk hung nearby, **untrustworthy**, **deceptive**, his **hypocrisy** plain, a **thief** in a **shabby** coat. There was a **libertine** with a **licentious** past, a **wicked** neighbour, **a wicked person** who spread **slander**; a **bigoted**, **narrow** councillor, **arrogant** and **haughty**, drunk on his own **megalomania**. One figure was **outrageous**, an **outrageous** bully with a **rude** tongue; another **brutal**, **savage**, a **thug** with a **villainous** face, whose **relentless** cruelty knew no limit. A **sequacious** follower hung beside a **tame**, obedient clerk — both content to let others think. Ruth did not flatter: she painted their **weakness**, their **inertia**, their **ignorance**, their **prejudice** and **folly**. Their **notoriety**, she wrote beneath, was deserved. Their **inability** to feel **sympathy** was the truest **disgrace**.

### 4. The odd ones

Between the two walls stood the curious souls. A **reticent** poet, **taciturn** and **shy**, whose **timidity** kept him silent; a **skeptical** scholar, **skeptical** of every claim, **cynical** but **sapient**; a **stubborn** old mule of a man — **as stubborn as a mule** — whose **obstinate** pride and **unyielding** will made him **a tough nut**. There was a **sluggish**, **lazy** dreamer, **slothful** and **indolent**, whose only **pastime** was sleep, and a **nimble**, **slippery** trickster, **sly** and **tricky**, whose **cunning** hid a **deceitful** heart. A **clown** of a man made the children laugh, though his **preference** for cruel jokes made others wince — a joke that stuck in the **throat**. An **eccentric** inventor, **whimsical** and **absurd**, wore a **stiff collar** and a **stiff** smile; a **meticulous** accountant was **fussy** and **precise**. One was **sensitive**, **hypersensitive** to criticism; another **unconcerned**, **indifferent** to praise. A **melancholy** widow, **pessimistic** about tomorrow, **be pessimistic about** everything; next to her an **optimistic** boy, **an optimistic attitude** written on his face, **jaunty** and **bouncing**. Ruth stepped back: their **temperaments** differed as their **faces** did, and each **idiosyncrasy** deserved its frame.

### 5. The last portrait

The final canvas was of Ruth herself. She painted a **single**, **independent** woman — **single** by choice, **unprejudiced** in judgment, **outspoken** when it mattered and **straightforward** always. Her hair was **slender**-threaded with grey, her frame **spare** and **stark**, her gaze **stern** yet **tender**. She had known **uncertainty**, **anxiety**, even **uneasy** nights, but her **resolution** never broke; **undaunted**, she kept her **rigid** standards and **stern** honesty. She thought of the **pygmy** tyrants and the **genius** saints she had painted, of **obedient** children **obedient to** their parents and **rebellious** youths who were **unruly**. "A person," she wrote at the bottom, "is not one **quality** but many." She set down her brush, **relieved** and **touched**, her **conscience** clear. The gallery opened at dawn; by noon the whole town had come to see **appearance** and **virtue**, **weakness** and **willpower**, all hanging side by side — and, for the first time, to judge a **figure** by the **temperament** behind the face.

## 四、学习建议

1. **同一维度的正反对照**：`virtuous ↔ vicious`、`reliable ↔ untrustworthy`、`sincere ↔ pretentious`、`optimistic ↔ pessimistic`、`upright ↔ crooked`——故事把正反人物挂在相邻画框，建议按这种"对子"记忆。
2. **品质名词搭配形容词**：`virtue / weakness / integrity / dignity / temperament / willpower` 是描述人的六个维度；`a person of virtue`（有德之人）、`strength of willpower`（意志力）是常见表达。
3. **明喻强化**：`as sober as a judge`（像法官一样清醒）、`as stubborn as a mule`（像骡子一样固执）——英语常用"as+adj+as+名词"强化品质。
4. **"a + adj + 名词"人物标签**：`a reliable friend`、`a shrewd businessman`、`a tough nut`、`a wicked person`、`a vain girl`——用一个形容词给人贴标签是介绍人物的高频句型。
5. **未逐一入文的词条**：`clown`、`pastime`、`preference`、`throat`、`rhetoric`、`pathetically`、`relentless`、`rude`、`sturdy`、`tenacious`、`righteous` 建议复述时补入，例如 "the **clown** made them laugh"、"Reading was her **pastime**"、"his **preference** for tea"、"a lump in her **throat**"、"empty **rhetoric**"、"a **relentless** pursuit"。
6. **搭配再造句**：`petty bourgeois`、`political rhetoric`、`a rude behavior`、`a stiff neck`、`a superior official`、`a vital wound` 建议各造一句——它们是介绍人物时描述身份、行为的地道短语。
