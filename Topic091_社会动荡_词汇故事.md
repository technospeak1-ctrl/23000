## 一、编排逻辑

本主题围绕"社会动荡"，词可归为四类：动荡性质形容词、冲突与困境名词、对抗与化解动词、剩余词。我用"外国记者约纳斯报道 VEYR 王国饥荒之年从民怨到改革的全过程"作主线，按"民怨积累 → 街头冲突 → 谈判僵局 → 转机救援 → 余波反思"推进，让饥荒、暴动、谈判、改革在同一因果链中依次展开：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The gathering storm 山雨欲来 | 困境名词 + 负面性质形容词 | 民怨积累 |
| 2 | The streets erupt 街头爆发 | 冲突名词 + 对抗动词 | 矛盾激化 |
| 3 | The deadlock 僵局 | 谈判名词 + 对抗动词 | 对峙不下 |
| 4 | The turning 转机 | 救援名词 + 化解动词 | 出现希望 |
| 5 | What remained 余波 | 剩余词 + 升华 | 收束主题 |

**设计要点**：`be confronted with`、`deter sb. from`、`summon up`、`stash away`、`degenerate into` 等搭配自然嵌入；`famine / privation / destitute / mendicant` 构成"饥荒苦难网"，`clash / tumult / turmoil / outcry / defiance / animus` 构成"冲突情绪网"；`aggravate / assail / provoke / disperse / repel` 与 `disarm / dispel / reconcile / rescue` 在"激化—化解"中形成正反对照。全篇对暴力与动荡只作事实陈述。

## 二、英文故事　The Kingdom of Ash

### 1. The gathering storm

Jonas arrived in the **kingdom** of Veyr in the year of the **famine**. The fields had failed, and **privation** stalked the villages; **destitute** families sold their last tools, and **mendicants** crowded the temple steps. The **inequity** of the tax system was plain: the rich grew fat while the poor were left to **starve**. A **designing** minister had **aggravated** the crisis by hiding the **aggravation** in official reports, and his **cupidity** knew no limit. The crown had **oppressed** the peasants for decades. When a **scandal** broke, the **irreverence** of the crowd turned to anger, and the **stigma** of corruption clung to the court. Jonas **disseminated** the story in his dispatches, but the censor ordered an **interception** of every letter. A **menace** hung in the air, and the **ambition** of the young officers made them bolder each day.

### 2. The streets erupt

By spring the **turmoil** had begun. A **belligerent** crowd gathered at the palace gate, their **defiance** loud and **unrestrained**. Some **demurred** at the use of force; others **assailed** the soldiers with stones. The **outcry** grew **uproarious** as the **clash** spread from the square to the market, and the **tumult** of the streets swallowed every voice. Troops tried to **disperse** the people and to **repel** the attackers, but the **shattered** windows and **destructive** fires showed how thin the government's control had become. An **atrocity** was reported at the river bridge, and the defiance of the crowd hardened into **animus**. The minister tried to **dispel** the rumours, to **disarm** the guards and to **extinguish** the anger, but it was too late. A **blockage** of the main road cut the city in two, and every **disturbance** was now a **disorder** the police could not contain. The king's **deterrent** — a public hanging — only served to **provoke** more anger, and he could not **deter** the crowds.

### 3. The deadlock

The talks began in a hall ringed by soldiers. The rebels presented an **ultimatum** and refused to **collude** with the crown; the king would not **confront** the truth of his own **regimentation**. Each side tried to **override** the other, and a complete **deadlock** followed. A **tangle** of **interference** and **mischance** — a leaked letter — made the **implication** clear: there had been a **defection** in the cabinet. The **proponent** of reform urged **temporizing**, but the king moved to **revoke** the tax, and his officers **contradicted** him and **despoiled** the granaries. The long **captivity** of political prisoners became a bargaining chip. Rebels began to **collide** with the guards; one commander threatened to **abdicate**. Caught in the **trap** of protocol, the two sides **extruded** blame onto each other, and the **interception** of a peace messenger made the deadlock worse. A frightened clerk began to **squeal** to the police.

### 4. The turning

A **rescue** came from an unexpected quarter. The **emergency** relief committee, with its **graduated** plan of aid, began to **stash** food in the villages. A **remission** of taxes was announced, and the king was forced to **alienate** his own minister to save the throne. An old soldier tried to **avenge** the dead, but the **proponent** of peace **summoned** the courage to speak. Jonas watched as an angry crowd was **captivated** by a single voice; the animus in their eyes gave way to something like hope. The **ostracism** of the corrupt minister was demanded in the square. In the turmoil, a **fiasco** had become a **rescue**; what had seemed **insuperable** now looked possible.

### 5. What remained

The **disarray** of those months slowly gave way to order. The **incongruent** banners of the old regime were taken down; the **transmuted** anger of the people was turned into patient work. A mob that had **pulverized** the gates dispersed at last. One officer who had **abetted** the riot was **bereaved** of his rank; another **deteriorated** into a bitter exile. The two parties **recombined** into a fragile council, and the **implications** of the famine were at last debated in public. The reforms would **entail** years of patience, but Jonas wrote that the remission of one tax had done more than a hundred **ultimatums**: a kingdom is not held by **deterrents** but by the daily rescue of ordinary trust. He closed his dispatch with the words he had heard in the square: "We were **shattered**, but we are not **destitute**."

## 四、学习建议

1. **苦难与冲突链**：`famine → privation → destitute → mendicant → starve` 写饥荒苦难，`clash → tumult → turmoil → outcry → defiance → animus → atrocity` 写冲突升级；按"从缺粮到爆发"的顺序记。
2. **激化与化解对照**：`aggravate / assail / provoke / repel` 是把事情推向暴力，`disarm / dispel / extinguish / rescue / reconcile` 是往回收；故事第 2 幕与第 4 幕正好形成正反两组。
3. **谈判僵局词**：`ultimatum`（最后通牒）、`deadlock`（僵局）、`interference`（干涉）、`tangle`（乱局）、`trap`（圈套）都出现在第 3 幕谈判场景，适合成组记忆。
4. **抽象名词辨义**：`implication`（含义）、`stigma`（耻辱）、`ostracism`（放逐）、`remission`（赦免）是高频抽象词，注意与具体动作词的搭配。
5. **易混词**：`turmoil`（骚动）与 `tumult`（喧闹）近义但侧重不同，`disorder` 与 `disturbance` 都指混乱；`deter`（威慑）常接 `deter sb. from doing`。
