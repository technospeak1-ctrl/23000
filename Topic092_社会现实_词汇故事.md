## 一、编排逻辑

本主题围绕"社会现实"，词可归为四类：现实性质形容词、社会现象名词、揭示与改变动词、搭配。我用"记者娜迪亚为一份城市特写系列走访贫民区、揭露挪用救济金、见证艰难改革"作主线，按"城市黎明 → 街头人群 → 事件与清查 → 艰难的和平 → 结果"推进，让废墟、难民、腐败、改革在同一因果链中依次出现：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The city at dawn 城市黎明 | 城市废墟名词 + 状态形容词 | 建立场景 |
| 2 | The people 街头人群 | 人物名词 + 品性形容词 | 呈现众生 |
| 3 | The reckoning 清算 | 事件名词 + 揭露动词 | 矛盾爆发 |
| 4 | The difficult peace 艰难的和平 | 必然性形容词 + 反思名词 | 改革尝试 |
| 5 | The outcome 结果 | 剩余词 + 升华 | 收束主题 |

**设计要点**：`a formidable task`、`intense heat`、`an incendiary bomb`、`indecent words`、`a reckless driver`、`be implicit in`、`equivalent election` 等搭配自然嵌入；`ruins / grid / tunnel / locality` 构成"城市空间网"，`refugee / prostitute / mendicant / innocence / morality` 构成"街头人物网"；`perpetrate / hoodwink / misrepresent / castigate / scrutinize` 与 `reconcile / effect / rescue / emerge` 在"揭露—改革"中形成对照。全篇以社会特写的客观笔调陈述现实。

## 二、英文故事　The City We Built

### 1. The city at dawn

Nadia climbed the hill above the city at dawn. Below her lay the **ruins** of the old quarter, its streets a broken **grid** that no map could straighten. Smoke rose from an **incendiary** fire at the docks, and the **foul** smell of the river reached her even here. This **locality** had once been a **front-line** town; now it was a place of **rentable** rooms and empty promises. The **intense** heat of summer pressed down on the roofs. **Fleet**-footed children ran between the **tunnels** where families **huddled** at night. Merchants had **colonized** the river bank with warehouses. Nadia had come to write about the **breakup** of a neighbourhood and the **transition** that followed. She **ascribed** the city's troubles to a **misconception** about poverty.

### 2. The people

In the market she met a **refugee** family who had **endured** a long **ordeal** to reach the city. The mother **trembled** as she spoke of the **load** she carried — not the sacks on her back but the **consciousness** of what she had lost. Her daughter, barely grown, worked nights; Nadia did not ask what she did, for **innocence** here was a luxury. A **boorish** shopkeeper shouted at them, and a **nuisance** of a man followed them home. A **prostitute** at the corner watched them with tired eyes. Yet the family's **morality** was stronger than the **obsession** of the crowd with cheap **gambles**. When a charity worker **recognized** their need, the **rejection** they had known gave way to a fragile **salvation**. The **contrite** landlord, ashamed of his own **indecent** rent, lowered the price — a small act that gave the children a place to **emerge** from the dark.

### 3. The reckoning

The **incident** at the warehouse changed everything. A **reckless** driver had struck a cart there, and the blaze had **inflicted** heavy losses. The **trigger** for the unrest, however, was a rumour that the **autonomous** council had **perpetrated** a **depredation** on the relief funds, and that hidden **intrigue** lay behind the contracts. A crowd **burst** into the square, and officials tried to **blur** the facts and **hoodwink** the public with **misrepresented** figures. A **bewildered** clerk could not explain the accounts. Nadia **castigated** the lie in her column, and the **scrutiny** that followed could no longer be **ignored**. She ascribed the anger to a misconception that the poor must always be to blame — a misconception she had herself **misconceived** in her first reports. Now she **proposed** a **reconciliation**: the council would **effect** real changes and **generate** new **incentives** for work, and an **infringement** of the workers' rights would no longer go unpunished.

### 4. The difficult peace

The road ahead was **formidable**. Old habits were **irrevocable**, and the **inevitable** resistance of the powerful made reform **inescapable** yet slow. **Ironically**, the **inexcusable** delays of the council gave the **dauntless** reformers time to organize. The two sides could not **reconcile** their differences, and a **perpetual** fog of doubt hung over the hall, but Nadia refused to **inhibit** her pen. She wrote that an **equivalent** sacrifice was required of every citizen, that every **facet** of the problem had to be named, and that the **converse** of every claim should be tested. **Whence** came this courage? From the **corpus** of ordinary kindness that no report could measure. At last the **manifestation** of hope appeared in the unlikeliest places.

### 5. The outcome

Years later, the **outcome** was still incomplete. New problems **arise** with every season, and the city will always **outdo** the plans made for it. But the **tunnel** at the end of the street now held lights, the **grid** had been redrawn, and children no longer **huddled** in the dark. Nadia looked back at the **ruins** and saw not an ending but a **transition** — a **perpetual**, imperfect, human **salvation** that begins whenever one person refuses to **ignore** another.

## 四、学习建议

1. **城市空间词**：`ruins / grid / tunnel / locality / front-line / rentable` 一起构成社会特写的"舞台"，建议先记住场景，再往里填人物与事件。
2. **揭露动词链**：`perpetrate`（犯下）→ `hoodwink / misrepresent`（欺骗歪曲）→ `blur`（模糊）→ `castigate`（痛斥）→ `scrutiny / recognize`（审查认清），是报道腐败题材的高频动词串。
3. **必然性形容词语气梯度**：`inevitable`（必然）→ `inescapable`（不可逃避）→ `irrevocable`（不可撤销）→ `inexcusable`（不可原谅），语气层层加重。
4. **抽象名词辨义**：`manifestation`（显现）、`corpus`（全集）、`converse`（反面）、`facet`（方面）、`whence`（根源）是书面语高频词，注意与故事中具体场景的对应。
5. **易混词**：`inevitable` 与 `inescapable` 都译"不可避免"，前者强调"注定发生"，后者强调"无法躲开"；`rejection`（拒绝）与 `reconciliation`（和解）在故事中一前一后，形成对照。
