## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"企业兼并"，金融与法律术语极多，最容易写成"并购词典"。我用"海尔食品公司的财务总监多拉·黑尔收购濒临破产的布拉姆靴厂"作主线，按"初步报价 → 拍卖竞标 → 查账与合同 → 正式兼并 → 一年之后"五幕推进，让每个术语成为人物在并购中做出的一次判断：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | An insolvent offer 一份棘手报价 | 价格/状态形容词 + 母公司 | 建立人物 |
| 2 | The auction 拍卖场 | 收购/拍卖/卡特尔词 | 铺陈交易 |
| 3 | The books 账本与合同 | 会计/合同/资产词 | 尽职调查 |
| 4 | The merger 正式兼并 | 兼并/整合/清算动词 | 情节高潮 |
| 5 | One company 一年之后 | 剩余词 + 收束 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`insolvent / interim / probable / cost-effective / contractual` 五个形容词分别贴到目标公司（破产、临时管理）与交易判断（大概成本、划算价格、合同义务）上；`auction / auctioneer / cartel / bid bond / floor price` 集中在拍卖一幕；`bookkeeper / audit / annual accounting / consolidated accounts / active assets` 落在查账一幕；`merge / coalesce / absorb / cede / alienate / capitalize / liquidate / economize` 八个动词串成"兼并—整合—收缩"的完整决策链；`accelerated depreciation / acceptance credit / agency commission / Chamber of Commerce / against all risks` 等搭配全部成为并购中真实出现的文件与机构。

## 二、英文故事　The Boot Factory Deal

### 1. An insolvent offer

Dora Hale, finance chief of Hale Foods, was called to a **landmark** meeting about Bram Boots, a small firm that was plainly **insolvent**. Its owner, Victor Bram, had run it as an **interim** manager since his partner left, and the **difference** between its debts and its assets was alarming. Dora thought a takeover was **probable**, but she asked for a **probable cost** before she would sign anything. The **parent company** had already sent a team to look over the books. "A **cost-effective** rescue is still a rescue," she said, and she studied the **cost-effective price** Victor had written on a card.

### 2. The auction

Because Bram Boots owed money, the bank arranged an **auction**. The **auctioneer** stood on a crate and called for bids, while rival buyers formed a quiet **cartel** to keep the price low. Hale Foods paid a **bid bond** for the right to enter, and the bank set a **floor price** that nobody could go below. Victor once enjoyed a **captive market** in the valley, and the stock was insured **against all risks**. Dora offered **acceptance credit** to win the seller's trust, and an **agency contract** named an agent who would earn an **agency commission**; it even excused a late delivery caused by an **accident beyond control**. The **Chamber of Commerce** sent an observer. The **acquisition** cost more than she had hoped, and the **acquisition cost** would have to be justified at the next board meeting. Victor watched the crowd in silence.

### 3. The books

Before the deal could close, a **bookkeeper** laid out the ledgers. Dora hired an outside firm to **audit** them, and the **annual accounting** showed a thin **annual income** against a heavy **annual expenditure**. The **active assets** were few; most of the value sat in old machines under **accelerated depreciation**. Hale Foods could not gain full **access** to the factory until the **contractual obligation** to the workers was settled, and the **contractor** who maintained the roof held a long **continuation** of his service **contract**. Dora checked the **acceptability** of every clause, then gave her **acceptance** with one condition: the **continuous process** on the shop floor must not stop. She also noted that the **charge** for raw leather was too high, and that a **campaign** to cut waste would come later. There was a real **difference** between saving a firm and stripping it. **Consolidated accounts** would tell the true story, and **corporation tax** would be due on any profit. The men were depressed.

### 4. The merger

The **merger** was signed in spring. Hale Foods would **merge** with Bram Boots, and the two workshops would **coalesce into** one larger plant. Dora planned to **absorb** the smaller firm completely, but she would not **alienate** the skilled workers by dismissing them at once. She put fresh **capital** into the line and decided to **capitalize** on the valley's loyal buyers. Victor agreed to **cede** his share of the business, and a **liquidator** was named in case a full **liquidation** ever became necessary. Nothing would be **liquidated** while the plant was still earning. To **economize on costs** she ended the leather **contract** with the dear supplier — a **contraction** of the supply chain that she hoped would not **accelerate** the decline of the town. It was a hard month.

### 5. One company

By summer the **cartel** had broken, the **auctioneer** had gone home, and the **landmark** deal was studied at the **annual general meeting**. Dora reported that the **acquisition** had saved twelve jobs and that the **acceptance** of the workers mattered as much as the **acceptance credit** she had offered. The **bookkeeper** now prepared a single set of **consolidated accounts**, and the **parent company** was pleased. Victor stayed on as an adviser, careful not to **alienate** the men he used to lead. Dora often thought of the **difference** between a **continuous process** and a **contraction**: a firm must **economize** without losing the skill that made it worth an **acquisition** in the first place. The **Chamber of Commerce** called it a model **merger**. The board spoke of expansion. She called it a day's honest work.

## 四、学习建议

1. **并购动词一条链**：`merge`（合并）→ `coalesce into`（并入）→ `absorb`（吞并）→ `capitalize`（获利）→ `economize on costs`（节约成本）→ `contraction`（收缩），正好是一次兼并从牵手到整合的全过程。
2. **清算与转让辨析**：`liquidation / liquidator / liquidate` 是"清算/清算人/清算"三词同根；`cede`（让出股份）与 `alienate`（转让财产）都涉及所有权变化，但感情色彩不同。
3. **财务文件搭配**：`annual accounting`（年度决算）、`consolidated accounts`（综合账目）、`active assets`（活动资产）、`accelerated depreciation`（加速折旧）、`corporation tax`（公司税）是财报高频词，建议整串背。
4. **交易保障搭配**：`bid bond`（投标保证金）、`floor price`（底价）、`acceptance credit`（承兑信用证）、`against all risks`（保全险）、`accident beyond control`（不可抗力）都出现在拍卖与合同一幕，场景化记忆更牢。
5. **价格判断词**：`probable cost`（大致成本）、`cost-effective price`（划算价格）、`charge`（索价）与 `difference`（差额）串起"估价—比较—拍板"的思维线，适合放在一段里对照复习。
