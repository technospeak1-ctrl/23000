## 一、编排逻辑

本主题围绕"天气状况"，可归为 4 个语义场：气象观测与时间、冷热干湿形容词、天象与降水名词、风与水的动作。用一位气象学者在观测站工作一整年的主线，按"春 → 夏 → 秋 → 冬 → 年度报告"推进，让四季各承载一组天气词：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Spring at the station 春季观测 | 气象/时间名词 + 温和天气形容词 | 建立情境（观测站） |
| 2 | A muggy summer 闷热之夏 | 炎热潮湿词 + 降水名词 | 冲突一（酷暑暴雨） |
| 3 | An autumn tempest 秋日风暴 | 风暴名词 + 寒冷形容词 | 冲突二（狂风降温） |
| 4 | A frigid winter 严寒之冬 | 严寒词 + 冰雪名词 | 收束（极端天气） |
| 5 | The annual report 年度报告 | 剩余搭配与总结词 | 升华（一年数据） |

**设计要点**：把反义天气词（sweltering 与 frigid、humid 与 foggy、muggy 与 nippy）放进相邻季节，形成对照；`an apparent change`、`optimum environment`、`significant difference` 等搭配承担报告的结论句。

## 二、英文故事　A Year at the Weather Station

### 1. Spring at the station

Dr. Lena Vale's **meteorological** team reached the **suburban** station in **springtime** to study the **air** above a **marsh**. At **dawn**, in **crepuscular** light, the sky was **azure**; by **daybreak** a **drizzly** haze had arrived. The **chill** was **nippy**, and the instruments were laid in a **horizontal position** while rain fell. "We need **an apparent change** in **temperature**," Lena said, "and **a chilly morning** is a good start." The **humidity** rose; **moisture** was **freshly** visible on the grass. **Convection** built **cumulus** towers, and a light **drizzle** began. The ground turned **soggy**; the wind began to **blow** from a new **direction**. Their readings came **annually**, and the **alternate** days were fair — the sky **alternately** cleared and clouded. They traced **diurnal** cycles, measured **precipitation**, and sealed samples in **pneumatic** tubes kept in the **optimum environment** of the lab.

### 2. A muggy summer

By July the **heat** was **sweltering** and **torrid**; a **sizzler** of a day made the **stuffy** tent **muggy**. **Sunshine** flooded the **terrain** while the **air** grew **humid** and **atmospheric pollution** hung above the town. The **centigrade** reading climbed past thirty; it was **evident** that a **significant difference** from spring had arrived. When a **cloudburst** broke at **sunset**, **thunder** rolled and **rain** came down in a sudden **downfall**; a strong **gust** struck the tent. They **dampened** the charts, but water **dripped** from the roof, and the **swirl** of muddy water **hampered** their work. In the **gloaming** they noted a **noticeable change** and a **dire** drop in pressure; the camp was becoming **nonviable**. A cold **mistral** rose, **piercing** despite the **sultry** afternoon, and the **windy** night kept them awake.

### 3. An autumn tempest

Autumn brought a **tempest**. The sky was **foggy** at **sunrise**; a **clammy** dawn gave way to hours of **drizzle**, and the **air** felt **relatively** cold. The wind **blustered** all day, and sleet began to **freeze** on the metal frames. By **twilight** the cold was **frigid** and nearly **perishing**; what had been **rain** now fell as **snow**. A **conspicuous** gap in the records **hampered** the forecast, but the **pronounced** shift was clear. **Sunlight** was weak, and at **dusk** the storm **blustered** itself out. The **atmospheric** pressure settled, and the team finally slept.

### 4. A frigid winter

Winter was **frigid** and **perishing**. A **chilly**, **foggy** morning followed another; the **air** was **piercing**, and a **downfall** of **snow** covered the **terrain** in a **horizontal** sheet. They read the **temperature** in **centigrade**, watched weak **sunrise** light, and stored more samples in the **pneumatic** tubes. The **marsh** froze over, the **moisture** in the air turned to frost, and every **noticeable change** was logged. Even the **mistral** seemed gentler than the autumn **tempest**.

### 5. The annual report

At year's end Lena wrote the report that the team published **annually**. It showed a **significant difference** between **springtime** and winter, and traced **an apparent change** in every **atmospheric** measure. **Atmospheric pollution** had **hampered** work at the **optimum environment** of the station, yet the data held: the **alternate** wet and dry **diurnal** cycles, the **crepuscular** **gloaming**, the **horizontal position** of the rain, and the **conspicuous** **cumulus** towers were all recorded. A **noticeable change** was **evident** in the **humidity**, and the **pneumatic** tubes hummed as they sealed the last sample. Lena dipped her pen in **freshly** made ink and concluded: from **sultry**, **sweltering**, **torrid** days to **frigid**, **perishing**, **nippy** nights; from **clammy** and **soggy** ground to **stuffy**, **muggy** rooms; from **humid**, **foggy**, **drizzly** air to **windy**, blustering storms — the year had offered **relatively** **significant** proof that weather is never still, and that even a **dire**, **nonviable** season has its **optimum environment** for those who watch closely. At **sunrise** on the last day, under an **azure** sky, she closed the book on the **sunset** of another year of **springtime**-to-winter observation.

## 四、学习建议

1. 按四季记词：春（springtime、dawn、drizzle、humidity）→ 夏（sweltering、muggy、cloudburst、thunder）→ 秋（tempest、foggy、bluster、freeze）→ 冬（frigid、perishing、snow、piercing）。
2. 反义对照：sweltering/torrid 与 frigid/perishing，humid/muggy 与 foggy/clammy，成组记忆更牢。
3. 搭配优先：7 条搭配中，`an apparent change`、`optimum environment`、`significant difference` 是学术写作常见表达。
4. 词形意识：过去式要会认（drip→dripped，dampen→dampened，bluster→blustered，hamper→hampered，freeze→froze 的原形 freeze）。
