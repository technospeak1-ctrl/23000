## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"货币银行"，词几乎全是银行业务术语，最易写成业务清单。我用"分行经理露丝在一周内处理开户、放贷、识破空头支票、办理外汇、最后迎来合并"作主线，按"开户 → 放贷 → 退票风波 → 货币划转 → 合并收尾"五幕推进，让每个词落在柜面操作与金融决策的具体动作上。

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A depositor walks in 储户上门 | 现金/账户/存款词 | 建立人物与日常业务 |
| 2 | The loan application 贷款申请 | 信贷/抵押/分期词 | 引入借贷决策 |
| 3 | The bounced cheque 退票风波 | 支票/汇票/透支词 | 制造冲突与风险 |
| 4 | Money crosses borders 资金跨境 | 外汇/汇率/转账词 | 扩展业务场景 |
| 5 | A quieter ending 平静收尾 | 合并/储备/提款词 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`cash / banknote / dime / copper / dollar` 集中在"开户"一幕的柜面点钞；`bank account / deposit account / passbook / PIN / bank book` 构成开户整套动作；`credit rating / credit limit / credit control / mortgage / installment / amortize` 集中在"放贷"决策；`cheque / draft / kiting check / overdraft / freeze / cross off` 集中在"退票风波"形成反面冲突；`exchange rate / convertible currency / soft currency / convertibility / bank transfer` 落在"跨境划转"；`merge / accrual / withdrawal` 在结尾收束。

## 二、英文故事　A Week at the Harbor Bank

### 1. A depositor walks in

On a wet Monday, Ruth Kerner opened the doors of the Harbor Bank branch. Her first customer was Mrs. Haddad, a careful **depositor** who carried her **passbook** and a **bank book** in the same bag. "I want a **bank account**," she said, "and a **deposit account** for my money." Ruth counted the **cash** on the counter — a **banknote** here, a silver **dime** and a **copper** cent there — and punched the figures into the **cash register**. She asked Mrs. Haddad to key in her **PIN**. The **deposit** was small, but every **dollar** mattered, and Ruth knew the branch's **cash ratio** had to stay healthy. Before the hour was out, three neighbors had come to **deposit** their **saving**s. Ruth's **banking** routine, and her habit of **saving**, had begun.

### 2. The loan application

By Tuesday, Mr. Alvarez, a shopkeeper, came to **borrow** for a new warehouse. As the **lender**, Ruth studied his file. The bank's **credit control** office had checked his **credit rating**, and the **assessment** was fair, so she offered a **credit account** with a modest **credit limit** and a **credit card** for purchases. The **loan** would be secured by a **mortgage** on the warehouse, and the **ceiling** on it was set at eighty thousand **dollar**s. Repayment would come in monthly **installment**s, and Ruth explained how the debt would **amortize** over ten years. She gave a firm **warranty** that the terms were fixed, and warned that if he fell into **arrears**, the bank would act. Mr. Alvarez signed at once.

### 3. The bounced cheque

On Wednesday a stranger presented a **cheque** and a **draft** at the counter. Ruth held the paper up to the light: the **acceptance draft** bore the mark of an **accepting house**, and the **advance bill** looked genuine, but the numbers on the **bankable bill** did not match. The man wanted **cash** **at par**, and even offered a **bank discount** to hurry things along. Ruth suspected a **kiting check** scheme. She refused, **crossed off** the entry in her ledger, and noted that the account was already an **overdraft**. When the man tried to **overdraw** again, she asked security to **freeze** the account. Her **bank statement** that evening showed no loss.

### 4. Money crosses borders

By Thursday the currency desk was busy. A trader wanted to **exchange** a large sum, and Ruth quoted the **exchange rate**. He held **soft currency** from a weak economy and asked for **convertible currency** instead; the bank's **convertibility** rules allowed it. Ruth arranged a **bank transfer** to an **aid account** abroad. Some clients kept **idle money** in low-interest accounts while others moved **active money** quickly, and the difference would **offset** the week's risk. Each signed **instrument** was stamped, the branch released an **interim dividend**, and its **reserves** grew. Ruth watched the **exchange** of notes with care.

### 5. A quieter ending

On Friday the head office announced, under the **auspices** of the regional board, that Harbor Bank would **merge** with a smaller rival. Ruth oversaw the **allotment** of new desks and the **accrual** of interest on old accounts. She reminded Mrs. Haddad that a **withdrawal** required a **withdrawal receipt**, and that she could **withdraw deposit** at any counter. A builder was asked to **lay** his account with the bank before work began. Ruth herself had learned the value of **saving**: she would **lay aside** part of her salary and **save on** unnecessary expenses. When the **bank holiday** came, the branch was finally quiet.

## 四、学习建议

1. **开户链**：`depositor`（储户）→ `passbook / bank book`（存折）→ `PIN`（密码）→ `deposit account / bank account`（账户）→ `deposit`（存款）→ `cash / banknote / dime / copper / dollar`（现金与硬币），沿"谁来存、用什么凭证、存什么钱"一条线记。
2. **信贷词族**：`borrow`（借入）/`lender`（贷方）成对；`credit rating → credit limit → credit control → credit account → credit card` 是信用评估到授信的完整流程；`mortgage / installment / amortize / arrears / ceiling` 围绕贷款偿还。
3. **风险反面**：`cheque / draft / acceptance draft / accepting house / bankable bill` 是正常票据，`kiting check / overdraft / overdraw / freeze / cross off` 是退票与透支风险，两相对照记。
4. **外汇场景**：`exchange rate / convertible currency / soft currency / convertibility / bank transfer / aid account / offset` 串起"跨境兑换—转账—对冲"的操作。
5. **收尾动作**：`merge / allotment / accrual / withdrawal / withdraw deposit / withdrawal receipt` 用于合并与结账；`lay aside / save on` 两个 `lay / save` 动词搭配专记"储蓄与节省"。
