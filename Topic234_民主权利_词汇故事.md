## 一、编排逻辑

本主题围绕"民主权利"，政治与制度名词高度密集，极易写成政体词条表。我用"一位独立记者旁听首都制宪议会、记录一场关于公民权与福利改革的辩论"作为主线，按"抵达首都 → 议会开幕 → 权利之争 → 改革承诺 → 表决与余波"五幕推进，把抽象政治概念落到记者所见、人物所言与具体动作上：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A city on the alert 戒备的城市 | 联邦/市政/军事形容词 + 机构名词 | 建立场景 |
| 2 | The hall of delegates 代表大厅 | 政体/权威/党派名词与形容词 | 铺陈人物 |
| 3 | The battle over civil rights 权利之争 | 选举/自由/专制词族 | 制造冲突 |
| 4 | The price of reform 改革的代价 | 福利/经济/公平词族 | 提出方案 |
| 5 | The vote and its aftermath 表决与余波 | 立法/治理/动员动词 | 点题收束 |

**设计要点**：`federal / national / municipal / martial` 按"联邦—国家—市政—军事"层级分布，配合 `national anthem / military affairs / municipal affairs` 等搭配落在第一幕的城市场景；`democracy / democratic / Democratic Party / democratic reform / suffragist / ballot / poll / enfranchise` 组成"民主与选举"链，集中在权利一幕；`authority / authoritative / authoritarian / prerogative / veto / dominance / puissance / hierarchy` 一组"权力"词通过老卫士与新改革者的对立人格化；`welfare / well-being / pension / dole / gratuity / compensation / subsidize` 落在福利承诺一幕；`legislate / administer / abolish / authorize / enforce / eradicate / evacuate / inaugurate / lead to` 等动词收束于表决与治理，正反结果同框，避免清单式罗列。

## 二、英文故事　The Assembly at Greyhaven

### 1. A city on the alert

At dawn the **federal** capital of the **federation** was already **alert**. Banners for the **national anthem** hung over the **municipal** square, and soldiers **put on the alert** at the approaches to the **Legislative Assembly** kept watch over **military affairs** while clerks sorted the **municipal affairs** of the district. Lena, an **independent** reporter, had come to cover the great debate; her **mission** was to record exactly what the crowd wanted. She heard the **martial** beat of a drum and watched a **multitude** of citizens gather, their faces a **motif** of hope and doubt. She felt the **caution** of a city that had known too many promises. The new **bureau** issued leaflets, but the **bureaucracy** moved slowly, and she noted with **indifference** how the clerks shrugged. An old **custodian** of the hall told her that **independence** had been won, though the meaning of that word was still being fought over.

### 2. The hall of delegates

The **congress** chamber was packed. The **chancellor**, an **authoritative** man who liked to **quote authoritative works**, called the session to order, and his **authority** carried weight. Around him sat every **delegate** of the **Democratic Party**, the **alignment** of smaller regional groups, and the leaders of an old **alliance** that still dreamed of **empire**. A **monarchy** had once ruled this land under an **emperor**, and though the **empire** was gone, the **hierarchy** of old families kept its **dominance**. Lena listened as a **lobby** of merchants argued that **democracy** had made the country weak in matters of trade and defence. A young reformer defended **democratic reform** as the only path forward. The **puissance** of the old guard was **misleading**, she thought; it rested on habit, not on consent. Voices rose in **conflict**, and for a moment the **agreement** that had brought them together seemed ready to break. Yet even the **misleading** slogans could not hide the fact that the people outside demanded change, and the **approval** of the people was the one thing no chamber could ignore.

### 3. The battle over civil rights

By midday the argument turned to **civil rights**. A **suffragist** named Mrs. Vale rose to demand the **ballot** for women, and a **poll** taken that morning showed the town split down the middle. "You cannot **enfranchise** half the people and call the other half free," she said, her voice steady. The old guard answered with a **veto** and an **arbitrary decision** that owed nothing to reason; they even twisted the **constitution** to defend a **prerogative** no election had given them. When a minister tried to **suppress** the leaflets, the **suppression** only drove more citizens into the street. An **authoritarian** in the gallery shouted that **autonomy** for the regions would break the union, while a woman beside him asked why **civil** obedience should be demanded of people denied a vote. Lena wrote it all down: the **conflict** was no longer about taxes but about who counted as a citizen.

### 4. The price of reform

In the afternoon the speakers turned to **welfare** as the real test of **democracy**. A **benevolent** doctor proposed a **pension** for widows, a **dole** for the unemployed, a **gratuity** for returning soldiers, and **compensation** for families whose land had been seized. The state would **subsidize** cheap housing, and the scheme, though costly, was a **viable proposition** grounded in real numbers. Critics pointed to the **riches** of a few and the growing **inequality** of the many, but the doctor answered with an **even-handed** plan that asked the wealthy to pay an **even-handed** share. "This is **consonant** with our oldest promises," she said, and she urged the chamber to **be consonant with** justice rather than with fear. Nothing, she added, was **permissible** that threatened the **well-being** of children; the terms were **negotiable**, the goal was not. Lena saw the **avail** of such words in the faces of the crowd: for the first time that day, hope seemed **viable**.

### 5. The vote and its aftermath

At dusk the assembly began to **legislate**. One bill would **abolish** the old tax, another would **administer laws** fairly and **administer a country** that served its people. The **chancellor** said the treasury could **authorize** the spending and asked every **civilian** to **comply with the law**, while the new **bureau** would **enforce** the rules without fear. Reformers promised to **eradicate** bribery from the **bureau** and to **empower** the poor; they **enabled** every citizen to vote and gave the regions a reason to **ally** with the capital. The **chancellor** still liked to **advocate thriftiness** in public. A few men still **slaved** on the old estates, and when they began to **riot**, guards moved in to **evacuate** the square and to **capture** the ringleaders. An **escort** of soldiers held the gate while a new hall was **inaugurated** on the site of the old one. The **divesture** of crown lands and the **consistency** of the new statutes, the chancellor said, would **lead to** a fairer peace, and he urged the people not to **abandon** themselves to anger. As the **national anthem** was sung once more, Lena closed her notebook. The **agreement** was fragile, the **approval** conditional, but the **civil rights** won that day would be defended.

## 四、学习建议

1. **层级形容词**：`federal`（联邦）→ `national`（国家）→ `municipal`（市政）→ `martial`（军事），按治理范围由大到小记，配合 `national anthem`、`military affairs`、`municipal affairs` 三个搭配一起记。
2. **民主与选举链**：`democracy` → `democratic` → `Democratic Party` → `democratic reform` → `suffragist` → `ballot` → `poll` → `enfranchise`，是公民权新闻的核心主线。
3. **权力词族**：`authority` / `authoritative` / `authoritarian` / `prerogative` / `veto` / `dominance` / `puissance` / `hierarchy` 通过"权威—专断—特权—否决"的语义阶梯辨析，配合正反人物对照记忆。
4. **福利词组**：`welfare`、`well-being`、`pension`、`dole`、`gratuity`、`compensation`、`subsidize`、`benevolent` 集中在社会政策场景，配合 `viable proposition`、`even-handed`、`negotiable` 学习。
5. **治理动词**：`legislate`（立法）→ `administer`（治理）→ `authorize`（批准）→ `enforce`（执行）→ `eradicate`（根除）→ `inaugurate`（落成）→ `lead to`（导致），按政策落地顺序记，配合 `abolish`、`evacuate`、`riot` 等结果词形成完整因果链。
