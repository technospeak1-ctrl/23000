## 一、编排逻辑

本主题围绕"人与人相处"，可归为 4 个语义场：人的性格气质、社交身份与场合、交往动作、社交礼仪。我用一场社区欢迎晚宴作主线，按"筹备 → 宾客 → 现场 → 尾声"推进，每个环节承接一个语义场：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The invitation 请柬 | 社交礼仪、身份名词 | 建立情境（办一场晚宴） |
| 2 | The guests 宾客 | 性格气质形容词 | 刻画人物（形形色色的人） |
| 3 | The conversation 交谈 | 交往动词 + 关系名词 | 冲突与互动（相处之道） |
| 4 | What transpired 后来 | 礼仪动词 + 剩余搭配 | 收尾升华（关系的真谛） |

**设计要点**：把正面（cooperative、punctual、worthwhile）与负面（cranky、detestable、snob）性格词放进同一场晚宴，形成人物对照；`irrespective of`、`surround yourself with`、`attribute…to` 等搭配承担情节转折，而非孤立罗列。

## 二、英文故事　The Welcome Feast

### 1. The invitation

The **community** had decided to hold a **celebration** for its new members — a **feast** meant to turn mere **acquaintances** into friends. Mara, who was **punctual** by habit, wrote the invitations herself. On each card she put the **addressee**'s name, a formal **salutation**, and the **letterhead** of the association. She checked the **directory** twice, because nothing is more **offensive** than a misspelled name, and she wanted a **definite answer** from everyone before the day. "A good **relationship** begins with respect," she said, "**irrespective of** your **status** or **stature**." She even phoned a local **celebrity** to ask him to come; his presence, she thought, would add **worth** to the evening. No **exaggeration**, no **boast** — just an honest **invitation**.

### 2. The guests

They came in all temperaments. Mr. Pell was **chatty** and **enthusiastic**, shaking every hand; beside him stood his wife, **prim** and slightly **stilted**, who disliked **frivolous behavior**. A **so-called** expert arrived late and spoke in such a **circuitous** way that nobody could follow him — his **convolution** of words hid a simple point, and he was so **pernickety** about detail that he made a **complaint** about the seating. A young man with a **stammering** delivery turned **timid** when everyone looked at him. The **snob** in the corner was **inhospitable** to anyone he judged **lowly**, and his **mawkish** compliments fooled no one; his charm **belied** a **detestable** vanity. An old **cranky** uncle sat in silence, watching every **activity** with suspicion. Others were **cooperative**, **direct**, and genuinely **worthwhile** company, and their easy **hospitality** put everyone at ease; even the uncle's **reputation** for ill temper proved exaggerated. By common **association** these opposites had gathered under one roof, and the **occasion** promised **remarkable** things.

### 3. The conversation

At dinner the talk soon turned sharp. The expert began to **deride** the snob, and the snob was **offended**; the two **clashed with** each other **over** a trivial point, and the **conflict** drew every eye. Mara tried to **allay** the tension and **coordinate** the conversation, hoping to **compromise** rather than let a **dispute** grow. She **apologized for** raising a difficult subject and thanked those who **cooperated with** her — such **cooperation** was the true **glue** of the evening. A quiet guest **attributed** the whole misunderstanding **to** too much **alcohol**, and everyone laughed; the laughter **allayed** the last of the **trouble**. The **participants** began to **converge on** a single topic — what makes a gathering work. One said it was **coordination** and **cooperative effort**; another insisted on **equality** and **thoroughness**. Even the **minion** of the celebrity was allowed to speak. A **comment** here, a **postcard**-sized note there, and the **convolution** of earlier argument gave way to ease.

### 4. What transpired

As the evening closed, it **transpired** that the greatest **tribute** was paid not to the famous guest but to Mara. People rose to give her **enthusiastic applause**; someone raised a glass and **toasted** her with **lavish** praise, and the room **ravished** her with kindness. To **commemorate** the night, a guest **amused** the company with a story, and soon people were **amusing themselves** with tales of past parties. Before leaving, they made a point to **call on** her again, to **socialize with** neighbours they had **mistrust of** only hours before, and to **back up** her plan for a second feast. They had **surrounded themselves with** people quite unlike themselves — and found it **worthy**. The famous guest had **invited** her to **contact** him for a larger gathering; on his card he wrote, "**A notable person** is made by notable manners, and **a piece of timely advice** is worth more than **lavish entertainment**." Mara kept that **piece of timely advice** on her desk, a small **reminder** that **awe** and **antipathy** both fade, but a **definite** kindness lasts.

## 四、学习建议

1. 抓人物对照：把正面性格（cooperative、punctual、direct、worthwhile）与负面性格（cranky、snob、mawkish、detestable）放在同一场晚宴里记，反差最强。
2. 按场景回忆四类词：请柬（礼仪）→ 宾客（性格）→ 交谈（动词）→ 尾声（收尾）。
3. 搭配优先：14 条常用搭配中，clash with…over、attribute…to、apologize for、surround yourself with、converge on 是高频考点。
4. 语域意识：mawkish、convolution、tribut e偏书面；chatty、snob、dash off 偏口语。
