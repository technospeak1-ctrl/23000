## 一、编排逻辑

本主题围绕"会议与谈判"展开，会议名词与立场形容词密集。我用"一位谈判专家受邀主持双边贸易会议，从筹备、交锋、突发混乱到投票结盟、落幕"的因果线作主线，按"受邀筹备 → 谈判交锋 → 混乱与阻挠 → 投票结盟 → 落幕回望"五幕推进，把每个词落到具体动作上：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | An invitation 受邀筹备 | 立场形容词 + 会议名词 + 礼仪搭配 | 建立人物与规则 |
| 2 | The negotiation 谈判交锋 | 辩论动词 + 交易名词 + 立场搭配 | 展开冲突 |
| 3 | The disruption 混乱阻挠 | 失误名词 + 阻挠动词 + 负面搭配 | 情节高潮 |
| 4 | A new alliance 投票结盟 | 组织名词 + 正面动词 + 合作搭配 | 转折推进 |
| 5 | The close 落幕回望 | 时间副词 + 收束动词 + 收尾搭配 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`formal / diplomatic / bilateral / impartial / ineligible` 等立场形容词分别贴到会议文体、外交关系、双边贸易、公正建议与投票资格上；`conference / convention / panel / auditorium / chair / presenter` 等会议名词按会场动线依次出现；`misplace / misread / misrepresent / procrastinate / prolong / defeat` 串成"失误—阻挠—挫败"的因果链，与 `organize / persuade / earn / cooperate` 的处置动作对照；10 条常用搭配如 `a formal style / at present / the preceding night / opposite direction` 自然嵌入叙事。

## 二、英文故事　A Seat at the Table

### 1. An invitation

Helen Reid was a **negotiator** asked to **chair** a **panel** at a two-day **conference** in the city hall **auditorium**. The **municipal** council would **convene** at nine to promote **bilateral trade** and repair **diplomatic relations** between two partners. "We need a **formal** tone," the **presenter** warned, handing her a **collection** of papers written in a **formal style**. A former pilot who had left the **cockpit** to join the **panel** sat beside her. Helen agreed the occasion was **decisive of** the whole season's talks. **At present** the mood was hopeful, and an **encouraging** letter from the **patron** had arrived in a plain **envelope**. **The preceding night** she had slept badly, yet she read the brief **closely** and **determined to** do her best. She knew every member **present** would be watching.

### 2. The negotiation

The **convention** opened with a **discussion** of a **proposition** from a **proponent** of cheaper steel. A young **investor** argued that the figures **amounted** to a hidden subsidy, and a **proxy** for the absent buyer read a long statement. **Concerning** the disputed clause, Helen asked for **impartial advice** from the **panel**. The draft would **incorporate** a new clause, and they met again to **discuss** the **deduction** that worried the investor. "Does 'subsidy' **denote** a gift or a loan?" she asked, and the **debate** grew heated. She **emphasized** that both sides must speak plainly and tried to **persuade** them to show their books. One man tried to **detract from** the issue with jokes; another began to **procrastinate**, hoping to **prolong** the session. Helen was **doubtful about** their promises but would not **erase** a line from the record. She moved to **grasp an opportunity** to speak and to **organize** the facts before the vote.

### 3. The disruption

Trouble came at noon. A clerk had **misplaced** the file and **misread** a key figure, and a rival had **misrepresented** the whole **proposition** in the press. A false **notification** of a **closedown** at the warehouse caused a brief **disruption**; the **duration** of the scare was only an hour, but the **omission** of a signature and a **duplication** of invoices made the audit painful. An **ineligible** proxy tried to speak, and Helen ruled him **ineligible for** the vote. A **proponent** of delay tried to **defeat** the motion, but the members would not **permit** it and voted him down. This set a **precedent** for the rest of the talks. Helen refused to **misrepresent** the facts or to **alter** the truth. "The deal must move in the **opposite direction** from the old one," she said, and the **discussion** grew **closely** focused on details.

### 4. A new alliance

By afternoon the mood changed. A new **alliance** of small firms, a **subsidiary** of the main supplier, and an **investor** funding a **consignment** of parts sent representatives. The **election** of a new **chair** gave Helen room to act; an **offset** in the budget covered the loss that had worried the investor. Because the **preceding** talks had failed, both sides now wanted a **bilateral** deal. "We need a **collaborative** effort," Helen said, and the **collaboration** that followed surprised everyone. She **earned** trust by giving **impartial advice** and refused to **detract from** the record. The rival began to **persuade** his board to sign, and an **acceptable** compromise took shape. They agreed to **alter** one clause and let the deal **amount to** a fair exchange.

### 5. The close

**Following** the session, Helen walked **closely** beside the **presenter** to the **auditorium** door. The **conference** had been **decisive of** the deal; **the preceding night** she had feared a **closedown**, but now a **substantial**, **formal** agreement lay among the papers in her **envelope**. She had learned to **grasp an opportunity**, to **determine to** finish what she started, and to **organize** facts before a **discussion** begins. A **doubtful** start had become an **encouraging** end. **At present** she felt only relief that the **municipal** hall was quiet again, and the **following** morning the **notification** of the signed deal was sent in the **opposite direction** from the earlier rumor.

## 四、学习建议

1. **会议链条**：`convene`（开会）→ `conference / convention`（会议/大会）→ `chair / panel / presenter`（主席/小组/介绍人）→ `auditorium`（礼堂），按会场动线记忆。
2. **立场形容词**：`formal / diplomatic / bilateral`（正式的/外交的/双边的）是正面措辞，`doubtful / ineligible / opposite`（可疑的/无资格的/相反的）是负面或对立措辞，放在同一谈判桌两侧对照。
3. **失误与阻挠动词链**：`misplace → misread → misrepresent → procrastinate → prolong → defeat`（放错→读错→误传→拖延→延长→挫败）因果相连，配 `omission / duplication / disruption / closedown` 等混乱名词。
4. **正面处置词**：`organize / persuade / earn / collaborate / acceptable` 与上面的负面链形成对照，体现"化解混乱"的过程。
5. **高频搭配**：`at present`（目前）、`a formal style`（正式文体）、`impartial advice`（公正建议）、`the preceding night`（前一晚）、`opposite direction`（相反方向）在写作与口语中都常用。
