## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"贸易 1"，词项覆盖全球市场、金融风险、货运海关与谈判，最容易写成"外贸术语表"。我用"一位贸易员把本国厨具出口到三国、从调研到签约再到复盘的一笔生意"作主线，按"全球机会 → 资金与风险 → 货运与海关 → 谈判 → 成交之后"五幕推进，让每个词和搭配都落在具体的贸易动作上：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A global opportunity 全球机会 | 全球/市场形容词 + 调研搭配 | 建立人物与目标 |
| 2 | Money and risk 资金与风险 | 金融/财务词 + 资金搭配 | 展现财务决策 |
| 3 | Cargo and customs 货运与海关 | 运输/关税词 + 海关搭配 | 铺开物流 |
| 4 | The negotiation 谈判 | 价格/博弈动词 + 谈判搭配 | 情节高潮 |
| 5 | After the deal 成交之后 | 恢复/转变动词 + 收束搭配 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`global / untapped / marketable / affordable` 等形容词贴到市场机会（全球村、未开发潜力、适销对路）上；`monetary / solvent / speculative / redundant` 贴到资金风险（货币政策、偿付能力、投机、冗员）上；15 条搭配如 `global village / untapped market potential / consistent policy / customs office / favorable balance of trade / synergic relationship` 全部嵌入生意流程，成为"他做了什么"的一部分，而非孤立罗列；`dicker / curb / constrain / dissuade / inflict` 等动词落在谈判的拉锯里。

## 二、英文故事　A Deal Across the Gulf

### 1. A global opportunity

Daniel Reed worked for a trading **firm** that believed the world had become a **global village**. His **motivation** was plain: buy goods that were **affordable** at home and sell them where they were **marketable** abroad. The **scope** of the plan was **global**, and the demand **index** in three countries pointed to an **untapped market potential** for kitchenware. "We must **investigate the market** before we **invest** a single coin," he told the board, ordering **intensive** field studies. Analysts measured the **percentage** of families likely to buy, the **amount** each would spend, and the **peak** month for orders. A **scarcity** of local suppliers, they found, could **generate** real **profit**. So began the firm's **transition** from a local shop into an **export** house.

### 2. Money and risk

The next hurdle was money. A cautious **banker** explained that **monetary policy** had pushed the lending **rate** up, so a **short-term** loan would be costly. Daniel **calculated** the balance between **revenue** and cost on a sheet of paper. "Only a **consistent policy** keeps us **solvent**," he said. The board wanted **a substantial salary** for the team and dreamed of **a highly profitable business**, but Daniel warned that **speculative** bets and **redundant** staff could sink them. He asked the bank to **allocate** more money to the project and **assured** the lenders of their ability to pay. When one buyer threatened **default**, Daniel moved fast to **cancel the debt** rather than let a small loss damage the young firm. A **benefit** of his caution was a **consistent**, **profitable** record.

### 3. Cargo and customs

The first shipment left the **dock** at dawn. A **ferry** carried part of the **cargo** across the **gulf**, while the rest went by **border trade** truck. At the **customs office** the clerk checked the **clearance** papers and explained the new **tariff**. "A **barrier** to trade is often just a **by-product** of fear," Daniel said, watching a **bilateral monopoly** form between two suppliers. His country had once tried **autarky** — a kind of **autarchy** that shut out the world — and he knew it failed. The **occupational** hazard of a trader, he joked, was reading **favorable balance of trade** figures one month and an **unfavorable balance of trade** the next. He kept both columns open on his desk.

### 4. The negotiation

Across the table, the buyer began to **bargain** and **dicker** over every cent. Daniel **bid for a project** that would double their order, and the two men went **back and forth** for hours. When the buyer tried to **curb** the price and **constrain** the delivery dates, Daniel refused to be **dissuaded**. "**Drastic changes** now would only create **slack** quality," he said, staying **unwavering**. He offered a **synergic relationship** instead of a fight: both sides would gain. A new **anti-dumping** rule, the buyer noted, was **dependent** on **the additional regulation** due next month, so Daniel agreed to **consolidate his position** by signing a long-term frame contract. "We **inflict** losses on no one," he said, "and we ask no favors."

### 5. After the deal

The first payment let Daniel **replenish** the warehouse and **revive** a product line that had gone quiet. Shoppers **plunged into** the sale, and for a while the market was **saturated with** cheap copies; soon prices began to **slide**. Daniel kept his **poise**. He sold the old stock at **auction**, trusted the **efficiency** of his **dock** team and the **resilience** of his **firm**, and **transformed** a shaky start into a stable trade. The **transition** he had planned on that first morning was complete.

## 四、学习建议

1. **市场机会链**：`global village`（地球村）→ `untapped market potential`（未开发潜力）→ `marketable`（适销的）→ `affordable`（买得起的）→ `investigate the market`（市场调研）→ `invest`（投资），是一条完整的出海路径。
2. **金融风险词**：`monetary policy`（货币政策）、`rate`（利率）、`solvent`（有偿付能力的）、`default`（违约）、`speculative`（投机的）、`short-term`（短期的）一起记，是看财务与贷款条款的高频词。
3. **货运海关**：`dock`（码头）、`ferry`（渡船）、`cargo`（货物）、`clearance`（通关）、`tariff`（关税）、`customs office`（海关）、`barrier`（壁垒）、`by-product`（副产品），外贸物流场景常见。
4. **贸易差额对照**：`favorable balance of trade`（顺差）与 `unfavorable balance of trade`（逆差）成对记忆；`bilateral monopoly`（双边垄断）、`border trade`（边境贸易）、`autarky`（自给自足）是贸易体制词。
5. **动词短语**：`bid for a project`（项目投标）、`dicker`（讨价还价）、`curb`（控制）、`constrain`（束缚）、`dissuade`（劝阻）、`inflict on`（强加给）、`consolidate one's position`（巩固地位）都落在谈判拉锯里，配套记忆更牢。
