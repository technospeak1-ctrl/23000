## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"研究探索 1"，词表由三部分交织而成：一是科学方法与思维词（`inductive / inferential / logic / conjecture / generalization / proof`），二是田野发掘与实验分析词（`burial / cuneiform / lens / dialysis / ion / sulphide`），三是项目制度词（`panel / patent / promoter / requirement`）。若按词性罗列必然成为清单。我以"林博士回到出生地，在一间空办公室里带队发掘河岸遗址，用归纳与实验破译一枚楔形文字泥板"为主线，按"空办公室 → 河岸 → 实验台 → 事实成形 → 最后一页"五幕推进，让每个术语都成为一次挖掘、一架仪器或一条推理：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The unused office 空办公室 | 项目制度 + 思维方法 + 起始性形容词 | 建立人物、任务与方法 |
| 2 | The river bank 河岸发掘 | 遗存名词 + 危险/状态形容词 + 探测动词 | 用一次发掘串起现场词 |
| 3 | The laboratory bench 实验台 | 物质/器材名词 + 溶解与分离动词 | 把分析落到具体操作 |
| 4 | A shape in the facts 事实成形 | 论证名词 + 制度词 + 创新动词 | 推向突破与点题 |
| 5 | The last page 最后一页 | 剩余抽象词 + 易混辨析 | 收束、回收搭配与辨析 |

**设计要点**：9 条搭配全部落进动作链——`inductive reasoning / inferential procedure` 是林博士开的第一次方法会，`precise calculations` 与 `calibration` 同框，`substantive law` 由专利律师带出，`unused office` 是全篇起点与终点，`extract...from / go halves / mingle with / radiate from` 分别落在提取盐分、分摊试剂、两液混合与灯光散热四个具体画面。`object`（物体）与 `objective`（目标）同幕对照，`solvent` 兼用"有偿付能力"与"溶剂"两义，`imitation / modification / mercury / worthwhile` 收在结尾的笔记里，避免冷词悬空。

## 二、英文故事　The Tablet from the River Bank

### 1. The unused office

For 20 days the rain had held off, and Dr. Lin was ready. She had grown up in a **natal** village of reed huts and had now returned to it as head of a small **research** team. The university gave her an **unused office** above the library; a wooden **plank** across two crates and a shelf of books made it her **hub**. Her **objective** was plain: to **uncover** what lay under the river bank. A **panel** of senior scholars had set the **requirement** that the work be careful, and a clear **prerequisite** was the trust of the villagers. A small grant kept the team **solvent** while they worked. Lin **figured** that patience, not speed, was the best **means** to an answer. She knew the **finitude** of a single lifetime, so she **conceived** a plan that younger hands could carry on. In her notebook she **premised** the whole study on one small find — a **cuneiform** tablet — and wrote down a **conjecture** in plain words. Her method would be **inductive**: collect facts first, then let an **inferential procedure** draw out the pattern. "This is **inductive reasoning**, not guesswork," she told the team, and her **prompt** smile **energized** them. It was a **creative**, **initiative** first step, **ingeniously** simple.

### 2. The river bank

At the dig the air was **hazardous** with dust, and poor **visibility** slowed them. Under a **shred** of clay Lin found the **burial** layer: an **amorphous** lump of metal, **assorted** beads, and an **iconographic** seal showing a reed and a star. A villager said the old **pictograph** meant "water," and Lin saw at once an **illustration** of the village's ancient name. Beads like these were sold in every village market. Rain would soon **efface** the marks, so she set a **lens** over them and **collated** every line. A **hybrid** device — part camera, part sonar — **detected** a **signal** under the mud. It was a pattern of **ripples**, like **echolocation** in a bat, and the team **decoupled** the sensor from its frame to **activate** the scan. A small pump gave a gentle **propulsion** as it drew clear water from the well, and Lin **modulated** the sound until the echo came back clean. That night, in the lantern light, beads of **condensation** ran down the cold glass. The find was **tentative**, she wrote, but real.

### 3. The laboratory bench

Back in the office Lin **modernized** the bench. In a dish lay an **insoluble** grey **substance** from the lump; a drop of **solvent** did nothing, so she began to **dilute** a stronger acid. "We must **extract** the salt **from** this **sulphide**," she said, "then **filter** it through a **sieve**." The **ions** would pass and the rest would stay. She used **dialysis** through a thin skin to separate them, and a faint glow began to **radiate from** the warm dish. She kept the heat low; a rise of even one degree was a **drawback** that could ruin the sample. Tom **halved** the powder into two dishes; Lin paid half and he paid half, so they **went halves** on the reagents. The two liquids **mingled with** each other at the edge, and after an hour the result was **verified**. "Those are **precise calculations**," she said, checking the **calibration** of the scale. **Supposedly** easy, the test had taken all day, and the **tension** in the room eased only when white crystals appeared. "This is **substantive**," she said. "It is **proof**, not a **conjecture**."

### 4. A shape in the facts

By the third week the pieces began to fit. Lin **downloaded** the field notes; to **innovate** the sorting, she taught the computer to read old marks. A **generalization** was taking shape: every seal pointed to a river festival. It was a **breakthrough**, and she saw in it the **forerunner** of a wider history of **metallurgy** in the valley. A lawyer came to speak about the **substantive law** of **patents**, and a **promoter** offered to fund a small display. Lin **enciphered** the raw coordinates so the site would stay safe. "We **seek** the truth, not fame," she said. She would **solve** the last puzzle — the star on the seal — by comparing it with **assorted** old signs. In the evening she **mingled with** the villagers again and listened to their stories; their words gave the numbers a **significance** she had missed. **Subdivision** of the site into small squares made the work exact, and a **mode** of drawing each find by hand slowed the **pace** but saved every **object** for study.

### 5. The last page

On the last evening Lin wrote the **logic** of the whole case in her notebook. The tablet was not an **imitation**; the **input** from the sonar, the **lens** and the scale all agreed. A **modification** to the first plan — using a **mercury** thermometer instead of the old one — had made the readings steady. The work was **worthwhile**, she decided, not because of the **patent** but because a village could now read its own past. The **objective** she had set in the **unused office** was met, and the **object** on the table was no longer a lump but a story. She closed the book while the last light ceased to **radiate from** the window.

## 四、学习建议

1. **方法词一条线**：`premise → conjecture → inductive reasoning → inferential procedure → collate → generalization → proof → breakthrough` 正是林博士的研究流程，按"假定—推测—归纳—推断—核对—概括—证据—突破"走一遍，比单背释义牢得多。
2. **实验动作链**：`extract...from → dilute → filter → sieve → dialysis → radiate from → verify`，配 `insoluble / solvent / ions / substance / sulphide` 一起记；`calibration` 与 `precise calculations` 同框，强调"精确"靠校准而来。
3. **易混同框记**：`object`（物体）与 `objective`（目标）在最后一幕同时出现；`substance`（物质）与 `substantive`（真实的）；`solvent` 兼"有偿付能力"与"溶剂"两义；`imitation`（仿制品）与 `modification`（修改）词形相近、意思相反方向。
4. **制度词成组**：`panel / requirement / prerequisite / patent / promoter / substantive law` 是项目得以进行的外部条件，放在"空办公室"与"律师来访"两幕里，比孤立记忆更自然。
5. **九条搭配按幕归堆**：准备（`unused office / inductive reasoning / inferential procedure`）→ 发掘（`go halves` 之外的现场动作）→ 实验（`extract...from / mingle with / radiate from / precise calculations`）→ 收束（`substantive law`）， recall 时按画面取词。
