## 一、编排逻辑

本主题以"海外扩张与贸易争端"为核心，贸易、零售、货运词与立场形容词交错。我用"一家连锁企业女发言人推动海外投资、在研讨会上递交文件，却遭遇出口禁运与码头怠工，经重新评估后扭亏为盈"的真实因果线作主线，按"海外蓝图 → 研讨会交涉 → 争端爆发 → 重新评估 → 复苏回望"五幕推进：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A plan across the sea 海外蓝图 | 立场形容词 + 扩张名词 + 投资搭配 | 建立目标 |
| 2 | At the symposium 研讨会交涉 | 会议名词 + 交涉动词 + 贸易搭配 | 铺陈规则 |
| 3 | The trade dispute 争端爆发 | 争端名词 + 受阻动词 + 危机搭配 | 情节高潮 |
| 4 | Reappraisal 重新评估 | 评估动词 + 处置搭配 | 转折 |
| 5 | Recovery 复苏回望 | 正面动词 + 收束搭配 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`complementary / comprehensible / receptive / recognizable` 等正面形容词与 `frustrating / steep / tough / overdue` 等负面词分置于扩张顺利与受挫两幕，形成对照；`embargo → go-slow → trade dispute → disequilibrium → regression` 串成因果递进的危机链；`damp down / hinder from / reconcile to / be required to` 等搭配落在处置动作里；14 条常用搭配如 `overseas investment / going price / trade terms / World Trade Organization` 全部嵌入业务场景。

## 二、英文故事　A Plan Across the Sea

### 1. A plan across the sea

The **group** had its **headquarters** in a glass building, and its **retail** chain was known for a **prime location** in every town. A **spokesman** named Clara Vance stood before the **shareholder** meeting with a plan for **overseas investment**. "The old **going price** will not hold," she said, "and our **traditional** suppliers cannot **conserve** the margins we once had." She spoke in a **comprehensible** way, asked for a **definite appropriation** that was **substantial** for the first stage, and requested an **exclusive interview** with the trade press. The plan was **complementary** to an existing line, not a rival to it. A **recipient** of the first report, an investor from **overseas**, thought the figures **definite** enough to trust. Clara felt the whole board was **receptive** to the idea, though she refused to speak **vaguely** about costs and warned that **steep** freight would make the first year **frustrating**.

### 2. At the symposium

A **symposium** was held to settle **trade terms** under the **World Trade Organization** rules. Clara brought a **stenographer** to record every **term**, prepared a **submission** for the panel, and checked the **remote control** of the warehouse doors. A **viewpoint** from a smaller **group** asked for a **concession** on **heavy traffic** surcharges. "No rule should **hinder** a small firm from trading," Clara argued. The panel's **rating** of the plan was high, and a **spokesman** for the **World Trade Organization** said the **stipulation** on **hazardous waste** must be met. Clara **circulated** her **submission** and **translated** the key clause into two languages. She **supposed** the deal would soon be signed. Goods in **transit** moved slowly, but a written **warranty** covered them. The **break** in the talks came when a **disagreement** about **mercantilism** surfaced and the panel took a **vote**.

### 3. The trade dispute

Then a **trade dispute** erupted. A foreign port declared an **embargo**, and a **go-slow** at the docks left containers in **transit** for weeks. Workers stopped to **load** the ships. An **overdue check** from a **recipient** of the goods bounced, and **heavy traffic** on the ring road made delivery worse. The **disequilibrium** between supply and demand produced a **regression** in sales; the firm's **rating** fell and the **viewpoint** of the press turned sour, as if a **great depression** were returning. Clara tried to **damp down** the panic, but a **frustrating** call from **headquarters** made her lose patience. "We must **conserve** what we have," she said, and she **ceased** all new orders. The dock union refused to **reconcile to** the plan, and a clause in the **warranty** seemed to **hinder** the claim. For a moment Clara thought the firm would **lose** money on a grand scale.

### 4. Reappraisal

Clara had to **reappraise** the whole plan. She would **be required to** report within days, so she gathered the **stenographer** and went through the **trade terms** clause by clause. The union agreed to **damp down** its demands, and the dock workers would **be required to** return. In a heated session the members voted to **decertify** the union, and Clara managed to **reconcile to** a new schedule with both sides. Goods began to **circulate** again, prices did not **gravitate** downward this time, and the firm began to **gain** ground. A **concession** on freight broke the deadlock, and the **disequilibrium** eased.

### 5. Recovery

By autumn the **embargo** was lifted and the **go-slow** ended. Sales **soared**, and the **profitable** line became **recognizable** on every high street. The **stipulation** on **hazardous waste** had been met, the **warranty** claims were settled, and a vote of confidence served to **uphold** the board. Clara's **submission** was **translated** into three languages and **circulated** to every **shareholder**. The **viewpoint** she had **supposed** would fail now seemed **definite**: a **tough job** done well. From a **great depression** in spring to a **profitable** autumn, the **group** had learned to **conserve** cash, to **damp down** panic, and to **thrive** on hard work.

## 四、学习建议

1. **危机因果链**：`embargo`（禁运）→ `go-slow`（怠工）→ `trade dispute`（贸易争端）→ `disequilibrium`（不均衡）→ `regression`（衰退）→ `great depression`（大萧条），一条从起因到恶化的链条。
2. **贸易搭配**：`trade terms`（贸易术语）、`trade dispute`（贸易争端）、`World Trade Organization`（世界贸易组织）、`overseas investment`（海外投资）、`going price`（现行价格）是外贸高频词。
3. **正邪形容词对照**：`complementary / comprehensible / receptive / recognizable / profitable` 是正面，`frustrating / steep / tough / overdue / vaguely` 是负面，分置扩张顺境与逆境两幕记忆。
4. **处置动词链**：`damp down`（抑制）→ `reconcile to`（使顺从）→ `be required to`（被要求）→ `reappraise`（重估）→ `uphold`（支持），配 `hinder from / decertify / circulate` 理解恢复过程。
5. **物流与文件词**：`transit / freight / warranty / submission / stipulation / stenographer` 同属货运与单证场景，`load / cease / conserve / gain / soar / thrive` 是状态变化的动词。
