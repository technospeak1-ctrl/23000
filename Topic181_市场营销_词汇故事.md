## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"市场营销"，词项以品牌、消费者、分销与广告搭配为主，最容易写成"营销术语表"。我用"一位营销经理为新款钢制水壶策划上市战役"作主线，按"策略brief → 打造品牌 → 渠道铺货 → 上市促销 → 结算复盘"五幕推进，让每个词和搭配都落在具体的营销动作上：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A strategic brief 策略简报 | 策略形容词 + 市场研究/细分搭配 | 建立人物与目标 |
| 2 | Building the brand 打造品牌 | 品牌/商标/文案词 + 广告搭配 | 塑造产品 |
| 3 | Channels and delivery 渠道与配送 | 分销/零售/服务词 + 配送搭配 | 铺开销售 |
| 4 | Launch day 上市当天 | 促销/赞助/价格词 + 促销搭配 | 情节高潮 |
| 5 | The reckoning 结算复盘 | 剩余动词 + 财务收束搭配 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`viable / strategic / regional / dominant / cut-price / cut-rate / prepaid` 等形容词分别贴到提案（可行性建议、战略投资、地区合作）与价格（低价试用、削价竞争、预付订购）上；27 条搭配如 `concentrated marketing / market segmentation / brand loyalty / distribution channel / door-to-door service / market penetration` 全部嵌入上市流程，成为"她做了什么"的一部分；`divest / reallocate / remainder / subscribe` 等动词落在预算调整与库存处理的决策里，形成因果推进。

## 二、英文故事　The Bottle That Found Its Niche

### 1. A strategic brief

Mara Chen was a marketing manager who believed a product needed a **viable proposition** before a single **brochure** was printed. Her firm made **consumer goods**, and the board asked her to launch a new line of **consumer durables** — steel bottles. "We cannot win with **undifferentiated marketing**," she told her team, spreading out the **research** on the table. "Nor can we fight **blind competition** with the **dominant** giants." She proposed **concentrated marketing** instead: pick one **segment**, study **consumer behavior**, and find a **niche** the big brands ignored. On her **market segmentation** map she drew a **description** of the **target customer** — a young commuter who would **go downtown** each morning. She walked **downtown** herself to watch real **customers** and their **consumer** habits. To win a **competitive edge**, she argued, the firm must **dominate** that one small **market segment** rather than chase everyone. The board called the plan **strategic**, approved a **strategic investment**, and a **regional cooperation** with a local design studio followed. Every figure in her report said the idea was sound and **viable**.

### 2. Building the brand

The studio shaped a bottle with an **idiosyncrasy** all its own. Mara registered a **trademark** and hired a writer to craft a **slogan**: "Carry less, live more." **Branding**, she insisted, was more than a logo; it was the **brand image** that people carried in their heads. She rejected a **multi-brand** plan and instead bet everything on one **own-brand product**. A glossy **brochure** went to the **subscriber** list, and early **subscription** orders arrived **prepaid**. To keep the **brand loyalty** of first buyers, she slipped a thank-you card into each box. An outside firm offered **below-the-line** ads in trade journals, and she ran a **classified advertisement** to test the response. When an accountant asked for a **valuation** of the whole campaign, Mara smiled — the real value lived in the **brand**, not in the bottles.

### 3. Channels and delivery

Mara knew a great bottle failed without **distribution**. She signed a **distributor** to run the **distribution chain** and opened a **distribution channel** through city **outlets**. She also tried **direct selling**: a team offered **door-to-door service**, promising to **deliver** each order **to the door**. "We **serve** the customer, so we **serve as** their partner," she told the new **retailer**s. Every **retail** shelf in the **retail sector** got a tester. Careful **sourcing** of steel kept costs low, and she **distributed a bonus** to the sales team who **served out** their first contract.

### 4. Launch day

Launch day brought **hype** the team had not expected. To win **market penetration**, Mara cut the **market price** and offered a **cut-price** trial pack; rival shops answered with **cut-rate** deals of their own. She did not panic. She **sponsored** a charity run in the park, and the **sponsorship** put her **slogan** on a thousand shirts. A **freebie** key ring went into every box. She used the moment to **promote business relationships** with local shops and to **promote the sale of** the new line. "We **boost** sales by helping, not by shouting," she said — and the **boost in sales** proved her right. She **subscribed** to a trade journal and urged loyal **subscriber**s to **subscribe to** its newsletter. Part of the budget funded **non-commercial activities** in the community, which she counted as **service**, not advertising.

### 5. The reckoning

When the quarter closed, not every early buyer stayed. Mara **reallocated** the ad budget and **divested** herself of the weakest **outlet**, hoping to **divest** the firm of dead weight. She had to **remainder** the last unsold boxes at a **cut-price** rate. "A **customer** is not a **subscription** you can simply file away," she wrote in her report. She owed **dues** to the trade association and thanked the **sponsor** who had stood by her. The **valuation** of the campaign finally matched her first **viable proposition**: by dominating one **segment** with a clear **brand** and honest **service**, a small firm could win a **competitive edge** in a **niche** and keep the **competition** at bay.

## 四、学习建议

1. **上市链条**：`viable proposition`（可行建议）→ `strategic investment`（战略投资）→ `market segmentation`（细分市场）→ `target customer`（目标顾客）→ `brand image`（品牌形象）→ `market penetration`（市场渗透），正好是一条新品上市的策划线。
2. **品牌词族**：`brand`（品牌）、`branding`（品牌设计）、`trademark`（商标）、`slogan`（标语）、`brand loyalty`（忠诚度）、`brand image`（形象）一起记，都是塑造品牌的高频词。
3. **价格对照**：`cut-price`（低价）与 `cut-rate`（削价）都带贬义色彩，配合 `market price`（市场价）与 `prepaid`（预付）对比记忆，描述价格战很好用。
4. **分销渠道**：`distribution chain`（分销链）、`distribution channel`（分销渠道）、`distributor`（分销商）、`retailer`（零售商）、`direct selling`（直销）、`door-to-door service`（送货上门）是渠道铺货场景的核心搭配。
5. **动词短语**：`divest sb. of sth.`（剥夺/剥离）、`reallocate`（再分配）、`remainder`（廉价出售）、`subscribe to`（订阅）、`serve as`（充当）、`serve out`（做到期满）都落在预算与库存的处理决策里，配套记忆更牢。
