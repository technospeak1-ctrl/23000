## 一、编排逻辑：为什么这样组织

本主题比 Topic 001 更大，但词群脉络更清晰，可归为 **6 个语义场**：住处设备、家人身份、钱财生活、日常劳作、杂物媒介、性情状态。我用一个年轻设计师诺拉「独自居住的第一年」作主线，把语义场分配到 5 个连续场景：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载的语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | The keys 钥匙 | 住处构件 + 家用设备 | 建立情境（搬进小公寓） |
| II | What she carried 她带着的人 | 血缘、身份、亲属 | 交代牵挂（她从哪来） |
| III | The price of a place 安家的代价 | 钱财、保险、节俭与挥霍 | 揭示冲突（理想与现实的账） |
| IV | Ten months elapsed 十个月 | 日常劳作 + 性情状态 + 杂物媒介 | 人物成长（从混乱到自足） |
| V | The potluck 百乐餐 | 款待 + 全部搭配收束 | 主题升华（家是习惯建成的） |

**对 EFL 学习者的价值**：\`be accustomed to\` 在首尾两次出现（先"不习惯"，后"习惯了"），形成呼应；\`a comfortable home\` 由"还不是家"到"家由小习惯建成"，构成主题句。词汇随情节推进被反复激活，而非一次性罗列。

## 二、英文故事　Flat 5B

### 1. The keys

At twenty-nine, Nora left the house she had grown up in and rented a **tiny** flat on the fifth floor of a **residential** block. It was a long way from the **chateau** her friend Isabelle called home, but it was hers. The **janitor** handed over the keys without a word; the building's **resident** cats watched from the stairs. On her first morning the **alarm** she had set for work did not ring, the **buzzer** by the door was dead, and a bare **bulb** swung in the hallway. The **dial** on the second-hand oven would not turn at all. A cracked pipe the week before had caused a small **accident** — a flooded floor — which explained the **moisture** creeping up the bathroom wall, so she bought a **dehumidifier**. The **air conditioning** coughed; the **furnace** in the basement groaned. A narrow balcony served as an **adjunct** to the living room. The **lavatory** leaked, the **garbage** bin overflowed, and a basket of **underwear** waited by the door. She hung **curtains**, laid a **mat** on the floor, plumped the **cushion** on a second-hand sofa, and stacked her books. There was no time to **dillydally**. It was not yet **a comfortable home** — not remotely — but the **property** was hers, her only real **asset**, and she meant to make it one. She was not yet **accustomed to** living alone, and the silence felt like an accusation.

### 2. What she carried with her

Her **ancestral home** stood three hundred miles away, in a village whose **ancestry** her **guardian** aunt could trace back five generations. Everyone called her "Nora," a **nickname** that had hardened into a kind of **alias** — her real name was Eleanor, a fact she seldom mentioned. Her aunt, who had never married, had quietly grieved an **abortion** years earlier and had poured all her love into Nora instead; hers was the best **advice** Nora ever got. There was also her **toddler** niece, who had been **a tiny baby** at their last meeting and now called her "Auntie No-No," leaving sticky fingerprints on every surface. The family was **akin** to a small, noisy nation, and Nora **was on familiar terms** with every member of it. Being the first in her family to live alone gave her a strange new **status** — half **adult**, half exile — and she wondered whether this **uniqueness** was a gift or a sentence. Some evenings she was **homesick** enough to cry into her tea. Her aunt's life had been **austere** and traditional; Nora's was **non-traditional** and, she hoped, **self-sufficient**. From her window she could see the **civic** square, and beyond it the **well-to-do** neighbourhood with its tidy gardens.

### 3. The price of a place

The flat had not been cheap. Most of her **income** as a designer went to the mortgage; she paid the **insurance** every year without complaint and kept what savings she had in a small **coffer** bought at a flea market. The air conditioning had been bought on an **installment** plan, and each month the **commitment** arrived in the post like a bill for her own ambition. Once, on a whim, she bought a **lottery** ticket and won what felt like a **fortune** — ninety pounds, enough for a new bulb and a bunch of flowers. Pure **fortuitous** luck, the sort of thing that made her laugh out loud. Isabelle, **well-to-do** and restless, spent her evenings on **prodigal entertainment** — expensive wine, long dinners, no questions about the bill. Nora chose **frugality** instead, treating every **coupon** as a small victory. She allowed herself one **indulgence** — a bouquet of white flowers on Fridays — and no more. Her aunt had always said that **a comfortable home** is built from small, stubborn habits, not large sums of money.

### 4. Ten months elapsed

Ten months **elapsed**. The **chaotic** flat she had moved into was now ordered, but not rigidly so: where there had been disorder, there was an **unhurried** rhythm; where there had been **junk**, there was purpose. She learned to **sew** a button back on, to **plait** her hair before work, and to **sprinkle** her pillows **with** a little lavender water, so that her sleep smelled of **fragrant memories** of her grandmother's garden. On Sunday mornings she would **pamper** herself with a long shower and an **emollient** bought on sale, then **personalize** old jam jars with careful labels — sugar, salt, Nora — until the kitchen felt unmistakably hers. She discovered she was **self-sufficient** in ways she had never expected: she could cook, mend, pay bills, and be alone without fear. Her **chores** became a kind of ritual — wiping the counter with a **sponge**, cutting ribbon with **scissors**, feeding the neighbour's cat a **pellet** of dry food. Even the **junk** drawer had its place. By Friday, **exhaustion** had her in its grip, and she fell into **a profound sleep** so deep that she did not hear the television, though she had become a devoted **viewer** of late-night documentaries. She recorded a **video** of her niece's first steps, sent a **videophone** message to her aunt, and kept every **videotape** and every **stamp** in an **album** on the shelf. She was finally **accustomed to** the quiet — and, to her surprise, found it **enjoyable**.

### 5. The potluck

In the tenth month she hosted a **potluck**. She did not try to **entertain** her guests **with** Isabelle's extravagance; she served **homemade** soup, borrowed chairs, and set a **bouquet** of white flowers on the table. She had **engaged in** cooking everything from scratch — a commitment she nearly regretted at midnight. Her aunt arrived with a letter and a fresh **bouquet**; her niece, now walking, demanded to be lifted onto the **mat**. Isabelle came too, and Nora **congratulated** her **on** her new job. The neighbours, with whom she **was on familiar terms**, brought dishes and laughter; the boy next door, home for the weekend from a **residential school**, played his guitar. Before the party she had tried on a new dress in the **fitting room** downstairs, and now, as the **curtain** stirred in the warm air, she decided the whole imperfect evening was entirely **fitting** — even the **buzzer**, which still did not work. Compared with what her aunt had survived, such troubles were a **trifle**. The evening was **hilarious**, warm, and unhurried. When the last guest left, the **response** she felt was not loneliness but something close to pride. She turned off the **alarm**, checked the loose **wire** in the hallway, and slept **a profound sleep**. In the morning the light through the curtain looked, somehow, **unspoiled**.

## 四、学习建议

1. **抓两条呼应线**：\`be accustomed to\`（不习惯→习惯）与 \`a comfortable home\`（还不是家→家由小习惯建成）。它们是本故事的骨架，比单个生词更值得记。
2. **按场景回忆六类词**：钥匙（设备）→ 家人（身份）→ 代价（钱财）→ 十个月（劳作与性情）→ 百乐餐（款待）。
3. **注意语域差异**：\`chateau\`、\`coffer\`、\`prodigal\`、\`austere\` 偏书面；\`buzzer\`、\`junk\`、\`dillydally\` 偏口语。故事刻意混用，以呈现真实英语的语体层次。
4. **优先记搭配**：10 条常用搭配与 5 个动词搭配（congratulate on / engage in / entertain with / sprinkle with / be accustomed to）是高频考点，比孤立词义更有用。
