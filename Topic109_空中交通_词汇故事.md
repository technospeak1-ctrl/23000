## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"空中交通"，词表既有机场与飞行器名词，也有电报通信、几何方向和险情词。我用"实习空乘诺拉第一次随机飞行、遭遇液压泄漏并协助发送求救信号"作主线，按"登机准备 → 认识飞行器 → 空中险情 → 安全迫降"推进，让每类词都在情节里各就各位：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Boarding 登机 | 机场设施 + 登机动词 + 通信/邮政背景 + 形容词语 | 建立场景与人物背景 |
| 2 | The machine 飞行器 | 机型与机件 + 方向/几何词 + 飞行姿态 | 认识飞行器 |
| 3 | Trouble in the air 险情 | 事故名词 + 险情动词 + 情绪词 | 情节高潮 |
| 4 | A message delivered 迫降 | 通信/投递动词 + 收束词 | 收束与点题 |

**设计要点**：`postage / telegraph / cable / network / medium / signal` 等通信词落在"诺拉早年做过机场电报邮政工作"的背景里，自然解释她为何擅长发报；`axis / vector / velocity / elliptical / perpendicular` 等几何与飞行词由机长在驾驶舱讲解；`by accident / identifiable identity / three dimensional image / personal airplane / in ultimate` 等搭配嵌入登机与收束场景；`leakage / drop / headlong / veer / crash` 串成一段因果递进的险情。

## 二、英文故事　The Flight That Taught Nora

### 1. Boarding

Nora had saved for a year to afford the **airfare**, and on the morning of her first real flight she went through the **entrance** of the terminal with a **quick** step, her pulse beating with **rapidity**. She was a trainee with a small regional **airline**, and today she would at last **embark** on a passenger **aircraft** and fly **aboard** it as a member of the crew. Before she joined the **airline**, Nora had worked in the terminal's tiny **postage** and **telegraph** office, where every **message** went out by **cable** and one **network** carried **communication** across the country; she knew how a **signal** travelled and that radio was only one **medium** among several. Now a loudspeaker began to **blare** the boarding call. The **facility** was new and bright. Nora took her **seat**, pushed her bag underneath it, and made her way along the narrow **aisle**, noting where the **exit** was, for the instructor had told her to stay **attentive**. A **three dimensional image** on the safety screen showed the evacuation slides in sharp **dimensional** detail, and her **identifiable identity** badge — a photograph and a name — made her **identifiable** to everyone on board.

### 2. The machine

Captain Vale, her instructor, met her **aboard** and led her into the cockpit. "This is what **aviation** is really about," he said, tapping the **gauge** on the panel. Every **part** mattered, he explained, from a single **spoke** of a landing wheel to the **parachute** folded into the **ejection** seat. He pointed out the **jet** engines of the **liner**, the turning rotors of a **helicopter** crossing the field, and even a remote **balloon** that drifted slowly **upward** in the **ambient** air. His own **personal airplane** rested in the sun nearby. On a chart he drew an **axis** and then a **vector** to show the line of flight, while the **velocity** climbed steadily on the dial. "A turn is no **elliptical** guess," he said; "the wings must stay **perpendicular** to the flow." He spoke of **shunt** valves in the fuel lines and of the **orientation** lights that marked the **upward** path. Outside, a flock of sparrows began to **flit** past the window — a small **aerial** ballet before takeoff.

### 3. Trouble in the air

An hour into the flight, **by accident**, a latch worked loose and a fine **leakage** of hydraulic fluid began to spray. The aircraft gave a sudden **drop** and rolled **headlong** toward the clouds, so Captain Vale had to **veer** sharply to avoid a **crash**. "I would not **dare** to guess what that noise was," Nora whispered — and she was right to be careful, for the bad weather had already **thwarted** two earlier flights that morning. In the **midst** of the alarm, the only **means** of staying calm was to breathe. The shaking would **frighten** the steadiest passenger, and the sight of a cracked panel **horrified** Nora, who pictured a smoking **wreck** on the runway and **wreckage** scattered across the grass. It was, she realised, an **accidental** loose latch — nothing more — but in the air nothing is small.

### 4. A message delivered

Working fast, Nora helped **transmit** an emergency **message** to the ground, and the controllers **communicated** a clear reply, their **signal** steady on the dial. "We have a **twofold** problem," the captain said, "a leak and a loss of height." He brought the aircraft down with practised calm, and they landed safely. Within minutes a ground crew's **delivery** of a spare part **arrived**, and a mechanic **delivered** the news that the fault was fixed. As they **arrived** at the gate, Nora understood the **ultimate** lesson: **in ultimate**, flying is not about speed but about care. The safe **delivery** of every passenger to the ground was the truest measure of success.

## 四、学习建议

1. **飞行链条**：`airfare`（票价）→ `airline`（航空公司）→ `entrance / facility`（入口/设施）→ `embark / aboard`（登机/在机上）→ `seat / aisle / exit`（座位/过道/出口）——按一次乘机的动线记忆。
2. **通信与投递组**：`postage / telegraph / cable` 是老式通信手段，`network / medium / signal / message` 是通用通信词，`transmit / communicate / deliver / delivery` 是"发送—传达—送达"的动词链。
3. **飞行器谱系**：`balloon`（气球）→ `helicopter`（直升机）→ `jet / liner`（喷气机/班机）→ `aircraft`（航空器总称）；`parachute / ejection / gauge / part / spoke / shunt` 是机件。
4. **几何与飞行**：`axis`（轴）+ `vector`（矢量）定方向，`velocity`（速率）表快慢，`perpendicular`（垂直）与 `elliptical`（椭圆）描述姿态，`orientation`（定位）与 `upward / ambient / aerial`（向上/周围/空中）描摹空间。
5. **险情递进**：`leakage`（渗漏）→ `drop`（下坠）→ `headlong`（一头栽下）→ `veer`（急转）→ `crash / wreck / wreckage`（坠毁/残骸），`frighten / horrify` 写情绪，`thwart / dare` 写阻挠与勇气。
6. **易混提示**：`aboard` 是"在（交通工具）上"，`abroad` 才是"在国外"；`ejection`（弹射/排出）来自动词 `eject`；`ultimate`（最终的）与 `ultimately`（最终地）同源，搭配 `in ultimate` 表示"到最后"。
