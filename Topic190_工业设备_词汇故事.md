## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"工业设备"，既有大量设备/材料名词，又有不少性质形容词与动词搭配，很容易被写成设备清单。我用"质量检验员里德小姐到哈尔登工厂做一次突击审计：从大门到车间，经历一次小事故，再在出货中查出赝品，最后写下报告"为主线，按"工厂大门 → 设备车间 → 火花事故 → 出货赝品 → 审计报告"五幕推进，把每个术语落到一台设备、一次操作或一个判断上。

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The gate of Halden Works 工厂大门 | 工厂/制造商/设计研究词 + 大规模设备 | 建立人物与场景 |
| 2 | The equipment floor 设备车间 | 材料/构件/状态形容词 + 物件名词 | 铺陈设备谱系 |
| 3 | The spark 火花事故 | 事故/安全动词 + 性质副词 | 用突发事件串起动作 |
| 4 | The forgery in the shipment 出货赝品 | 质量/废料/回收词 | 情节推进、检验主题 |
| 5 | The verdict 审计报告 | 评价形容词 + 收束搭配 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`bulky equipment / stainless steel / composite / hinge / elastic / harness / storage` 全部落到车间里的具体装置；`spark → collision → explosion → uncontrolled → securely / rapidly → electrician → splint` 串成一条"起火—碰撞—爆炸—控制—急救"的安全因果链；`forgery / coin / nugget / horology / integrity` 围绕"钟表厂送检的金币疑案"形成一个微型悬疑；动词搭配 `trigger a switch / prop up / loom large / recall sb. to life / retrieve…from / approximate to` 全部嵌进事故与检验动作里，避免清单化。

## 二、英文故事　The Audit at Halden Works

### 1. The gate of Halden Works

Ms. Reed drove **a considerable distance** to reach Halden Works, a **factory** that had become a symbol of local **industrialization**. The **manufacturer** had grown from one small shop into a giant. At the gate she showed her badge and was met by the chief **designer**, who had drawn up the new line with **technical** skill and real **technical expertise**. "We began with **extensive research**," he said, "and for **various reasons** we chose this **spot**." A **stall** by the gate sold tea. The plant floor lay far beyond, and an **elevator** carried them up to the bright **illumination** of the offices. Below, **bulky equipment** stood in rows.

### 2. The equipment floor

The works made goods in many **categories**: steel **compound** panels, a **composite** alloy, and a **stainless** shell that never rusted — the secret was **stainless steel**. The old **technique** had been handed down, and **productivity** kept rising. **Hinges** turned smoothly on every door, strips of **elastic** held the guards in place, and a canvas **harness** hung by the **storage** racks. "We keep a year's **provision** of parts," the designer said, "and we check the **quantity** and the **width** of every coil." Reed noted a faint **corrosion** on one frame and a trace of **impurity** in the alloy; such small **nuances** mattered, and the **distinction** between good and bad metal was a test of **continuity** in the supply.

### 3. The spark

Suddenly a **spark** flew from a motor; a **collision** of two carts followed, and a small **explosion** of dust shook the aisle. Nothing stayed **uncontrolled** for long: the crew acted **rapidly** and **securely**, and an **electrician** used a **splint** to brace a bent guard. He reached up to **trigger a switch** and cut the power. As a crane swung past, its its shadow **loomed large** on the wall on the wall — a shape that made the danger **loom large** in everyone's mind. One worker was briefly knocked out, and the medic managed to **recall him to life** with smelling salts. An unforeseen **contingent** — a delayed delivery — made matters worse, but the team **propped up** the sagging rack and **extended** a temporary shield. The loss **approximated to** little more than an hour of output. For the safety plan it was a real **workout**.

### 4. The forgery in the shipment

In the warehouse Reed examined a **shipment** of small metal parts marked as gold **nuggets** for a **horology** firm that made watch cases. The works used to **forge** its own steel, and one **coin** in the sample was a clever **forgery** — the **integrity** of the whole batch was now doubtful. Mixed in were **scrap** and **remnant** pieces, and the **wastage** in the bin was higher than the books admitted. She ordered the crew to **recycle** what they could and to **retrieve** the good parts **from** the rejected pile. A fishing **seine** and tools for a nearby **ranch** were also on the order sheet, proof that the works supplied many trades. Reed praised the **productive effort** of the team and filed her report.

### 5. The verdict

In her final report Reed wrote that the plan to expand was sound, though the new advertising was **overly** bold. "**Superb advertisements** cannot hide **an impractical plan**," she warned, "but the **exploration** of new markets is wise." She liked the **unconventional ideas** of the young designer, praised the **qualitative analysis** of the alloy and the finish of the **stainless** line, and agreed that the **moderate prices** were fair. The safety stock was **sufficient for** a fortnight, she noted, and that was enough.

## 四、学习建议

1. **审计叙事串词**：把 `factory → manufacturer → designer → technical expertise → extensive research → equipment → sparks → forgery → report` 当成一条参观动线，每个词都是审计员"看到或做到的一件事"，比按字母背更牢。
2. **安全因果链**：`spark → collision → explosion → uncontrolled → rapidly / securely → electrician → splint → trigger a switch → recall sb. to life`，正好是一次事故的处置流程，六条动词搭配全在其中。
3. **易混近义词**：`corrosion / impurity / wastage / scrap / remnant` 都指"不好的残余物"；`integrity / continuity / distinction / nuance` 是质量报告里的抽象词，放在一起对比记忆。
4. **形容词配对**：`bulky`（笨重）↔ `moderate`（适中）、`uncontrolled`（失控）↔ `securely`（稳妥）、`impractical`（不切实际）↔ `productive`（富有成效）、`unconventional`（非传统）↔ `technical`（技术），正面与负面性质词在同一场景里形成对照。
