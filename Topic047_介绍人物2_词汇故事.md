## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"人物品质形容词 + 人物特质名词 + 少量动词"，比上一篇更细：既有 `adept / diplomatic / judicious` 这类褒义，也有 `crooked / contemptible / ludicrous` 这类贬义，还有 `facade / foible / forte / mien` 这类"识人维度"名词。我把它们编排成一本**选角簿**：老导演 Mrs. Vale 为一出叫《千人千面》的戏挑演员，按"建档 → 可塑之才 → 麻烦人物 → 怪异档案 → 首演之夜"推进。褒贬人物被放进同一批试镜，形成对照；`come in handy`、`an innate ability`、`a facade of charm` 等搭配成为推动情节的线索，而非孤立罗列。

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The casting book 选角簿 | 识人维度名词（attribute/feature/forte/foible/mien…） | 建立框架 |
| 2 | The hopefuls 可塑之才 | 正面品质形容词 + 常用搭配 | 正面群像 |
| 3 | The misfits 麻烦人物 | 负面品质形容词 + 动词 disapprove/recount | 反面群像 |
| 4 | The odd files 怪异档案 |  remaining 人物名词 + 剩余形容词 | 中间地带 |
| 5 | Opening night 首演之夜 | 剩余词 + 升华 | 收束主题 |

**设计要点**：把 `adept in`、`an attractive personality`、`a judicious choice`、`moderate character`、`mercurial wit`、`inert gases` 等搭配直接写进旁白；`advantage / aptitude / merit / foible / forte / mien / sophistication / nobility` 成为导演权衡演员的"维度"。三个动词 `disapprove of`、`exhilarate`、`recount` 分别落在"淘汰""振奋""复述"三个动作节点上。

## 二、英文故事　The Casting Book

### 1. The casting book

Mrs. Vale was casting a pageant called *The Town of Many Faces*, and she kept a thick book of **attributes** on her desk. "Every **feature** of a person," she told her assistant, "is either an **advantage** or a **foible**." She filed each applicant by **identity** and **gender**, noting their **aptitude** for the stage, their **forte**, their **intelligence** and their **intuition**. A good **mien**, a gracious **gesture**, a lively **mind** — these were the **merits** she sought. **Courtesy** and **goodwill** counted for more than a famous name. She asked about each candidate's **hobby**, their **interest** in the part, even the **mood** they brought into the room. To be fair she devised **an equitable system** of auditions, giving every hopeful the same three minutes. "I would **bet** my reputation," she said, "that the right face is already in this book." Her **intuition** rarely failed her.

### 2. The hopefuls

The first auditions glittered. There was Clara, an **adept** young violinist **adept in** the bow, **attractive** and **cordial**, with **an attractive personality** that warmed the room; she was **credible** to the last word and **explicit** in her answers. Next came a **diplomatic** mediator, **equitable** in every ruling, **judicious** with his praise, **magnanimous** to rivals and **moderate** in his demands — **moderate character** was written all over him. A **gracious** old actress arrived with real **hospitality**, **mature** and **modest**, **modest character** in every bow; her **mercurial wit** kept everyone laughing. A **loyal** stagehand, **loyal to his duty**, proved **handy** with a ladder — he **came in handy** again and again. There was **an intelligent reader** with an **innate** ear for verse, **an innate ability** no school could teach, and an **interpersonal** charm that drew strangers together. **Extraordinary** children, **perky** and bright, recited **explicit instructions** from memory; even a **chaste**, **delicate** heroine and a **bald** old clown found their places. Mrs. Vale made **a judicious choice** at once.

### 3. The misfits

The second pile was harder. A **crooked businessman** arrived, **crooked** in his accounts and **contemptible** in his flattery, **cynical** about every promise; Mrs. Vale **disapproved of** him at once. Beside him an **aggressive** challenger showed **an aggressive behavior**, **impetuous** in his speech — **an impetuous young man** who shouted before he thought. A **gullible** clerk believed every rumor; a **naive** girl wore **a naive attitude** like a badge, and a **loose woman** with a **loose** tongue laughed too loudly. One candidate had **an ambivalent mind**, torn between two parts; another's **an erratic behavior** kept the rehearsal in an uproar, and a **hectic** afternoon left everyone breathless. A **husky** old singer with **a husky voice** croaked a ballad, while an **irreverent** joker made **an irritating habit** of mocking the script. There was a **lewd** remark from a **loose** backstage hand, a **grim** critic with a **lofty** nose, a **ludicrous**, **ungainly** dance, and a **mean**, **naked** ambition that hid behind **a facade** of charm. Even **inert gases** seemed livelier than the **inert** silence that followed. An **inexperienced** understudy, **color-blind** to the costumes, muttered an apology and showed **a curious behavior** that made the cast stare. An **inveterate** gossip **recounted** every scandal in the book.

### 4. The odd files

A few files defied every label. There was an **elderly** **fanatic** who hoarded old **kudos** from forgotten plays, a **gourmand** who treated rehearsals as a **diversion** between meals, and a **skinflint** celebrated for his **thrift** — he spoke often of **a needy family** yet never gave a coin. A **neurotic** actress with a **stammer** paced the room in **fury**, kicking up a **fuss** over a missing prop; her **melancholy** was as plain as her gift was great. A **masculine** young soldier carried himself with the **might** of a **mighty** oak, and a refined widow brought a **sophistication** that reminded Mrs. Vale of true **nobility**. "Their **stardom**," she murmured, "is built on **mien** and **merit**, not on a painted **facade**." One **curious** old entry even made her smile: a **naive**, **perky** boy who had once mistaken a **husky** voice for a dirge.

### 5. Opening night

On opening night the theatre was **hectic** and bright. Mrs. Vale watched her **interpersonal** mix of players — the **adept**, the **magnanimous**, the **modest**, even the **crooked** and the **cynical** — take their bows. The **gourmand** brought cakes, the **skinflint** unexpectedly paid for the lamps, and the **neurotic** actress forgot her **stammer** in the applause. Someone **recounted** the whole audition in an **erratic**, **husky** voice, and the **fanatic** leapt up to **exhilarate** the crowd with a speech. "A person," Mrs. Vale wrote on the last page, "is never one **attribute** but a dozen — an **advantage** here, a **foible** there, **an innate ability**, **a naive attitude**, **an irritating habit**, a **loose** tongue, **a husky voice**, **a judicious choice** made at the right time." She closed the book, proud of the **goodwill** in the room and of the **equitable system** that had given every strange soul his three minutes on the stage.

## 四、学习建议

1. **按"识人维度"记名词**：`attribute / feature / advantage / foible / forte / merit / mien / aptitude` 是评价一个人的八个维度，可串成一句："Judge a person by **merit** and **mien**, not by **forte** alone."
2. **正反对照记形容词**：`adept ↔ ungainly`、`credible ↔ contemptible`、`judicious ↔ impetuous`、`magnanimous ↔ mean`、`moderate ↔ loose`。故事把两类人放在同一批试镜里，建议按"对子"复习。
3. **人物标签句型**：`a crooked businessman`、`a needy family`、`a husky voice`、`an impetuous young man`——"a + adj + 名词"是介绍人物的高频结构。
4. **三个动词的动作节点**：`disapprove of`（淘汰）、`exhilarate`（振奋）、`recount`（复述）分别对应选角、首演、回味三个环节，结合情节记忆不易忘。
5. **名词的抽象含义**：`facade`（虚伪外表）、`foible`（性格弱点）、`sophistication`（老练）、`nobility`（高贵）偏书面，造句时多用"built on / hidden behind / reminded her of"这类框架，如 "His charm hid **a facade** of vanity."
