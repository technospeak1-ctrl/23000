## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"语言文学 1"，100 个词项横跨语法术语、文学体裁、修辞格与翻译动词，词量超大，最易写成清单。我用"一位译者 Marco 受命为一本文学评论杂志编纂多语种文选，从接稿、汇稿、逐字校改、卷入抄袭风波到成书"作主线，按"接受委托 → 汇集各种声音 → 语言的机器 → 抄袭风波 → 书终于印成"五幕推进，并把每幕再分成若干子场景分层承载：委托场景放语言与翻译词，汇稿场景放体裁与神话词，校改场景放语法与修辞词，风波场景放批评与动词，成书场景放总结词：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The commission 接受委托 | 语言/翻译/双语/文化词 | 建立人物与任务 |
| 2 | Gathering the voices 汇集各种声音 | 体裁/诗歌/小说/神话词 | 分层铺陈文选素材 |
| 3 | The machinery of language 语言的机器 | 语法/措辞/修辞格词 | 落到校改的具体动作 |
| 4 | The scandal 抄袭风波 | 批评/抄袭/笔名/动词词 | 矛盾激化 |
| 5 | The book is printed 书终于印成 | 总结/复兴/成果词 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`bilingual / multilingual / mandarin / vernacular / dialect / linguistic feature` 集中在"接受委托"一幕，配合 `a literal translation / take sth. literally / interpreter / interpretation / verbal agreement / nominal head` 把翻译的契约与陷阱写实；`canto / dirge / ditty / madrigal / idyll / eclogue / doggerel` 等诗歌体裁与 `biography / autobiography / fiction / novel / playwright / myth / mythology` 分层放在"汇集各种声音"一幕；`alphabet / notation / preposition / pronoun / conjunction / tense / plural / ellipsis` 是语法骨架，`idiom / metaphor / pun / irony / sarcasm / aphorism / dictum / platitude` 是修辞格，集中在"语言的机器"一幕的校改动作上；`plagiarism / pseudonym / critic / generalize / overstate / modify the approach / convey sth. to sb.` 集中在"抄袭风波"；`renaissance / fruitful soil / masterpiece / creation / historic moment` 收束于成书，避免清单化。

## 二、英文故事　The Translator's Anthology

### 1. The commission

Marco Bellini worked as a translator at a small journal of **literature** in the old city. One wet Monday the editor handed him a thick folder: the review would publish an **anthology** of world writing, and Marco was to carry the soul of each piece into **fluent English**. The brief was explicitly **multilingual**, and a **bilingual** edition was planned: **bilingual news** from the provinces would sit beside notes on **classical music** and photographs of **cultural relics** from the museum. "We need an **interpreter** whose **interpretation** keeps the **linguistic feature** of the original," the editor said, "and a **verbal agreement** with every author — a **nominal head** on the committee is not enough." Marco knew that **a literal translation** could flatten a **dialect**, and that to **take sth. literally** was a translator's oldest trap. He thought of the **mandarin** scholar who wrote in the **vernacular**, of the **linguist** who mapped a **language** no dictionary held, and of the **historic moment** when their **culture** first met the printed page. The **basis** of the whole book, he decided, was trust.

### 2. Gathering the voices

Over the next weeks the folder filled with voices. There was a **canto** from a long poem, a **dirge** for a lost city, a **ditty** sung by market children, a **madrigal** for two voices, an **idyll** of shepherds, and a shepherd's **eclogue**; a **doggerel** verse made the whole office laugh. A **playwright** sent the outline of a **plot** drawn from **fiction**, while a **biography** of a painter arrived with an **autobiography** by a retired **critic**. That critic liked to **portray** himself as **a mythical hero** among **fabulous heroes**, turning his own life into **myth** and **mythology**. Marco had to **depict** the same people in plain prose. He copied a **refrain** that ran through three **novel** chapters, marked the **prototype** of every **narrative** voice, and loved one **lyric** line that could carry a whole **classic** page.

### 3. The machinery of language

Editing the **anthology** meant staring at the machinery of **language** all day. Marco checked every **alphabet** and **notation** mark, restored an **ellipsis** where a line had been cut, and argued about **tense** and the **plural** with the proofreader. A **preposition** here, a **pronoun** there, a **conjunction** to join them: each **phrase** had to **enunciate** cleanly, and every **wording** choice had to be **distinctly** better than the first. He listed **derivative** forms in the **vocabulary** notes and learned the **usage** of **colloquial expressions** that would never survive in print. The **diction** of the market songs was **artless**, their **intonation** **markedly** different from the **classical** reciters'; an **interjection** or a **pun** could **alliterate** with the line before it, and **sarcasm** or **irony** would **convey** a meaning no **literal** reading allowed. "Take the **idiom** out and you lose the **implication**," he told the editor, pointing at an **aphorism** whose **dictum** had softened into a **platitude** in every retelling. The **graphic** cover used **computer graphics** to draw ruined walls; the **gaudy dress** on the model made Marco wince, while a page of **elegant handwriting** showed how **bombastic** prose could still look **elegant** on paper. A **metaphor** might be **indirect**, but a **verbal** slip was **markedly** obvious.

### 4. The scandal

Then the trouble came. A **critic** from a rival journal claimed that one of the **novel** chapters was **plagiarism**, written under a **pseudonym** and **deriving** whole pages from a forgotten **ditty**. He tried to **generalize** from a single **quirk** of **diction**, and to **overstate** the case until the editor's **basis** for trust began to crack. Marco had to **modify the approach**: he would **convey sth. to sb.** — in this case to the reader — by printing the disputed **interpretation** beside the original. The accused writer **reminisced** about a **mannerism** learned from an old **linguist**, and swore the **acid** tone was his own. Marco **derived** the suspect **phrase** from a **renaissance** **classic**. He refused to **generalize** about **illiterate** village singers just because their **usage** was **colloquial**. The **readability** of the final page mattered more than the **sarcasm** of the **critic**, and a **parody** turned the **platitude** into **irony**.

### 5. The book is printed

By autumn the **anthology** was bound, and the **historic moment** came on a grey morning when the first copy lay on the editor's desk. The **masterpiece** they had hoped for was not any single **classic** but the **creation** of a page where every **dialect** and **vernacular** could breathe. The editor called it a **renaissance** for the little review and spoke of the **fruitful soil** it prepared for younger writers. Marco **reminisced** about the first folder and the **verbal agreement**, and smiled at the **nominal head** who had doubted him. The **critic** who had cried **plagiarism** wrote a kinder **interpretation** of the book. On the last page, under the **elegant handwriting** of the dedication, Marco wrote a single line: "Every **metaphor** is a **myth** carried across the page."

## 四、学习建议

1. **翻译契约链**：`bilingual`（双语的）、`multilingual`（多语的）、`interpreter`（口译者）、`interpretation`（口译/解读）、`a literal translation`（直译）、`take sth. literally`（按字面理解）、`verbal agreement`（口头协议）串起"多语翻译"的完整场景。
2. **诗歌体裁群**：`canto`（诗章）、`dirge`（哀歌）、`ditty`（小调）、`madrigal`（情歌）、`idyll`（牧歌）、`eclogue`（田园诗）、`doggerel`（打油诗）、`refrain`（副歌）集中在汇稿一幕，与 `lyric`（抒情的）、`narrative`（叙述的）对照记。
3. **语法骨架**：`alphabet`（字母表）、`notation`（符号）、`preposition`（介词）、`pronoun`（代词）、`conjunction`（连词）、`tense`（时态）、`plural`（复数）、`ellipsis`（省略）是语言的形式层，配合 `diction / wording / usage / vocabulary` 一起记。
4. **修辞格**：`metaphor`（隐喻）、`idiom`（习语）、`pun`（双关）、`irony`（反讽）、`sarcasm`（挖苦）、`aphorism`（格言）、`dictum`（断言）、`platitude`（陈词滥调）都落在校改动作上，`artless`（自然）与 `bombastic`（夸大）、`gaudy`（华而不实）与 `elegant`（优雅）形成风格对照。
5. **批评与成书**：`critic`（评论家）、`plagiarism`（抄袭）、`pseudonym`（笔名）、`generalize`（一概而论）、`overstate`（夸大）写风波；`modify the approach`（改变方法）、`convey sth. to sb.`（传达给某人）、`derive`（推导）、`portray`（描绘）、`depict`（描绘）、`enunciate`（表达）、`alliterate`（押头韵）、`reminisced`（追忆）是七个翻译与写作动词；`renaissance`（复兴）、`fruitful soil`（沃土）、`masterpiece`（杰作）收尾。
