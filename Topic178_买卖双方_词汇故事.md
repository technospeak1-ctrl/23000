## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"买卖双方"，整篇都是交易对手之间的角色、条款与单据词，最易写成"商贸术语表"。我用"一位小店经销商与供货方推销员谈成一笔进口厨房用品的交易"作主线，按"询价 → 看市场 → 报价议价 → 单据条款 → 成交"五幕推进，让每个词落在具体的买卖动作上：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | An enquiry from a dealer 经销商询价 | 交易角色名词 + 建议/帮助 | 建立人物 |
| 2 | Reading the market 研判市场 | 需求/市场名词 + 渗透动词 | 铺陈背景 |
| 3 | The quotation 报价单 | 价格/条款名词 + 报价动词 | 展开议价 |
| 4 | Terms and documents 条款与单据 | 票据/服务搭配 + 支付动词 | 推进成交 |
| 5 | The deal 成交 | 剩余词 + 收束动词 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`dealer / vendor / salesman / wholesaler / franchisee / creditor / drawer / drawee` 等角色词分别贴到买卖双方具体人身上；31 条搭配如 `aggregate demand / seller's market / bottom line / soft terms / blank cheque / endorse a bill / after-sales service / sell at a loss` 沿谈判流程依次出现，成为"他们谈了什么、签了什么"的一部分；`quote / bargain / fluctuate / disburse / lend / penetrate the market` 等动词落在询价、议价、付款、渗透市场的动作链里，绝非孤立罗列。

## 二、英文故事　The Deal on Market Street

### 1. An enquiry from a dealer

Rina Patel was a **dealer** with a small shop on Market Street. One morning an **enquiry** arrived from a **franchisee** who wanted a new **article** for her store. Rina worked with a **salesman** named Tom, who represented a **vendor** and a large **wholesaler** across town. Tom gave her **advice** and offered **aid** with the forms, but Rina put the glossy catalogue **back** on the desk. "I must **afford** the stock before I order it," she said. Her **creditor** at the bank had warned her about taking on too much debt.

### 2. Reading the market

The market was changing. Tom spoke of **aggregate demand** and **aggregate supply**, of an **emerging market** overseas and a **lucrative market** for kitchen goods. "Our **target market** is young families," he said, "and right now it's a **seller's market** — buyers compete for stock." Rina was not so sure. She remembered a **slump in demand** the winter before, and she knew the town was nearly **saturated** with cheap imports. To **penetrate the market** a new brand had to be priced well, and cheap goods could **penetrate into** every shop. Tom said his firm had tried **decentralization**, giving each region its own buyer, and that careful **positioning** and a fresh **marketing mix** had helped. A **marketing alliance** with a local chain had opened doors, but a **bottleneck** at the port still held shipments up. "Our **estimated demand** for spring is modest," Rina said.

### 3. The quotation

Tom handed her a **quotation** with a **bid quotation** for the whole lot. He said the **bidding** at last year's fair had been fierce. There was a **bargain** hidden in his figures, but Rina wanted the **bargaining** to begin. She asked for a **discount**, and Tom offered a **note discount** instead. "Your **markup** is too high," she said. "Look at the **bottom line**, not the **bottom price**," he answered. The **book value** of the goods was lower than the **outlay** he wanted, and a **gap** remained between their numbers. He wrote the final figure as a **lump** sum. "These are **soft terms**," he said, "and an **additional clause** covers returns. If you pay **away from the market**, you lose the discount." Rina asked him to **quote** again.

### 4. Terms and documents

Before they **filled in** the order form, Tom produced a **blank cheque**, a **blank note**, and a **blank endorsement** for the bank. He would **endorse a bill** the next day, and the **advice for collection** was already in the post. An **affidavit of service** confirmed delivery, and the **after-sales service** team promised a quick **delivery cycle** of two weeks. The **drawer** signed; the **drawee** would pay. Tom's firm kept **storage** in the old depot, but a slow **telesales** call centre was a weakness. Prices would **fluctuate between** five and eight percent, or **fluctuate around** six, he said, and the contract would **expire** in May. Rina would **disburse** the payment only after the goods arrived, and her bank agreed to **lend** her the missing cash.

### 5. The deal

In the end Rina decided to **import** a small trial lot. She would **sell a bargain** line in her shop but refused to **sell at a loss** just to clear old stock. She could not **afford to** lose money on the first **sale**, so she asked Tom to **diminish** the **markup** a little. He agreed, and she **balanced** the books that night. The goods arrived at **wholesale** price in plain cartons, and the **after-sales service** team answered every call. Rina smiled: the **bargaining** had been hard, but the **bargain** was real.

## 四、学习建议

1. **买卖角色链**：`enquiry`（询价）→ `salesman / vendor / wholesaler`（推销员/卖方/批发商）→ `dealer / franchisee`（经销商/特许经营人）→ `drawer / drawee / creditor`（开票人/受票人/债主），把交易对手一次认清。
2. **市场与需求词**：`aggregate demand / aggregate supply`（总需求/总供应）、`slump in demand`（需求骤降）、`seller's market`（卖方市场）、`emerging / lucrative / target market`（新兴/可获利/目标市场）成组记忆，是分析行情的高频表达。
3. **价格条款词**：`quotation / bid quotation`（报价单）、`markup`（加价）、`bottom line / bottom price`（底线/底价）、`discount / note discount`（折扣/贴现）、`soft terms / additional clause`（优惠条件/附加条款）都出现在议价环节。
4. **票据与服务搭配**：`blank cheque / blank note / blank endorsement`（空白支票/汇票/背书）、`endorse a bill`（背书票据）、`advice for collection`（托收委托书）、`affidavit of service`（送达证明书）、`after-sales service`（售后服务）、`delivery cycle`（交付周期）属单证流程词。
5. **动词搭配**：`afford to`（买得起）、`fluctuate between/around`（波动）、`penetrate the market`（打进市场）、`sell a bargain / sell at a loss`（廉价出售/亏本出售）都落在"看市—报价—成交"的动作链里，配套记忆更牢。
