## 一、编排逻辑

本主题词量达 100 个，是全套中最密集的主题之一，"党派对峙"又容易退化成政治术语清单。我把它拆成"殖民遗产 → 议会与选票 → 指控与阴谋 → 分裂危机 → 收场和解"五个子场景，每个子场景承载一个语义场，让人物在同一条时间线上层层升级冲突：马洛与沃斯从政体之争走到选票之争，再走到阴谋与分裂，最后在峰会上收场。

| 幕 | 子场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A colony comes of age 殖民遗产 | 政体/殖民/权力形容词 | 建立对峙根源 |
| 2 | The caucus and the ballot 核心会议与选票 | 政党机器/选举/忠诚名词 | 铺陈博弈规则 |
| 3 | A campaign of accusation 指控 campaign | 阴谋/颠覆/执法动词 | 冲突升级 |
| 4 | The north draws a line 北方划线 | 分裂/侵入/对抗动词 | 危机高潮 |
| 5 | A valedictory settlement 告别式和解 | 收束/一致性/和解词 | 点题完成 |

**设计要点**：`colony / colonial / autonomy / territory / county / regime / despotism` 铺陈历史根源；`dominant position / predominate / puissant / ascendancy / control` 组成"权力优势"链；`radical / reactionary / expedient / duplicitous / incompatible` 写成两派互相贴的标签；`caucus / cabinet / faction / ballot / vote / campaign / watchdog` 式的选举机器词集中在第二幕；`assassination / antagonism / corrupt / expurgate / betray / subvert / usurp` 沿阴谋升级链分布；`schism / scission / secede / invade / warfare / strangulation` 落在第四幕的分裂场景；`an unanimous decision / deficient in / dominant position / independent country / radical idea` 五条常用搭配分别嵌进对应的幕，动词搭配 `to deflect attention from / be exempted from / overturn a decision / regulate one's spending / make an attack on` 则写成人物实际采取的动作。

## 二、英文故事　The Valedictory Vote

### 1. A colony comes of age

The **colony** of Varda had been **colonial** property of a distant crown for a century. When the crown finally granted **autonomy**, the **territory** was divided into three **counties**, and every **county** wrote its own **statute**. In the north the old **regime** survived as a **bureaucracy** of clerks; in the south a family **despotism** still held **control** of the **domain**. Two men now contested the **system**. Both claimed the right to **govern** the three counties. Marlowe's **conservatism** was honest; Voss wore it only when it suited him. Marlowe was a **statesman** of the old school: **decidedly** calm, **deep** in his reading of the law, and **puissant** in debate. Voss was a **politician** of the **radical** sort, **deliberately** **duplicitous**, who called every compromise **expedient** and every opponent **incompatible** with the **union**. A **reactionary** faction stood behind him, and his **radical idea** was to dissolve the courts. Voss's party held a **dominant position** in the south and began to **predominate** in the assembly. The new administration was **deficient in** trained officials, but he kept that weakness **doggo**, while Marlowe spoke of **independent** courts and a **defence** shared by all three counties — in effect, an **independent country** in everything but name.

### 2. The caucus and the ballot

Election morning found the **caucus** locked in the **cabinet** room. Each **faction** wanted its own name on the **ballot**. Marlowe's **campaign** promised **unity**; Voss offered **policy** by slogan and a **guideline** for every grievance. The **competitor** in the middle district, a former **magistrate**, warned that a **loophole** in the voting law would let one side be **exempted from** the count. "Be **exempted from** nothing," he said. **To deflect attention from** the missing names, Voss blamed the **minority**. The **opponent**'s **homage** to the crowd won the day. By nightfall confidence was **deficient** again, and a **breakdown** of trust had begun. The clerk in charge was only a **puppet** of the southern **faction**, while the **rabble** in the square cheered. Voss claimed an **ascendancy** he had not won. He demanded **allegiance** to the party above **loyalty** to the county, and the **magistrate** hoped for **an unanimous decision**; instead the **vote** was **decidedly** split. Marlowe offered a **concession** on taxes, but the **dilemma** was one of **complexity**, not courage. Every **deed** signed that week favoured the south, and the **settlement** promised by the **treaty** began to unravel.

### 3. A campaign of accusation

Then the accusations began. An **assassination** attempt against a northern **magistrate** was blamed on Voss's men, though the **antagonism** had grown for years. A clerk admitted he had been paid to **corrupt** the register and to **expurgate** the names of **minority** voters; others had **collaborated** with the southern boss. Marlowe **betrayed** no confidence, but he could not **pretend** the **schism** was small. At a public hearing Voss tried to **cleave** the **union** in two, and the crowd began to **pummel** the doors. Marlowe did not **loathe** Voss; he pitied him. He swore to **overthrow** the scheme, to **overturn a decision** that had been bought, and to **expurgate** the corrupted lists. He promised to **impair** no lawful right and proposed a rule to **regulate one's spending** in a campaign. As the uproar began to **recede**, a silence began to **reign** over the hall.

### 4. The north draws a line

The **contumacy** of the north now became open. Voss urged its leaders to **secede**, and rumours spread that the south would **invade** across the river. The **deflection** of public anger towards the capital was clumsy, and Marlowe would **oppose** it. When a **faction** of **insurgent** officers tried to **make an attack on** the assembly, the **intrusion** of soldiers into the hall and the **intervention** of the crown deepened the **strangulation** of debate. Voss called the walkout **apostasy**, but Marlowe called it conscience. The **extirpation** of the old **faction** was demanded in the square. A **scission** followed: three **magistrates** walked out, and the **breakdown** of the **union** seemed complete. Marlowe called it **warfare** by other means. He warned that to **subvert** the courts was to **usurp** the people's will, and that **overthrow** by ballot remained **overthrow** all the same.

### 5. A valedictory settlement

At the **summit** that followed, Marlowe made a **valedictory** speech. "I do not **loathe** my **opponent**," he said; "I want a **system** that can survive the **warfare** of **politics**." A **unanimous** committee was formed, and **an unanimous decision** was at last recorded on the **ballot**. Voss, **decidedly** beaten, agreed to **regulate** the **campaign** and to accept the **defence** of the **minority**. The **rein** of law was restored, the **treaty** renewed, and the **loyalty** of the **rabble** turned to **homage** for the **statesman** who had held the **union** together. The **complexity** of the **dilemma** had not vanished, but the **concession** had bought time, and no one called it **expedient** now.

## 四、学习建议

1. **权力优势链**：`dominant position` → `predominate` → `ascendancy` → `control` → `rein` → `puissant`，按"占优—支配—掌控—强势"串记，配合 `independent country` 与 `autonomy` 理解分离主义语境。
2. **选举机器词**：`caucus / cabinet / faction / ballot / vote / campaign / policy / guideline / loophole / magistrate` 一次记全一套选战流程，再配上 `an unanimous decision` 这个高频搭配。
3. **负面标签形容词**：`radical / reactionary / expedient / duplicitous / incompatible / deliberately / doggo / deep / decidedly` 是两派互贴的标签，正反同框放进第一、二幕对照记忆。
4. **阴谋升级动词**：`corrupt → expurgate → betray → subvert → usurp → overthrow → overturn a decision` 沿"腐败—删名—背叛—颠覆—篡权—推翻"层层加重，配合 `assassination / antagonism / strangulation` 理解冲突升级。
5. **分裂与收场**：`schism / scission / secede / invade / warfare` 写分裂危机；`summit / valedictory / unanimous / settlement / treaty` 写和解收场，两组形成完整叙事闭环。
