## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"数学 1"，词项以几何图形、数与运算、函数变化、数据统计四条线为主，极易写成术语清单。我用"数学老师 Mr. Hale 在校庆数学开放日带学生闯四关、最后解一道综合题"作主线，按"图形庭院 → 数与方程 → 函数与运动 → 数据与概率 → 终极大题"五幕推进，让每个术语都成为老师手中的教具、黑板上的例子或学生要解的题：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The geometry garden 图形庭院 | 几何图形 + 度量 + 立体 | 用校园器物建立形状词族 |
| 2 | Numbers and signs 数与符号 | 十进制 + 单复数 + 加减乘 | 铺陈数与运算词 |
| 3 | Functions and motion 函数与运动 | 线性/指数 + 变化 + 圆周运动 | 把抽象函数变成可看的运动 |
| 4 | Data and chance 数据与概率 | 统计 + 计算 + 概率 | 让数据词落在问卷统计上 |
| 5 | The final problem 终极大题 | 解题动词 + 剩余修辞词 | 用"解题步骤"收束全篇 |

**设计要点**：12 条搭配全部落在真实教具与动作里——`a centigrade thermometer` 量气温、`a decimal system`/`digital control` 写在黑板与屏幕上、`circular motion`/`centrifugal` 用旋转车轮与飞出的水滴演示、`linear algebra`/`quantitative analysis` 是课堂内容、`in multiples` 是老师提醒计数的话。几何线按"triangle→hexagon→octagon→polygon→quadrilateral"成串，度量线按"radius→diameter→circumference→perimeter→girth"成串；`disproportionate / incommensurate / irreducible / tenable / inappropriate / proper` 等评价性形容词分别贴到比例、分数、理论与符号的选择上；12 个解题动词在最后一幕按"normalize→conflate→realign→subtract→reduce→calculate"的真实步骤依次出现，形成因果链而非词表。

## 二、英文故事　The Mathematics Open Day

### 1. The geometry garden

On the morning of the school's mathematics open day, Mr. Hale led his class into the courtyard. "**Mathematics** is the study of shape, number and change," he said. On the ground lay a large **triangle**, an **oblong** **rectangle**, and a six-sided **hexagon** next to an eight-sided **octagon**; together they formed a **polygon** display. A **quadrangle** and a **quadrilateral** were chalked on the paving, and students measured every **vertex** and every **diagonal** line. "The **hypotenuse** is the longest side of a right triangle," Hale said, pointing to an **angular** corner, a **triangular** frame and a **perpendicular** post. One group used a **sextant** to sight the flagpole, while another drew a **tangential** line that just touched a **circular** disc. They measured the **radius**, the **diameter** and the **circumference** of the disc, and the **perimeter** of a **rectangle**; a cord around a **spherical** globe gave its **girth**. A **cube** and other **three-dimensional** solids stood on a bench. "A single **dot** starts a **curve**," Hale said, "and a curve can close into a ring; every **shape** has at least one **dimension**." There was a **raff** of shapes on that bench, and a **centigrade** thermometer beside a beaker recorded **minute** changes in the morning air.

### 2. Numbers and signs

Inside the classroom Hale turned to number. He wrote a **decimal** system on the board and set a **digital** control panel beside it. "In the decimal system each place is worth ten times the one before," he said, "and this screen shows the same **numerical** symbols." He wrote the symbols 0, 1, 2 and explained that **odd** numbers such as 3 and 5 alternate with even ones, while a **plural** form like "threes" names more than one **singular** value. "Use the plural form for a set and the singular for a single case," he said. Then came the signs: a **plus** and a **minus**. "**Addition** and **augmentation** both make a quantity grow," he explained, "while **reduction** and **subtractive** change take away." He wrote a **quarter** as 1/4 and a **triple** as three times as much; a **tetrad** was a group of four, and **nil** meant nothing at all. "In **arithmetic** we **count**, add and **multiply**; in a **matrix** we arrange numbers in rows and columns." A student asked why the same sign could be **inappropriate** in one line and **proper** in another. "At the **proper** time," Hale smiled, "a sign is chosen to fit the problem."

### 3. Functions and motion

After break Hale drew a graph of a straight line. "This is **linear** growth," he said; "in linear algebra we study lines and their combinations. A single **variable** x stands for a number we do not yet know, and a **parameter** is a fixed number that shapes the whole family. Put them into a **formula** and you get an **equation**; feed a value in and a **function** gives one back." On the same axes he sketched an **exponential** curve that rose ever faster, and a second line that wavered **erratically**. "A wheel in **circular** motion turns at a steady rate," he said, "but a drop of water thrown from a spinning tyre flies off along a **centrifugal** path: it **gyrates** around the rim and then leaves it. If the radius grows only **marginally**, the change can look **disproportionate** to the eye; one ratio may be **incommensurate** with another and refuse to simplify into an **irreducible** fraction." He wrote a **tenable** theory on the board — one that survives every test — and crossed out a claim that was clearly **inappropriate**.

### 4. Data and chance

The last board was given to data. Hale handed out a **printout** of the class survey and a **graph** showing the results, then a **graphics** chart drawn by the school computer. "The **average** of these marks is the **computation** we make after every **calculation**," he said. "We **quantify** each answer, **numerate** the list, and write up the **quantitative** analysis in our report." A student pointed at one surprising **instance**: "This **variant** of the problem gave a different answer." "Good," said Hale; "a small **imbalance** in the sample, or an **asymmetry** in the way we asked the question, can change the **probability**. One answer sheet had a **remaining** blank, another showed **nil** where a number should be." He added the marks in **multiples** of five and asked the class to **multiply** the total by two. "In multiples," he reminded them, "means again and again."

### 5. The final problem

At the end of the day Hale set one last problem on the board and told the class to **analyze** it step by step. "First **normalize** the data so every value uses the same scale," he said; "then **conflate** the two tables into one, **realign** the columns that do not match, **subtract** the smaller total from the larger, **reduce** the fraction, and **calculate** the answer. If you conflate two different ideas by mistake, you will **allay** no one's doubts, but a clear method calms the room." He opened the textbook to a short **subsection** on word problems. "Do not treat a **disproportionate** guess as a proof," he warned. "A **treble** of the first value and a **triple** of the second are the same idea in two words." When a student still hesitated, Hale drew the **circular** ring and the **three-dimensional** cube again. "At the **proper** time every number finds its place," he said. "That is the **tenable** theory we take home."

## 四、学习建议

1. **几何词族成串记**：`triangle / hexagon / octagon / polygon / quadrangle / quadrilateral / oblong / rectangle` 都是"边数"词，配合 `vertex / diagonal / hypotenuse` 一起画在图上记；度量链 `radius → diameter → circumference → perimeter → girth` 按"从圆心到圆边、再绕一圈"的顺序背。
2. **数与符号对照记**：`plus / minus`、`odd / even`（本主题先记 odd）、`singular / plural`、`addition / reduction / subtraction`、`quarter / triple / tetrad / treble` 都是成对出现的反义或倍数词，放在同一幕最省力。
3. **变化与运动词看图像记**：`linear / exponential / erratic` 对应三条不同的曲线，`circular motion / centrifugal / gyrate` 对应"车轮转—水滴飞—绕圈"的动作，`marginally / disproportionately / incommensurate / irreducible` 是描述变化幅度和比例关系的评价词。
4. **解题动词按流程记**：`normalize → conflate → realign → subtract → reduce → calculate`，再配 `analyze / quantify / numerate / count / multiply / allay`，想象自己按步骤解一道大题，动词就不只是定义。
5. **易混辨析**：`girth`（周长，多用于绕圆形物体一周）与 `perimeter`（周长，多用于多边形）；`minute` 作形容词读 /maɪˈnjuːt/ 表"微小的"，与名词"分钟"同形异音；`treble` 与 `triple` 都表"三倍"，前者多用于音乐高音或倍数，后者更通用。
