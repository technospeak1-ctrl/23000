## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"商业 1"，名词不多但商贸搭配极密集（26 条），最易写成"商务术语表"。我用"一位白手起家的裁缝从小店做到批发企业的成长史"作主线，按"创业起步 → 产品与材料 → 资产与财务 → 订单与运营 → 收束"五幕推进，让每个搭配落在具体的经营动作上：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A self-made shopkeeper 白手起家 | 创业身份 + 启动搭配 | 建立人物 |
| 2 | Materials and goods 材料与货品 | 材料形容词 + 产品搭配 | 铺陈产品 |
| 3 | Assets and money 资产与财务 | 资产/股票/财务搭配 | 展现经营 |
| 4 | Orders and trading 订单与交易 | 订购/批发/贸易搭配 | 情节推进 |
| 5 | The ledger 账本回望 | 剩余词 + 收束搭配 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`self-made / self-employed / stainless / synthetic / tangible / wholly-owned / sterling` 七个形容词分别贴到人物身份（白手起家、自主经营）与产品属性（不锈钢、合成、有形资产、全资、纯银）上；26 条搭配如 `stainless steel / tangible asset / start-up funding / trade fair / waste utilization / working cash` 全部嵌入经营流程，成为"他做了什么"的一部分，而非孤立罗列；`shut up a business / streamline / subcontract / undercharge` 等动词落在扩张与收缩的决策里。

## 二、英文故事　The Shop That Grew

### 1. A self-made shopkeeper

Sam Vale was a **self-made** man who had started as a **tailor** with a single **trolley** of cloth. His **self-employed business** began in a small room, funded by **start-up funding** he had saved from years of **waste** picking. "A **tangible asset** is worth more than a promise," he said, pointing to his **stainless steel** needles and a **sterling** silver thimble. A **shopkeeper** nearby told him about **single proprietorship** and warned him that a bad **trade** **tie** could ruin a young firm. Sam registered a **trademark** the day he made his first coat, and he kept the **scrap value** of every offcut in his **yearly budget**. Under his **white goods** sign — a joke at first, for he only sewed — he sold **tailor-made products** to the town. He even joined a **trade fair** to learn the trade.

### 2. Materials and goods

As orders grew, Sam studied **synthetic materials** and a **synthetic product** that a **spin-off** of a chemical firm had developed. "Steel is **stainless** only if you care for it," he said, comparing a **stainless** button with a common **screw**. He bought **spare part** kits for his machines and used **waste utilization** to turn clippings into stuffing. A firm offered him **unlisted securities** in payment, but he preferred cash. The **shelf life** of his cloth mattered, he told his apprentice, as did the **weight** of each bolt and the **unit** cost of every button. He refused to use **scrap** from unknown sources. "A **wholly-owned retail business** is my dream," he said, "but quality comes first."

### 3. Assets and money

By his fifth year Sam kept a proper ledger. He listed his **stock turnover**, his **working asset**s, and the **working cash** he needed each month. A **subcontractor** offered to sew for him, and Sam agreed to **subcontract** the simple work while keeping the fine **tailor-made products** in his own shop. He bought **stock** in a supplier and watched the **total** of his **surplus** rise. When a banker spoke of a **trade discount**, Sam **streamlined** his buying to earn it. He had learned that **state subsidy** could help a small firm, but he rarely claimed it. A **wholesaler** offered to carry his goods, and Sam calculated the **trade secret** of his pricing would stay with him. He paid his taxes in **sterling** and kept an **upbeat** notebook.

### 4. Orders and trading

Trade brought new problems. An **urgent order** arrived with a **shipping invoice** that listed a **transshipment** through two ports; Sam checked the **total** twice. He gave a **trade discount** to a loyal **wholesale** buyer, but he would not **undercharge** the new customers who tried to **tie** him to a loss. A late delivery led to a **suspension** of one contract, and Sam had to **shut** the line down for a week — to **shut up a business** for good, he knew, was the last resort. He entered each **tender** carefully and joined the **tertiary industry** association. A **vintage** label on a bolt of old cloth gave him an idea for a limited line, and **work in progress** filled the back room. When the **off-season** came, he **streamlined** the shop again and put **waste disposal** on a proper footing.

### 5. The ledger

At year's end Sam closed the **yearly budget** and read the **total** aloud. The **upbeat** figures hid a hard truth: **wastage** had risen, and a **surplus** of old stock sat in the store. He had started with a **trolley** and **start-up funding**, and now he ran a **wholly-owned retail business** with a **wholesale** arm. The **tangible asset**s on his list — the **stainless steel** machines, the **stock**, the **working cash** — were real; the **trade fair** contacts and the **trademark** were just as real in another way. "A **self-made** man must watch the **scrap value** of everything," he wrote, "even his own **shelf life** in this trade." He left the **shipping invoice** on the desk and went home, content that the **tie** between his name and his goods would **streamline** the years ahead.

## 四、学习建议

1. **创业链**：`self-made`（白手起家）→ `start-up funding`（启动资金）→ `single proprietorship`（独资）→ `self-employed business`（自主经营）→ `wholly-owned retail business`（全资零售），正好是一条从小到大企业成长线。
2. **资产与财务词**：`tangible asset`（有形资产）vs 无形的商标/商誉；`stock turnover`（库存周转）、`working cash`（周转资金）、`surplus`（盈余）、`total`（总计）一起记，是看财报的高频词。
3. **"`…steel`" 与材料**：`stainless steel`（不锈钢）是最常见搭配；`synthetic materials/product`（合成材料/产品）、`scrap value`（废料价值）、`spare part`（零部件）属制造词族。
4. **贸易高频搭配**：`trade fair`（交易会）、`trade discount`（商业折扣）、`trade secret`（商业秘密）、`shipping invoice`（装运单）、`urgent order`（紧急订单）、`transshipment`（转运），外贸场景常见。
5. **动词短语**：`shut up a business`（停业）、`streamline`（精简增效）、`subcontract`（转包）、`undercharge`（要价过低）都落在"扩张与收缩"的经营决策里，配套记忆更牢。
