## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"人事变动"，词表围绕裁员、解雇、招聘、任命、辞职、退休展开，几乎是一份"人事变动通知单"，最忌写成术语堆砌。我用"新任人事主管里德女士对公司做一次大调整：先审查编制、再忍痛裁员、继而招聘补缺、妥善处理离职、最终建立新秩序"作主线，按"编制审查 → 裁员风暴 → 招贤补缺 → 妥善安置 → 新的平衡"五幕推进，让每个词都对应一次真实的人事动作：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The staffing audit 编制审查 | 编制/评估形容词 + 人力名词 | 建立任务与背景 |
| 2 | The axe falls 裁员风暴 | 解雇/劳资纠纷词 + 负面动词 | 冲突与低谷 |
| 3 | New blood 招贤补缺 | 招聘/任命词 + 聘用条款 | 转折与补位 |
| 4 | Settling the accounts 妥善安置 | 补偿/离职名词 + 待遇词 | 人性化收尾 |
| 5 | A new balance 新的平衡 | 收束动词与搭配 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`overstaffed / understaffed / manning / manpower resources / feasible project` 集中在"编制审查"，让她拿评估表算人手；`axe / layoff / dismissal / demote / discard / expel / industrial dispute / industrial action` 集中在"裁员风暴"，形成一条从决定到冲突的链条；`headhunter / hunt / application form / job vacancy / appoint / deputy / vice-president` 集中在"招贤补缺"，写招聘全流程；`indemnification / perquisite / retirement / resignation / suitability` 集中在"妥善安置"；结尾用 `supersede / whittle down / keep in contact with` 收束"变动之后的新秩序"。

## 二、英文故事　The Personnel Shake-up

### 1. The staffing audit

When Ms. Reed took over as head of **personnel**, she studied every **evaluation** form and the **status** of each post. She looked at **manning** levels and at the **manpower** the company actually had. Some departments were **overstaffed**, while the assembly line was badly **understaffed**. Her first **feasible project** was to balance the two, and the board asked her to plan **objectively**. A review of **resources** showed that **manpower resources** were being wasted, and she wrote a short information note for the directors. Whatever she decided, she would have to act fairly.

### 2. The axe falls

The cuts began in March. The board took **the axe** to three teams, and a wave of **layoff** notices followed. Offices that were **overstaffed** lost people, and **redundant labor** was let go with a **dismissal** letter. One manager was **demoted** for ignoring the rules. Two workers were **discarded** after a **labor dispute**, and a third was **expelled** from the site for misconduct. A **redundant** supervisor tried to fight back: he called an **industrial dispute**, threatened **industrial action**, and accused the firm of breaking **industrial relations** and **labor relations** traditions. **Staff turnover** rose sharply. Ms. Reed knew that **discipline** had to be fair, but she also knew the company could not afford to keep everyone.

### 3. New blood

To fill the gaps, the company called in a **headhunter**. She began a **hunt** for talent in a tight **labor market**, where a **labor shortage** made every **job vacancy** hard to fill. She would **headhunt** from rival firms, then study each **application form** and **job description**. They **hired** twenty people, and Ms. Reed had to **choose** carefully whom to keep. She **appointed** a **deputy** and a **vice-president**, **assigned** each newcomer a mentor, and explained **conditions of employment** and **base pay** on day one. The board confirmed each **nomination** the next day. A **casual labor** contract covered seasonal work, and **maternity leave** was guaranteed for new mothers.

### 4. Settling the accounts

Those who left did not go empty-handed. Each got an **indemnification** payment and kept the **perquisite** of a company car until the end of the year. An older manager decided to **retire** early with honour, and a small **retirement** party was held the same week; a young analyst decided to **resign** and join a **shrewd businessman** she had met at a conference. A **suitably** worded letter thanked them all, and Ms. Reed checked the **suitability** of every successor. The **income** of the remaining staff rose once the **disposal** of idle posts was complete. Even the **resignation** of the vice-president was handled with grace.

### 5. A new balance

In the end the changes **superseded** the old order. Ms. Reed **organized** a small **ceremony** to welcome the new team and spoke **objectively** about what had happened. She kept in **contact** with people who had left, and she refused to let old quarrels **entangle** the new managers. With the staffing **status** now **unrestricted** by old rules, she could **whittle down** the last of the waste. "Personnel work is not about **the axe**," she said. "It is about putting the right people in the right **status** and keeping **staff turnover** low." And with that, the human resources office opened a new page.

## 四、学习建议

1. **把变动流程当一条线记**：`evaluation → overstaffed/understaffed → layoff/dismissal → redundancy → headhunt/hire → appoint/assign → indemnification → retirement/resignation → supersede`。故事五幕正好是这条完整流程，按流程记比按字母记牢固。
2. **近义"解雇"词辨析**：`layoff`（因岗位裁撤而解雇，常非个人过错）→ `dismissal`（解雇，正式）→ `the axe`（"砍掉"，口语比喻）→ `sack`（卷铺盖走人，含侮辱意味）→ `discard`（抛弃式解雇）→ `expel`（开除、驱逐出厂）。第 2 幕让它们同场出现，语气层层加重。
3. **劳资纠纷固定搭配整组记**：`an industrial dispute / industrial action / industrial relations / labor dispute / labor relations / labor market / labor shortage`。它们都出现在"裁员风暴"与"招贤补缺"两幕，注意 industrial 与 labor 各自搭配哪些词。
4. **动词搭配整体记**：`a casual labor`（临时工）、`a feasible project`（可行性计划）、`an industrial dispute`（劳资争议）、`industrial relations`（劳资关系）、`redundant labor`（过剩劳动力）、`a shrewd businessman`（精明的商人）、`application form`（申请表）、`base pay`（基本工资）、`conditions of employment`（雇用条款）、`industrial action`（劳工行动）、`job description`（职位描述）、`job vacancy`（工作空缺）、`labor dispute`（劳资纠纷）、`labor market`（劳动市场）、`labor relations`（劳资关系）、`labor shortage`（劳动力短缺）、`manpower resources`（人力资源）、`maternity leave`（产假）、`staff turnover`（人员流动率）、`keep in contact with`（与……保持联系）、`whittle down`（削减）。正文中都已逐字使用。
5. **人事名词的"人"与"事"**：`personnel`（人事部门）、`deputy`（副手）、`vice-president`（副总裁）、`headhunter`（猎头）指人；`nomination`（任命）、`resignation`（辞职）、`retirement`（退休）、`dismissal`（解雇）、`suitability`（合适性）指事。故事通过"谁做了什么"把两者连在一起，复习时回想里德女士的动作即可。
