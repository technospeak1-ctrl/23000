## 一、编排逻辑

本主题词量超大（121 词，另有 30 条常用搭配与 16 条动词搭配），且以商贸、财务、营销术语为主，极易写成"商业术语表"。我用"一位营销企业家为大型公司策划一场全城促销活动，从提案、现场、查账到遭遇对手打压、最终翻盘"的真实因果线作主线，按"提案立势 → 促销现场 → 账本与银行 → 对手破坏 → 收束升华"五幕推进，把每个词与搭配落到具体动作上：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A pitch for Northstar 提案 | 身份/组织名词 + 立势动词 + 宣言类搭配 | 建立人物与目标 |
| 2 | The campaign floor 促销现场 | 营销/商品名词 + 现场动词 + 促销搭配 | 铺陈活动 |
| 3 | The ledger 账本与银行 | 财务名词 + 会计动词 + 30 条财务搭配 | 展现经营细节 |
| 4 | The setback 对手破坏 | 竞争/负面名词 + 处置动词 + 危机搭配 | 情节高潮 |
| 5 | The season closed 收束升华 | 收束动词 + 正面搭配 | 点题升华 |

**设计要点**：`entrepreneur / agency / firm / corporation / multinational` 等组织词贴到人物与客户的身份上；`advertisement rates / loss leader / prime goods / soft sell` 等促销搭配嵌入活动策划；`current assets / fixed assets / tangible asset / net margin / trading profit` 等 30 条财务搭配集中在查账 scenes 中，成为审计员逐项核对的动作；`blackball / monopolize / overcharge / mangle / smirch` 等负面动词与 `rectify / mitigate / negotiate / sanction / undergird` 的处置动词形成正反对照；52 个动词各自落在"提案—执行—查账—危机—收尾"的具体环节里。

## 二、英文故事　The Season of the Great Sale

### 1. A pitch for Northstar

Vivian Vale was an **entrepreneur** who ran a small **agency** called Vale Partners. Her **firm** occupied a single floor, yet she meant one day to see it **incorporate into** a **multinational** group; two smaller agencies would **incorporate with** them if the terms were fair. Mark, her **helpmeet**, was a careful **practitioner** who had once worked as a foreign **correspondent** and knew how to **convey a message** without **affectation**. Ten years had taught him to **accumulate experience**, and now they were ready to **launch** the boldest **movement** in the city's shopping calendar: a season-long promotion for Northstar, a **corporation** whose **management** wanted to **escalate** sales and win a **vantage** over its rivals.

Northstar was no **proprietorship** but a **public limited company (plc)**, with a board of **stakeholders** and a **trustee** guarding the books. Its **proprietor** had made his name in the **public sector**, and a **guru** of the trade still advised him. Vivian knew that winning the **dealership** would bring **renown** to her agency, so she prepared a **manifesto**, a clear **strategy**, and a **proclamation** for the press. She would **espouse** only claims she could **recognize** as honest, and she told the board, "We **specialize in** real value, not the **stereotype** of a loud sale." She prized **judiciousness** above flash; her **minimum** demand was a fair **payment** and a clear **requirement** in the contract. She chose a **soft sell** over a hard pitch. Her greatest **resource** was the loyalty of her staff, and she kept a **replacement** plan for every key role.

### 2. The campaign floor

The promotion opened with an **auction** of **luxury** lots. Vivian had to **advertise for** two extra clerks and bought modest **advertisement rates** in the local papers; a **combination** of **prime goods** and one **loss leader** drew the first crowd. A **model** stood at the door to **salute** each guest, and every stall had its own **specialty**. Staff wore a silver **insignia** on their jackets, and a crew had come before dawn to **rig up** the stage. One table held a tiny **gadget** that could **pucker** a collar, another a **makeup** kit **bedizened** with gold, and a third the catalogue of a **quoted company**. To her surprise, a **multiplicity** of small traders turned up, and many preferred **barter**, a simple **trade-off** of services, to cash. A **slapstick** act made the children laugh. Vivian asked her team to **interact with** every visitor and told a buyer he could **enquire about** a lot or **enquire of** the clerk; the auctioneer would **quote** a starting price, but no lot was allowed to **jump in price**. She warned the clerks not to **overcharge** anyone, promised a full **refund** if an item proved faulty, and did not **expect** any complaint. "This is a **gamble**," Mark whispered, yet the **takings** were already **soaring** and the event began to **thrive on** word of mouth. Mark **broached** the subject of costs, and a nervous clerk began to **spout** figures until Vivian told him to stop his **palaver** and get back to work.

### 3. The ledger

That evening an accountant arrived to **audit** the books. On her laptop a **function key** opened the accounts, and row by row she checked the **current assets** against the **current liabilities**, the **fixed assets** against the **fixed capital**, the **fixed charges** against the **fixed costs**. Northstar's **fixed income** from a **fixed property** lease looked sound, but its **invisible assets** were harder to value than the **tangible asset** on the showroom floor. She also verified the **cycle inventory** of fast-moving goods. A **virtual bank** handled the **swap transaction**, and a **sinking fund** had been set aside to cover a **zero coupon bond** issued years before. The banker noted the **net margin** and the **trading profit**, then asked whether **dear money** had **incurred** extra costs. Vivian answered with a **ratio** of sales to stock and a **rate** of return she could defend; she would not **misuse** a single figure. The firm chose to **be affiliated with** a Japanese partner, so part of the **payment** arrived in **yen**, and an **intra-corporate transfer** moved **ownership** of two vans. Its **solvency** was never in doubt, though a small **debt** remained and a **dividend** was due; the **premium** on the insurance rose, but the **profitability** of the campaign was clear. "A firm must **incur debt** only for good reason and never **incur losses** through carelessness," Vivian said. "We must **levy a tax** on imports, not invent a hidden charge, and a **specific tariff** applies only to gloves." No **forfeiture** of deposit was threatened; on the contrary, a healthy **balance** remained. The **take-home pay** of the casual staff had been **increased**, and Mark had to **reach for** the file when the tax inspector called. She was careful to **minimize the risk of** a quiet loss.

### 4. The setback

Success bred trouble. A rival **tycoon** who held a **monopoly** on local advertising tried to **blackball** the campaign, and a **representative** of his **subsidiary** began to **spout invective** against Vivian in the press. He tried to **cheapen** her name, to **smirch** the **insignia** she was proud of, and even to **besot** a few journalists with free dinners. When a rival firm **monopolized** the best stalls, Mark wanted to **resort** to harsh words too, but Vivian refused to **inflame** the row. She had her **agent** call the paper, and the honest clerks did not **disaffect**. A angry supplier threatened to **mangle** the displays, but Vivian chose to **efface** the rumor with facts and to **retouch** the notices, and she moved to **terminate** the contract of the dishonest supplier. The rival even tried a **takeover** of a small supplier and tried to **rig** the auction, but the accountant began to **rectify errors** in the books and found the trick. Orders did not **curtail**; instead, a sudden **cutback** in the rival's own ad spend sent shoppers back to Vivian. She **negotiated** with the supplier and **mitigated** the damage, while a **reform** of the rota let the staff **collaborate** better. In the end the whole affair became a **liberalization** of a hidebound market.

### 5. The season closed

The promotion was **sanctioned** by the board and **eulogized** in the press. Northstar's sales **soared**, the little gadget **outsells** every rival even now, and Vivian's agency came to **be recognized as** the city's best. Standing at the door where the **model** had **saluted** the crowd, Vivian **reached for** her notebook and wrote the last line: the **balance** and the **ratio** were healthy, the **debt** was shrinking, and the **takings** had doubled. The **combination** of a **soft sell** and fair **advertisement rates** had **undergirded** the whole season. She had learned to **minimize the risk of** a **gamble**, to **incur debt** only for good reason, and to **rectify errors** before they spread. The **guru** had been right: a **firm** that dares to **launch** a **movement** must also know when to **curtail** it. As the lights went out, Vivian **thrived on** the quiet, already planning how to **collaborate** with a **multinational** next year.

## 四、学习建议

1. **组织成长链**：`entrepreneur`（企业家）→ `agency / firm`（代理处/商行）→ `corporation`（公司）→ `multinational`（跨国公司），配 `subsidiary`（子公司）、`dealership`（经销权）、`takeover`（接管），是一条从小到大的企业线。
2. **财务搭配群**：`current assets / current liabilities`（流动资产/流动负债）、`fixed assets / fixed capital / fixed cost / fixed charge`（固定资产/固定资本/固定成本/固定支出）、`tangible asset / invisible assets`（有形/无形资产）、`net margin / trading profit`（净利/销售毛利）集中在账本场景，配套记忆最牢。
3. **促销词族**：`launch / movement / strategy`（推出/活动/战略）与 `advertisement rates / prime goods / loss leader / soft sell`（广告费率/精品/亏本商品/软推销）都在活动现场，`auction / barter / trade-off`（拍卖/物物交换/交换）是交易方式。
4. **正反动词对照**：正面的 `sanction / eulogize / undergird / mitigate / negotiate` 与负面的 `blackball / monopolize / overcharge / mangle / smirch / inflame` 放在"对手破坏—化解"同一场景里对照记忆。
5. **动词搭配聚焦**：`accumulate experience / convey a message / be affiliated with / interact with / incur debt / levy a tax / rectify errors / reach for / thrive on` 都是高频动词短语，配合故事动作更不易忘。
