## 一、编排逻辑

本主题是"学校生活"，词项从宿舍、食堂延伸到课程、考试、奖助与夜思，最容易写成一张"校园设施表"。我用"奖学金学生 Mei 从清晨到学期末的一整段校园生活"作主线，按"宿舍的清晨 → 一天的课程 → 考试周 → 颁奖与资助 → 夜晚的沉思"五幕推进，让每个词都落在她真实经历的时间与因果里：

| 幕 | 场景 | 承载语义场 | 叙事功能 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Morning in the dormitory 宿舍的清晨 | 住宿器物 + 校园人物 + 时间名词 | 建立人物、身份与生活起点 |
| 2 | A day of lessons 一天的课程 | 课堂器物 + 学科分科 + 语言学习形容词 | 铺陈学习日常 |
| 3 | Examination week 考试周 | 考试动词 + 压力名词 + 应考态度 | 制造紧张与转折的高潮 |
| 4 | Awards and aid 颁奖与资助 | 奖助名词 + 典礼器物 + 身份转变 | 给出回报与升华为结果 |
| 5 | Evening reflection 夜晚的沉思 | 性格形容词 + 治学动词 + 人生格言 | 收束并点明主题 |

**设计要点**：`fluent English`、`analytical data`、`convincing speech`、`elaborate designs` 四条搭配落在课堂与集会的具体展示里；`elementary school / preparatory school / senior student / optional courses / vigorous students / strenuous efforts / permissible attitude / rational explanation / be weary of doing sth.` 九条搭配按"过来人回想"的口吻自然散入五幕；`radiotelegraphy / spinal / thesaurus / shorthand / cyberspace / hardware / archive` 这些冷门词分别贴住历史、生物、写作与机房四个场景。`headmaster / president / faculty / graduate / greenhorn / prodigy` 构成"人的阶梯"，`canteen / refectory` 这组同义词在同一句里自然对照。

## 二、英文故事　A Term in School

### 1. Morning in the dormitory

At six the **light** came on in the **accommodation** block where Mei, a **senior student** who had been a **junior** the year before, lived. It had taken her a whole term to **accommodate to** boarding life, but now the routine was a **necessity** she no longer questioned. She filled her **backpack** with **stationery**, smoothed the **scroll** of her timetable on the wall, and joined the **aggregation** of students in the corridor. The **interval** before the first class was short. A notice on the board announced an **alteration** to the morning schedule. In the **refectory** — older students still called it the **canteen** — breakfast was plain but **excellent**. The **headmaster** stood at the door while a **faculty** member checked the names, and a uniformed **cadet** passed with a salute. The school had practised **coeducation** for decades, and Mei had once volunteered at the **elementary school** down the road, just as she herself had once been a **greenhorn** at the **preparatory school** that fed students into this college. The **principal** reason she stayed was the **guidance** of her tutor.

### 2. A day of lessons

Classes began in the teaching block of the local **institute**. In English, Mei wrote **fluent English**, **underlined** new words in her **thesaurus**, and took notes in **shorthand**; the teacher praised her **comprehension** and the **analytical data** she had copied from the board. Next came biology, where a chart traced the **spinal** cord, and history, where an old **archive** box and a **radiotelegraphy** set from the school museum were carried in. The lesson covered one **branch** of science after another. At assembly an **eloquent** senior delivered a **convincing speech** full of **wit**, a friend's **badinage** about an old **truism** set the room laughing, and the art room displayed **elaborate designs** that drew a crowd. The class was **dynamic** and **responsive**, and even the quietest pupils were **literate** enough to follow. In the computer room the **hardware** was old but usable, and Mei reached the library through **cyberspace** to fetch more pages. She **craved** a quiet corner, but the **stimulation** of new ideas kept her alert. Her tutor said the real work of school was to **implant** a love of learning, not merely to pass the **exams**.

### 3. Examination week

On Wednesday the **exams** began. Teachers **invigilated** every **exam** hall, and one careless **indiscretion** — a missing name on the cover — could **jeopardize** a whole term's work. Two clocks ticked **simultaneously** above the desk, and one chair was **conspicuously** empty. Mei felt the **burden** of **stress** and grew **weary of** revising late into the night, fighting the **boredom** of another long session with a cup of tea. A rumour of **truancy** among two boys drew a sharp **remonstrance** from the **headmaster**. The paper was **primarily** a test of understanding, so she tried to give a **rational explanation** for every answer, but one question made her **slide into** panic for a moment. The **vigorous students** in the yard outside were shouting and running, which somehow steadied her. She made **strenuous efforts** to recover, **increased** her pace, and managed to **scrape through** the hardest paper. Afterwards she **contended** that the whole week had been fair.

### 4. Awards and aid

At the end of term the **reception** hall was decorated. The **president** of the college read out the **scroll** of names: Mei had won a **scholarship**, a **grant** from the local **institute**, and a **fellowship** for a **graduate** year. A young **prodigy** from the **elementary school** stood there with his parents, and a second **alteration** to the award list was announced amid the **aggregation** of families. She had chosen **optional courses** in music and law and had never regretted it. Looking back, she felt she had crossed a **threshold** from **greenhorn** to senior, and that the **training** of those early mornings had made the difference. A small **symposium** on careers was held after the ceremony, and she would **contend** for a place at university the following autumn.

### 5. Evening reflection

That night Mei sat **pensive** by the window. Her roommate, still **weary of** homework, was already asleep. She thought of the **permissive attitude** of her favourite teacher, who never raised her voice yet kept perfect order, and of the **excellent** lessons that had made even a **dreary** Monday worth attending. School life, she decided, was **primarily** a matter of small daily choices: to **underline** one more word, to **crave** one more answer, to **accommodate to** every change, and to keep going when the work felt **strenuous**. The old **truism** that a **literate** person is never alone suddenly seemed new again. She switched off the **light**, and in the quiet her mind ran on, **dynamic** and grateful.

## 四、学习建议

1. **一天一条线**：`accommodation → backpack → stationery → refectory/canteen → faculty → lesson → exam → reception → scholarship → reflection`，把第 1、2、4、5 幕按真实时间顺序串起来背，校园生活词就成了一条动线。
2. **同义与近义对照**：`canteen` 与 `refectory` 都指食堂（前者口语常用，后者偏学院旧称）；`headmaster`（中小学校长）与 `president`（大学校长）分属不同学段；`greenhorn`（新手）与 `prodigy`（神童）恰好是两端。
3. **性格形容词两极**：`dynamic / vigorous / excellent / eloquent / literate / responsive` 一类正面，`dreary / weary / pensive` 一类偏沉静或负面，`permissive / rational / strenuous / principal` 中性偏理性——把第 3、5 幕放在一起形成情绪对照。
4. **考试动词串**：`invigilate`（监考）→ `jeopardize`（危及）→ `slide into`（陷入）→ `strenuous efforts`（竭尽全力）→ `scrape through`（勉强通过）→ `contend`（主张），按考场因果顺序记，比孤立背单词牢固得多。
5. **易混辨析**：`weary`（疲倦的）与 `wearisome`（令人厌烦的）不同；`principal`（主要的）是形容词，别和名词 `principle`（原则，见 Topic 127）混；`grant`（助学金）与 `scholarship`（奖学金）、`fellowship`（研究生津贴）是三种不同的资助名目。
